# Jesse M. Engreitz — functional maps linking regulatory elements and genes

[Русский](researcher-02.md)

**Access.** Checked 25 October 2026: official laboratory website, publication list and selected scE2G sections (2026). The original address without www failed; the www version was accessible. The complete research corpus and code were not assessed.

The laboratory combines experimental and computational genomics, biochemistry and human genetics to map regulation, including questions of heart development and disease. A concrete collaborative example is scE2G, which relates enhancer–gene predictions to perturbation evidence. The central question is which elements control which genes in a defined cellular context.

**ORI critical analysis.** This line matters because it connects large-scale mapping with attempts to preserve the functional meaning of a link. However, different perturbations answer different questions. Deleting an element, suppressing its activity and changing one nucleotide are not interchangeable tests: each affects different system properties. Comparisons between publications should therefore record the counterfactual question being tested and the actual measured outcome.

Cell selection matters equally. A link measured in a convenient line may be a useful starting hypothesis for tissue without proving that it operates in an organism. ORI should track cellular context separately from experimental-validation status. A map of millions of candidates may be more useful for discovery than a small confirmed set, but the two products serve different purposes and should remain distinguishable in the review.

The laboratory is worth following for methods that connect predictions with controlled functional evidence. A next analysis could compare studies by transfer between cells, negative-label definitions and independence of evaluation sets. Unconfirmed links should be preserved alongside successes because they reveal methodological boundaries. Mentioning Engreitz implies no collaboration with ORI, endorsement or external review of its results. Institutional statements about future therapies describe research objectives rather than established clinical achievements. The programme can learn from the evidential design while retaining its own responsibility for evaluating each published claim.
