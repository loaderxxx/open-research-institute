# Robin H. van der Weide — multilayer genome regulation and cell identity

[Русский](researcher-01.md)

**Access.** Checked 24 October 2026: UMC Utrecht institutional profile and indexed abstract, selected result and availability sections of Magnitov et al. (2026). The full publication corpus and supplements were not examined.

The profile describes experimental and computational investigation of regulatory layers of the genome. A concrete collaborative study with Magnitov and colleagues combines haplotyping, multiomic measurements and variant-effect prediction. For PIK3R5, the authors report functional testing of a variant disrupting an OCT2 motif. This is a contribution by the paper's author team, not something automatically attributable to the researcher's current laboratory.

**ORI critical analysis.** Allelic comparison offers the opportunity to examine two regulatory versions in closely matched cellular surroundings. Yet alternatives remain: linked variants on the same haplotype, mapping errors and structural differences may influence the observed imbalance. Moving from an allelic signal to a specific nucleotide therefore requires separate justification. Phasing clarifies which observations belong together but does not itself prove causation.

This research line is worth following because it relates sequence, factor binding, chromatin accessibility and expression. ORI should distinguish statistical associations, model predictions and intervention-tested links. Confirmation of one example demonstrates feasibility without establishing accuracy for the entire variant map. A rare variant also cannot automatically be considered a cause of disease simply because it affects expression.

A useful next review would compare several loci with different evidence completeness and identify what each data type adds. This would capture limits of functional interpretation as well as successful examples. Applicability depends on the studied cells, genomes and conditions; transfer to other tissues needs testing. Mentioning van der Weide concerns published scientific contributions and implies no partnership, external review of ORI materials or access to private group data. The programme's focus is the structure of the evidence connecting regulatory layers, rather than treating multiomic agreement as an automatic guarantee of causality.
