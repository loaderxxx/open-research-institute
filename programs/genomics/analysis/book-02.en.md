# James D. Watson et al. — Molecular Biology of the Gene, 7th edition (2014)

[Русский](book-02.md)

**Access and status.** Checked 25 October 2026: the Pearson URL was inaccessible; the CSHL Press index confirmed the title and seventh edition, but its page returned 403. Substantive context is limited to ORI's existing map. Chapters were not read; a substantial dossier requires the full text.

The programme map describes the book as an introduction to transcription, regulatory elements and genetic-experiment reasoning. That description is retained as secondary context, not presented as a new reading. Its proposed analytical role is to examine how a molecular experiment connects a defined sequence change with an observed outcome.

**ORI critical questions.** Once access is obtained, reading should examine how gene structure, expression and regulation are distinguished. One example could be followed through competing explanations: altered recognition, complex assembly, synthesis rate or product stability. These can yield the same final RNA abundance. Measuring only that endpoint therefore does not always identify a mechanism, even when a genetic association is persuasive.

Transfer between experimental systems is another important question. A simple regulatory construct can test element necessity, yet its behaviour in native chromatin may depend on the surrounding context. ORI needs records of where activity was tested, how controls were constructed and what a negative result means. No effect in one condition does not establish absence of function across all cells and stages.

The 2014 edition should be compared with current single-cell and perturbation studies through principles of inference, without expecting coverage of technologies developed over the following decade. A useful reading outcome would analyse one experiment while separating observation, causal interpretation and limits of generalisation. Until lawful full-text access is available, specific arguments, teaching examples and explanatory completeness cannot be assessed reliably. This entry supplies a substantive reading agenda and an explicit access gap, rather than claiming a completed book review.
