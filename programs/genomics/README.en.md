## Programme scope

The programme concerns the path from DNA sequence and chromatin state to gene activity and cell properties. It focuses on regulatory elements, cell identity and the functional effects of genetic variants. Single-cell data and spatial measurements provide ways to connect molecular processes to tissue organisation.

## Programme questions

- **Gene regulation.** How do regulatory sequences and chromatin state influence when genes become active?
- **Cell identity.** How do patterns of gene activity distinguish cell types and developmental transitions?
- **From variant to function.** Which evidence links a DNA change to a change in cellular behaviour?

## First stage: information gathering

The next step is to gather and organise information. The proposed sequence is:

1. Define the scope of the first review by choosing a cellular system and gene-regulation questions that can be compared using published data.
2. Gather review articles and primary studies on regulatory elements, chromatin states and cell identity. Record which conclusions come from observation and which from targeted interventions.
3. List open single-cell datasets, documenting their technologies, samples, annotations, quality and terms of use.
4. Select one dataset and prepare a plan for comparing cellular states, including quality control and possible differences between measurement batches.

## Planned materials

- An annotated bibliography on gene regulation and cell identity.
- A map connecting DNA variants, regulatory mechanisms and cellular traits, with an assessment of available evidence.
- A review of gene regulation and a justified selection of an open dataset.
- After gathering and selecting materials: a reproducible comparison of cell states, with code, visualisations and a documented analysis.
