**Access and status.** Checked 25 October 2026: Norton description; the demonstration interface did not expose chapter text. This is an initial assessment of the book's role, not a completed reading. A substantial dossier requires the full text.

Norton presents the seventh edition as a synthesis of cell biology and highlights updates on cellular organisation, biomolecular condensates and microscopy. Within ORI, the book provides a conceptual foundation for relating molecular organisation, measurable morphology and cellular interactions without treating these levels as interchangeable.

**ORI critical analysis.** The main task for close reading is to reconstruct the passage from components to behaviour. A receptor's presence does not establish a particular signal; a junction does not specify the force it transmits; altered shape does not prove altered cell fate. A useful reading record would therefore connect each molecular participant to a measurement, an intervention, an observed outcome and a stated domain of applicability. This turns a textbook diagram into an explanation that can be tested and exposes links that remain hypothetical.

The distinction between a cell as a measurement unit and tissue as an interacting system matters particularly here. Similar images after two perturbations could reflect a shared mechanism, a common stress response or similar segmentation errors. The textbook should help formulate alternatives, while primary studies must discriminate between them. For self-assembly, adhesion, polarity, proliferation and cytoskeletal rearrangement should be considered separately because several processes can produce the same final geometry.

This assessment has a substantial limitation: a publisher page cannot establish the depth of experimental reasoning, balance of examples or quality of particular figures. The next substantive step is lawful close reading of material on junctions and signalling, followed by comparison of one mechanism with an open imaging dataset. The useful outcome is an observation that could contradict the proposed explanation, rather than simply a familiar biological term attached to an image.
