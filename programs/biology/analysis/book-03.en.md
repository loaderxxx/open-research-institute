The publisher describes Cooper's textbook as a concise introduction for a semester course, combining fundamental concepts, research examples and active learning. Its overview explicitly names genomics, transcriptional regulation and epigenetics. For ORI's cellular programme, its role is to connect molecular explanations with observable cellular behaviour before moving to collective tissue organisation.

**ORI critical analysis.** A useful initial question is what an experiment actually measures. An organelle image, an RNA abundance and a change in migration speed concern different properties of a system. Their joint variation can suggest a mechanism without determining the direction of causation. Reading notes should distinguish directly measured quantities, computed variables and explanatory constructs. This would prevent every morphological shift from automatically becoming evidence that a cell has been “activated”.

Another important transition is from individual responses to population behaviour. An average can change because every cell responds, or because the proportions of cellular states change. Future imaging analyses need different comparisons to distinguish these possibilities: distributions, trajectories or culture conditions. A textbook can supply the language for asking the question; selecting statistical units and checking batch effects remain separate analytical responsibilities.

The accessible overview does not support assessment of specific evidence, examples or exercises in edition eight. Information from an openly available older edition or from edition nine should therefore not be silently substituted. The next reading should identify the precise edition and follow one measurable process, such as changing cell contacts. A practical outcome would be a set of competing explanations and independent observations capable of discriminating between them in the selected dataset. This preserves the book's educational role while keeping the evidential burden attached to actual experiments.
