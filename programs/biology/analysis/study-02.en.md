# Cimini et al. (2023) — Optimizing the Cell Painting assay for image-based profiling

[Русский](study-02.md)

**Access.** Checked 24 October 2026: indexed abstract, introduction and optimisation sections of the PMC author manuscript; direct retrieval displayed CAPTCHA. Supplements and raw images were not inspected. This is a methods paper, not a test of one disease mechanism.

The authors optimise Cell Painting through the ability of morphological profiles to detect reproducible effects and group related perturbations. Version 3 uses multichannel staining of cellular components and simplifies parts of the procedure. Rather than selecting visually attractive staining, quantitative evaluation targets the usefulness of the resulting profiles for comparing conditions.

**ORI critical analysis.** The paper illustrates why a measurement instrument needs explicit quality criteria. Strong visual contrast is not equivalent to useful biological discrimination. Conversely, a visually subtle signal may yield a stable feature after appropriate processing. Future analyses should therefore specify whether a metric assesses replicate consistency, recognition of related perturbations or sensitivity to a particular cellular structure.

The choice of positive comparisons determines which biology is optimised. If conditions are labelled similar in advance, grouping them successfully tests agreement with that knowledge; it does not discover every possible mechanism. A new cell type requires checks that death, density or uneven illumination do not dominate the differences. Batch correction should also preserve perturbation effects: eliminating all variation is not evidence of successful normalisation.

A reasonable ORI next step is to match the selected dataset's documentation to its actual protocol version and identify changes that could affect extracted features. One conclusion could then be tested under several cell-aggregation strategies. The article helps assess measurement reliability but cannot turn morphological similarity into proof of causal relationships between signalling pathways. Transfer to curved tissue surfaces or dynamic live observations needs separate evaluation because geometry, timescale and system composition change. No optimisation experiment or computational reproduction was performed for this initial assessment; the suggested comparisons describe future work.
