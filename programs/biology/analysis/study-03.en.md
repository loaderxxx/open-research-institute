# Piernitzki et al. (2025) — Self-assembly of hybrid 3D cultures by integrating living and synthetic cells

[Русский](study-03.md)

**Access.** Checked 24 October 2026: abstract, introduction and initial results sections of the open Nature Communications article. Supplements, movies and the complete results were not inspected.

The study compares incorporation of different synthetic cell models into three-dimensional cancer cultures. The authors connect co-assembly to adhesion and surface tension; live observations show synthetic components entering aggregates during formation. This is an experimental platform for controlling selected properties of a cellular environment, rather than a reconstruction of an entire natural tissue.

**ORI critical analysis.** Its strength is the possibility of separating parameters that vary together in a living neighbour. However, controllability does not guarantee independence: changing a material surface may simultaneously alter attachment, force transmission and molecular accessibility. Further reading should identify comparisons that discriminate between these explanations and establish how the relevant parameters were measured.

For self-assembly, initial incorporation, subsequent retention and functional interaction are distinct outcomes. A component could remain as a mechanical inclusion without supplying the intended signal; strong attachment does not establish complete material exchange. Geometrical incorporation, persistence and cellular response should therefore occupy separate rows in an evidence map. Timing matters as well: adding a component to an established aggregate tests a different process from co-assembly initiated together.

ORI should compare this work with natural adhesion as a controlled simplification. A useful next question is which organisational changes remain after matching aggregate size and cell viability. That comparison could distinguish a specific interface effect from general disruption of growth. Cancer cell lines and artificial materials limit transfer to healthy tissues, while laboratory control of a microenvironment does not establish clinical efficacy. This initial assessment neither evaluates reproducibility of the whole platform nor claims independent confirmation. Its contribution is to clarify which levels of self-organisation the experiments address and which require additional evidence.

**Source:** [Nature Communications article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66789-3).
