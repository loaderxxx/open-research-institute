# Steven (Shaogeng) Tang — cell surfaces and membrane fusion

[Русский](researcher-03.md)

**Access.** Checked 25 October 2026: Yale profile and indexed abstract, results and discussion of Lu, Ikawa and Tang (PNAS, 2025). The institutional profile is the main basis; supplements were not inspected.

Tang's programme combines structural biology, protein engineering and cellular methods to investigate cell recognition and fusion. The 2025 OBF13 study provides structural and functional evidence supporting allosteric inhibition of IZUMO1–JUNO interaction during mouse fertilisation. Antibody binding occurs away from the JUNO-binding site, making the conformational mechanism central to interpretation.

**ORI critical analysis.** This example helps separate events often grouped under “contact”: molecular recognition, adhesion between cells and membrane merger. Success or inhibition at one stage does not automatically determine the others. For cellular-systems research, each method must be connected to its specific measured outcome rather than allowing the broad term fusion to cover all observed interactions.

A protein-complex structure proposes a possible explanation but does not alone reveal dynamics on a living membrane. Linking structural changes, surface binding and cellular outcomes is therefore essential. Further reading should distinguish purified proteins, membrane-bound receptors and whole gametes. These systems provide complementary evidence without constituting identical measurement conditions.

The group is worth following for studies connecting molecular specificity to consequences of cellular interaction. ORI could construct a comparison of recognition, adhesion and fusion, specifying a separate control for each transition. The concrete example remains limited by its species and reproductive context; it cannot automatically explain fusion of muscle, immune or other cells. Proposed therapeutic applications do not establish clinical effectiveness. Mentioning the laboratory reflects interest in published work and implies neither collaboration nor endorsement. Its broader methodological value is the discipline of assigning an effect to a defined transition rather than assuming that a shared endpoint identifies a universal mechanism across cell types.
