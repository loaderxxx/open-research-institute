# Starter literature map: biology of cellular systems

[Русская версия](RESEARCH_MAP.md) · [Programme README](README.en.md)

**Checked 24 October 2026.** This map covers cellular organisation, intercellular signalling and mechanics, tissue assembly and adaptation; imaging is treated as a measurement resource. It is a starter selection, not a systematic review. Metadata, abstracts, publisher pages, dataset descriptions and available excerpts were checked; availability of a full text does not by itself mean it was read.

## Books

1. **Bruce Alberts et al., _Molecular Biology of the Cell_ (7th ed., 2022).** [W. W. Norton](https://seagull.wwnorton.com/molecularbiologycell). Broad grounding in cell organisation, signalling, cytoskeleton, junctions and multicellular development. Metadata and selected demo excerpts are available; the full text and teaching materials are paid.
2. **Cheryll Tickle, Alfonso Martinez Arias, Marysia Placzek and Lewis Wolpert, _Wolpert’s Principles of Development_ (7th ed., 2025).** [Oxford University Press](https://www.oupjapan.co.jp/en/products/detail/65084). Explains morphogenesis and interactions among cell fates in developing tissues, helping connect local signals to form. The page is open; the book is sold.
3. **Geoffrey M. Cooper, _The Cell: A Molecular Approach_ (8th ed., 2019; ISBN 9781605357072).** [Oxford University Press resources for the 8th edition](https://learninglink.oup.com/protected/files/content/file/1561083639416-TheCell8e-Instructor-Manual-%26-Active-Learning-Guide.pdf). An introductory text that clearly bridges molecular mechanisms and cellular behaviour. Cooper is the author listed for this edition; Hausman co-authored earlier editions. The linked instructor PDF requires sign-in. The [edition overview](https://learninglink.oup.com/access/cooper8e) is open; the complete book was not obtained here.

## Papers and datasets

1. **Chandrasekaran et al. (2024), JUMP Cell Painting pilot.** [Official dataset catalogue](https://broadinstitute.github.io/cellpainting-gallery/complete_datasets.html) · [consortium](https://jump-cellpainting.broadinstitute.org/). An open set of images and cell profiles after chemical and genetic perturbations in two cell lines; useful for quantitative study of morphology, organelles and cellular response. The pilot requires substantial storage (about 12 TB); cultured cells do not represent intact tissue, and morphology alone does not reveal mechanism.
2. **Cimini et al. (2023), “Optimizing the Cell Painting assay for image-based profiling”.** [Nature Protocols / open PMC text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10536784/). Compares imaging-protocol variations and publishes images, profiles and metadata in the Cell Painting Gallery. This is a methods resource and data example; cross-lab comparability depends on staining, microscope and processing conditions.
3. **Piernitzki et al. (2025), “Self-assembly of hybrid 3D cultures by integrating living and synthetic cells”.** [Nature Communications, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66789-3). Tests how cell adhesion and surface tension in synthetic cell models affect co-assembly of three-dimensional tumour cultures. Open article; engineered hybrid cultures are not equivalent to natural tissue.
4. **Eckert et al. (2025), “Capturing nematic order on tissue surfaces of arbitrary geometry”.** [Nature Communications, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-62694-x). Develops a description of orientational order in cellular structures on curved tissue surfaces. Article metadata are available from the journal; the geometric measure describes organisation but does not establish its causal mechanism.
5. **Taslim A. Al-Hilal et al. (2025), “Durotaxis is a driver and potential therapeutic target in lung fibrosis and metastatic pancreatic cancer”.** [Nature Cell Biology, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-025-01697-8). In mouse models, shows that migration along stiffness gradients can direct fibroblasts to injury and contribute to metastasis; it links the effect to FAK–paxillin and YAP. Open article. Translation to humans and therapeutic efficacy need separate tests.

## Historical landmarks

- **1838–1839 — Schleiden and Schwann’s cell theory.** [Brief history at Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/science/cell-biology/History-of-cell-biology). Established the cell as the basic unit of plants and animals; this early theory preceded modern molecular biology.
- **1952 — A. A. and H. Moscona, dissociation and reaggregation of chick-embryo cells.** [Open full text in PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1273752/). An early experiment on how cells reassemble into aggregates after dissociation; it became part of the foundation for research on sorting and tissue self-assembly. Its historical importance should not be mistaken for a complete modern explanation of adhesion.
- **1972 — S. J. Singer and Garth L. Nicolson’s fluid mosaic model of membranes.** [Science paper and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.175.4023.720). Explained the dynamic membrane structure needed for reception and signalling; the original model has since been developed to account for the cytoskeleton and membrane domains.

## Active researchers and groups

- **Alpha Yap — Institute for Molecular Bioscience, University of Queensland.** [Group profile](https://imb.uq.edu.au/research-groups/yap) · [2025 work](https://about.uq.edu.au/experts/150?page=0). Studies adherens junctions, tissue mechanics and epithelial homeostasis; specific recent work includes a review of epithelial mechanoadaptation and a 2025 paper on nematic order on tissue surfaces.
- **Oskar Staufer and colleagues — Leibniz Institute for New Materials / Max Planck Bristol Centre for Minimal Biology.** [Institutional profile](https://www.leibniz-inm.de/mitarbeiter/dr-staufer-oskar/) · [group paper (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66789-3). Investigate interactions between synthetic and living cells and self-assembly of hybrid 3D cultures. Their open paper appeared in December 2025; it presents a laboratory platform for controlled microenvironments.
- **Steven (Shaogeng) Tang — Yale University School of Medicine.** [Institutional profile and publications](https://medicine.yale.edu/profile/shaogeng-tang/) · [specific 2025 PNAS paper](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2425952122). His group studies cell surfaces and membrane-fusion mechanisms; the paper shows allosteric inhibition of the IZUMO1–JUNO complex by antibody OBF13. This is a model of fertilisation, not a general explanation of all forms of cell fusion.

## Current directions and open questions

2025 papers strengthen two connected lines: quantitative descriptions of cellular order on complex surfaces and tests of how mechanical properties of the environment alter collective cell behaviour. Public morphological resources such as JUMP are also expanding and support phenotype comparisons after perturbations. These data show that “cell shape” depends on protocol, cell line and culture conditions; moving from an image to a mechanism requires causal perturbation and independent measurements.

Key open questions are how to link an individual cell’s shape to the state of its neighbours and tissue; how well in-vitro findings hold in an organism; and how to integrate geometry, signalling and mechanics without confusing correlation with causation. For any dataset, check metadata, batches, resolution, access and licence in advance.

**Suggested next reading:** read Alberts’ chapters on cell junctions, cytoskeleton and signalling; then the Cell Painting protocol as an example of reproducible measurement; finally compare hybrid self-assembly, tissue order and durotaxis as three scales of cellular organisation.
