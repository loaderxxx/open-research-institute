Cells sense their surroundings, exchange signals and organise into tissues. This programme connects cell structure, collective behaviour and adaptation through quantitative models and open biological data.

## Programme scope

The programme concerns the transition from the organisation of individual cells to the collective organisation of tissue. Its interests include cell shape and internal structures, signalling, mechanical interactions and responses to the environment. Quantitative models and open images should connect these levels of description to specific observations.

## Programme questions

- **Cell states.** How are a cell's shape, internal organisation and activity related?
- **From cells to tissues.** How do local signals and mechanical interactions guide tissue organisation?
- **Adaptation and resilience.** Which mechanisms help cellular systems maintain function as their environment changes?

## First stage: information gathering

The next step is to gather and organise information. The proposed sequence is:

1. Define the scope of the first review of cellular self-organisation, including cell types, processes and timescales.
2. Gather review articles and primary studies on cell states, interactions between cells and tissue resilience. Record the biological system, measurement methods and main findings.
3. List open imaging datasets, documenting their provenance, resolution, annotations, acquisition conditions and terms of use.
4. Select one dataset and define a focused question for later analysis, such as the relationship between measurable cell shape and cell state.

## Planned materials

- An annotated bibliography covering the three programme questions.
- A map of cellular self-organisation mechanisms, biological systems and available evidence.
- A review of cellular self-organisation and a catalogue of suitable open datasets.
- After data selection: an analysis plan, followed by reproducible computations, visualisations and an explanation of the results.
