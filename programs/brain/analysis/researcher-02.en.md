# Jun Nagai — Glia–Neuron Circuit Dynamics, RIKEN

[Русская версия](researcher-02.md)

**Access and reading limits.** Checked the RIKEN profile, listed methods and publications, and accessible sections of Dewa et al. 2025.

**Research direction.** Nagai’s laboratory investigates how glia contribute to neural circuits and behaviour. The RIKEN profile lists imaging, electrophysiology, molecular methods and controlled interventions in cellular signalling. Dewa et al.’s 2025 study provides a concrete example through a lasting astrocytic ensemble state associated with memory stabilisation. The research question concerns interactions between cell types rather than the properties of an isolated neuron alone.

**ORI critical analysis.** For this programme, the important issue is which variables belong in an explanatory model. If glia regulate conditions for plasticity, excluding their state could make a model incorrectly attribute slow effects to changes in neuronal connections. Expanding a model is justified, however, only when the added variable improves discriminating predictions and can be independently measured.

The group is worth following through the question of intervention specificity. An astrocyte-targeted manipulation should be examined for cellular selectivity, spread of effects and consequences for the tissue’s general condition. A behavioural change alone does not identify whether the intervention altered memory content, conditions for retrieval or physiological support for activity.

Studies that align fast neuronal responses with slow glial states within the same task would be especially valuable. They offer ORI a way to connect timescales without prematurely equating their functions. A practical monitoring output is a map of measured cellular states, proposed relationships and transitions supported by perturbation. Findings from one fear-learning model should not automatically be transferred to all learning or clinical conditions. The group is listed as a source of testable mechanisms and methods, not as a partner or evidence that ORI has adopted a general theory of glial memory.
