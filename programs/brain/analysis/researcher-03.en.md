# FlyWire, Mala Murthy and H. Sebastian Seung

[Русская версия](researcher-03.md)

**Access and reading limits.** Checked Murthy’s and Seung’s profiles and accessible sections of the connectome paper. The interactive resource was not independently used.

**Research direction.** Murthy’s profile connects the study of Drosophila social communication and behavioural flexibility with experimental and computational methods. Seung’s profile emphasises reconstruction of neural connections and the use of wiring maps to predict function. FlyWire’s adult connectome is a concrete example of these directions meeting. It is international, collective research infrastructure, so its achievement should not be attributed to a single laboratory leader.

**ORI critical analysis.** The most interesting transition for the programme is from a map to an experiment. A structural hypothesis becomes stronger when it specifies in advance how a response should change after intervention on a particular cell or connection. When function is assigned after observing behaviour, alternative explanations and multiple possible pathways require special attention.

Resource openness also has several levels. A public browser makes exploration easier, but reproducible analysis needs a data version, cell identifiers, filtering rules and a saved query. Reconstruction corrections can alter small circuits, making version provenance as important as convenient visualisation. Collective proofreading improves quality without automatically eliminating all residual errors.

The consortium should be followed through work that joins anatomy to dynamics and behaviour, compares animals and tests the portability of cell classifications. For memory, a distinct question is which structural observations genuinely constrain mechanisms of learning and which merely describe available routes. A useful ORI output would be a small, testable circuit account with explicit uncertainties, rather than a promise to explain the entire brain through one network map. This also provides a manageable unit for comparing structural predictions with physiological observations. Listing these researchers provides a public scientific orientation and does not imply collaboration with ORI.
