# Squire and Kandel — Memory: From Mind to Molecules

[Русская версия](book-03.md)

**Access and reading limits.** Checked 24 October 2026: the original publisher page was unavailable. The publisher-provided description reproduced in the Salk catalogue and the contents were read, and compared with the CARTA record. The book itself was not read.

**Orientation from accessible information.** The publisher description presents the book as an introduction connecting cognitive neuroscience and the molecular biology of memory. The second-edition catalogue contents distinguish declarative memory, skills, conditioning and other forms of learning, alongside short-term and long-term changes. This establishes the book’s intended role, but does not support judging its arguments in detail or attributing modern findings to its authors. The following is ORI’s own framework for a future critical reading.

**ORI critical analysis.** The first requirement is to avoid using “memory” as the name of a single process. Recognising an event, acquiring a skill and changing a response through repetition may require different tasks and different retention criteria. Comparison should begin with operational definitions rather than a shared storage metaphor.

The second requirement concerns the transition from a molecule to a remembered event. A lasting change in synaptic transmission might be necessary for particular learning without showing which information that change represents. Connecting a mechanism with content requires discriminating conditions and behavioural tests. Conversely, preserved performance after an intervention might reflect compensation by another system rather than the investigated process having no role.

The programme should also distinguish acquisition, stabilisation and retrieval. An apparently similar effect measured immediately or weeks later can support different explanations. A useful reading worksheet would record the memory type, organism, interval and form of causal evidence for each example. It could then be compared with recent engram and astrocyte studies without retrospectively rewriting the older book. The volume remains a starting point for organising concepts; its specific explanations still need examination against the complete edition when access is available.
