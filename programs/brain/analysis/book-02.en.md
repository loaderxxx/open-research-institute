# Kandel and colleagues — Principles of Neural Science, sixth edition

[Русская версия](book-02.md)

**Access and reading limits.** Checked the sixth-edition contents and preface on McGraw Hill’s platform. The substantive chapters were not read in full; the original retail URL did not load.

**Source content.** The sixth-edition preface defines an explanatory path from genes and molecules through cells and circuits to behaviour, prioritising principles over encyclopaedic detail. The contents cover signalling, perception, movement, development, memory and disease. The preface specifically discusses population recording and optogenetic and chemogenetic interventions as tools for investigating circuits. This is an educational synthesis of several research traditions rather than a single experiment with one common evidential standard.

**ORI critical analysis.** For the brain programme, the textbook is especially useful for checking transitions between explanatory levels. A change in gene expression cannot directly count as an explanation of memory without specifying how it affects a cellular process, circuit activity and measured behaviour. The reverse inference is also limited: successful task performance does not identify a unique molecular mechanism.

Reading should distinguish observation from intervention. A signal correlated with learning identifies a candidate process. A behavioural change following manipulation strengthens a causal argument, but sensory, motor and motivational side effects still need controls. This distinction is particularly helpful when interpreting engram studies in which an activity label participates in several stages of an experiment.

The book’s breadth creates a further risk: an orderly presentation can resemble a complete causal chain even when its evidence comes from different species and tasks. ORI should map those transfers explicitly, recording the organism, intervention and timescale. New connectomic and glial studies can be compared with foundational principles without automatically treating them as confirmation of every textbook generalisation. The useful output is a coherent vocabulary of measurements and mechanisms that supports reading specialist papers without conflating cellular, circuit and behavioural explanations.
