## Books

1. **Laurence F. Abbott and Peter Dayan, _Theoretical Neuroscience: Computational and Mathematical Modeling of Neural Systems_ (2001; paperback 2005).** [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262041997/theoretical-neuroscience/). A quantitative foundation for coding, neural circuits, plasticity, learning and memory. Metadata are open; print/e-book versions are sold by the publisher, and the full text is paid.
2. **Eric R. Kandel, John D. Koester, Sarah H. Mack and Steven A. Siegelbaum, _Principles of Neural Science_ (6th ed., 2021).** [McGraw Hill](https://www.mheducation.com/highered/product/principles-neural-science-kandel-koester/M9781259642234.html). A large reference from cellular neurophysiology to memory and behaviour, useful for connecting levels of evidence. Publisher page is open; full text is paid.
3. **Larry R. Squire and Eric R. Kandel, _Memory: From Mind to Molecules_ (2nd ed., 2009).** [Roberts & Company / publisher page](https://www.roberts-publishers.com/memory-from-mind-to-molecules.html). A focused introduction to biological memory systems and molecular mechanisms. Metadata are open; full text is commercial or library-access.

## Research and open data

1. **Dorkenwald et al. (2024), “Neuronal wiring diagram of an adult brain”.** [Nature, DOI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07558-y) · [FlyWire Codex](https://codex.flywire.ai/). An open connectome of an adult female Drosophila, with about 139,000 neurons and 54.5 million synapses, annotations and programmatic access. It supports circuit hypotheses but does not measure activity and represents one animal.
2. **Ko et al. (2025), “Systems consolidation reorganizes hippocampal engram circuitry”.** [Nature, DOI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08993-1). In mice, links time-dependent changes in hippocampal engrams and neurogenesis to a shift from precise memory toward generalisation. Open article; the model and memory tasks limit direct transfer to human autobiographical memory.
3. **Dewa et al. (2025), “The astrocytic ensemble acts as a multiday trace to stabilize memory”.** [Nature, DOI and data access](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09619-2). Identifies an astrocyte ensemble whose state after emotional experience and recall helps stabilise memory; single-cell data (GEO GSE272414) and RIKEN imaging data are released. Open article. The result broadens a neuron-centred account of memory but tests a specific mouse fear model.
4. **Albert Lin et al. / FlyWire Consortium (2024), “Network statistics of the whole-brain connectome of Drosophila”.** [Nature, DOI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07968-y). Analyses motifs and rich-club organisation in the whole-brain connectome and shares derived data in FlyWire Codex. Open article; connections and centrality alone do not prove computational or behavioural function.
5. **MICrONS Consortium (2025), “Functional connectomics spanning multiple areas of mouse visual cortex”.** [Nature, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08790-w). Combines functional activity with large-scale ultrastructural reconstruction in a fragment of mouse visual cortex; public data enable comparison of connectivity and activity. The sample is limited to a small cortical region and a visual task; consult the paper and linked portal for access conditions.

## Historical landmarks

- **1949 — Donald Hebb, _The Organization of Behavior: A Neuropsychological Theory_.** [Psychology Press / Routledge reprint (2002)](https://www.routledge.com/The-Organization-of-Behavior-A-Neuropsychological-Theory-1st-Edition/Hebb/p/book/9780415654531). The original Wiley book appeared in 1949; this card is for the 2002 reprint and does not support ISBN 9780807101329. Hebb proposed an influential hypothesis about connection plasticity during co-activity. It is a theoretical synthesis, not a direct modern measurement of synaptic learning.
- **1973 — Bliss and Lømo, long-lasting potentiation in rabbit hippocampus.** [Journal of Physiology, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.1973.sp010273). Experimentally described persistent strengthening of synaptic transmission after tetanic stimulation; an important candidate cellular mechanism, but not a complete explanation of memory.
- **2000 — Eric Kandel receives the Nobel Prize for work on memory storage in nerve cells.** [Official Nobel Prize overview](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2000/summary/). Aplysia studies linked short- and long-term memory to changes in synaptic function and gene expression; transfer of this specific model to complex mammalian memory requires care.

## Researchers and groups

- **Paul W. Frankland and Sheena Josselyn — Hospital for Sick Children / University of Toronto.** [Frankland’s institutional profile and publications](https://www.sickkids.ca/en/staff/f/paul-frankland/) · [2025 paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08993-1). In 2025 they co-authored a study of hippocampal engram-circuit reorganisation during memory consolidation; it links neurogenesis with changes in memory precision.
- **Jun Nagai — RIKEN Center for Brain Science, Laboratory for Glia–Neuron Circuit Dynamics.** [Institutional profile](https://cbs.riken.jp/en/faculty/j.nagai/) · [recent paper with open data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09619-2). He led the 2025 study of an astrocyte ensemble as a multiday memory trace; RIKEN confirms the group and lists the paper, while the paper links to data and code.
- **FlyWire Consortium / Mala Murthy and H. Sebastian Seung — Princeton Neuroscience Institute and international collaborators.** [Murthy profile and consortium](https://murthylab.princeton.edu/mala-murthy) · [Seung profile](https://pni.princeton.edu/people/h-sebastian-seung) · [connectome paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07558-y). Murthy and Seung co-led the work on the complete adult fly connectome; the team continues to connect the wiring map with dynamics and function. This is consortium activity, not a claim that one person “leads” neuroscience.

## Current directions and open questions

Work in 2024–2025 brought connection maps close to whole-brain scale in the fly and linked ultrastructure to functional measurements in mouse cortex; engram studies show that memory changes during consolidation and that glia also contribute to stabilisation. Publications from 2025 confirm recent activity. These advances do not close the gap between connectivity, neural dynamics and behaviour.

Open questions include how well one brain represents others; how to measure the causal contribution of a particular connection rather than merely its presence; how to reconcile trace stability with flexible generalisation; and what evidence distinguishes reconsolidation from new learning. Connectomes, activity and behavioural measures should be treated as different data modalities.

**Suggested next reading:** use Dayan–Abbott for the quantitative language of circuits and plasticity; then Hebb → Bliss–Lømo for the historical logic; finally compare Drosophila/FlyWire resources with the 2025 mouse studies of memory and astrocytes.
