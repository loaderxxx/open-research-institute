## Subject and main ideas

The textbook connects thermodynamics with quantum information. Its author abstract identifies three parts: thermodynamic foundations and their quantum formulation; cycles and work extraction; and information processing. This moves from describing a physical system to asking which resources are needed to control it. The intended reader already has grounding in quantum mechanics and statistical physics.

## Relevance to ORI

Our analytical interpretation of this scope is that information should be considered together with its physical carrier. Counting logical operations alone does not establish the efficiency of a computation. A comparison must specify the system, environment, control and measurement mechanisms, and what counts as work supplied or extracted. These are questions for constructing a future comparison, not results already produced by ORI.

## Limits and reading questions

An abstract cannot establish the correctness of derivations, the conditions of individual theorems or the completeness of experimental caveats. It also does not show that making a device quantum automatically improves its energy efficiency. Close reading should separate coherence, temperature, protocol speed and control costs. Quantum and classical comparators need a common accounting boundary; excluding a controller from only one system would make the comparison misleading.
