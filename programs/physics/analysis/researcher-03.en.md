## Direction and source

The Binghamton profile lists complex systems, self-organisation, artificial life and AI among Gershenson's interests. His 2025 review offers a personal account of the “what,” “how” and “why” of self-organising systems. It is explicitly an author's conceptual perspective rather than a definition agreed by everyone.

## Main significance

This research line is useful for comparing systems in which collective behaviour arises through interactions among elements. Our task is to preserve differences between mechanisms. Patterns in physical media, animal movement and human organisation can illustrate a common question, but similar vocabulary does not establish a shared causal law.

## Critical reading

For each example, readers should ask where the system boundary lies, what counts as external control, which variable represents order and whether another observational perspective changes the conclusion. Without those details, self-organisation risks becoming too broad a label. This is our suggested reading method, not a claim that the author neglects those difficulties. Concrete measurement choices should be recorded before attempting to compare very different domains.

## Practical use

For ORI, the profile helps assemble a language for interdisciplinary comparison before selecting a quantitative model. A useful next step is to choose two contrasting examples and identify observables, interactions and transfer limits for each. All applications in the review have not been independently checked.
