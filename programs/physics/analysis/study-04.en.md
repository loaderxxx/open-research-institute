# Minagawa et al. — Universal validity of the second law of information thermodynamics (2025)

[Русский](study-04.md) · [Programme](../README.en.md)

Checked: 25 October 2026. Initial source analysis; selected parts of arXiv v3 were read; not all proofs or a complete comparison with the journal version.

## Authors' question and result

The paper considers measurement, outcome-dependent feedback and erasure of controller memory. The authors characterise protocols compatible with the second law while relaxing earlier readout assumptions. In the accessible author version, the framework includes a target system, internal memory, a classical outcome register and thermal reservoirs. It is a mathematical investigation of consistency between descriptions.

## Interpreting universality

Our reading of the claim's boundary is that “universal” must remain attached to the problem's conditions. The protocols are assumed to be overall compatible with thermodynamics. This is neither an independent experimental proof of the second law nor a recipe for extracting work freely from information. The complete account includes the controller, rather than only its target.

## What still needs checking

The abstract, introduction and opening description of the framework were inspected. The theorem proofs and all special cases were not checked line by line, so mathematical completeness is not evaluated here. Further reading should track definitions of extracted and injected work, memory state after a cycle and assumptions concerning reservoirs. Changing those conditions may define a different physical problem.

## Programme relevance

For ORI, the article illustrates how specifying a model changes permissible claims about information and energy. A comparative review should relate its assumptions to classical bit erasure and to a particular experiment. The comparison should concern system boundaries and cycle-completion criteria, not merely similar terminology. Our next question is which elements of the theoretical protocol are actually controlled and measured in a laboratory.
