## Research direction

The Caltech profile identifies Preskill's central interests as quantum information, computation and error correction. Of particular relevance here are protecting quantum states against noise and establishing whether an experiment prepared the intended state. This is a research profile, not a description of an ORI partner.

## Concrete example

The 2025 paper by Huang, Preskill and Soleimanifar studies certification using relatively few single-qubit measurements. The authors prove a result for almost all target states. A theoretical certification guarantee should be distinguished from implementation on a particular physical device; neither substitutes for the other.

## Reading questions

Our questions are how closeness to the target is defined, what “almost all” means, which information about the target is assumed available, and which costs are included in the measurement count. A small experimental measurement count alone does not establish a small total cost for preparation, classical processing or target description. Those matters need checking in the complete proof and protocol rather than inference from a headline.

## Why follow this work

For ORI, these publications offer precise formulations of quantum-information reliability questions. A useful reading practice is to compare a research paper with teaching material while keeping conceptual explanation distinct from a new result. This initial profile neither covers the researcher's whole career nor ranks researchers. A subsequent review should separately record the system class, experimental resources and conditions under which a guarantee remains meaningful. Only the institutional profile and selected publication information were inspected here.
