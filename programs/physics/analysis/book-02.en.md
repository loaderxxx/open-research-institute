# Nielsen & Chuang — Quantum Computation and Quantum Information (2010)

Checked: 25 October 2026. Initial source analysis; the Cambridge record was inspected; full-text access was not obtained.

## What the book covers

The publisher presents a systematic introduction to quantum computation and information, including algorithms, teleportation, cryptography and error correction. Preparatory material connects quantum mechanics with computational concepts. Its value for this programme is a shared language in which physical states, permitted operations and information-processing tasks can be described consistently.

## Main significance for ORI

Our assessment is that the book can supply foundations for reading studies of logical qubits. A claim that errors have been reduced cannot be evaluated from the label “quantum.” Readers need to identify the encoded information, noise assumptions, measurements and comparison between recovered and intended states. The textbook should establish definitions; a contemporary paper should supply the particular experimental evidence.

## What needs caution

This is an initial assessment from the publisher record, not a page-by-page analysis. The book's prominence or number of exercises does not establish every practical promise associated with quantum technologies. A 2010 edition cannot establish the state of hardware in 2026. Conceptual foundations and current engineering achievements therefore require separate dates and sources.

## Close-reading task

An extended analysis should connect three questions: what constitutes a computational advantage, how measurement constrains access to a state, and which conditions permit error correction. Definitions and worked examples must be checked in the chosen edition rather than reconstructed from memory. Until full text is inspected, the precise argument structure, assumptions of particular results and suitability of individual sections for our present question remain unverified. This distinction keeps an introductory reading guide separate from a claim of comprehensive book coverage.
