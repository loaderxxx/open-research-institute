## Subject

The review asks how self-organisation can be described when structure itself lacks a precise mathematical definition. It surveys historical approaches and two modern lines: intrinsic computation and evolution operators. The authors propose a route towards describing organisation far from equilibrium. This is a review and theoretical programme rather than a single experiment.

## Main reading idea

Our interpretation is that a productive question is which distinctions among states are needed to predict subsequent dynamics. That moves the discussion from the appearance of a complicated pattern towards an explicit criterion of organisation. The usefulness of a representation still needs to be tested on specified systems and observations.

## Limitations and alternatives

Computational structure, thermodynamic entropy and biological function should not automatically be treated as the same quantity. They may concern different observables and tasks. For practical application, one must ask whether the inferred structure depends on resolution, observation duration and variable selection. A model describing one set of trajectories may fail in another environment or under another measurement scheme. Such sensitivity is a question to investigate, not a defect established by this initial reading.

## Use within ORI

We propose treating the review as a conceptual map for comparing models. A future analysis could describe the same dataset using several representations and test whether claims of organisation survive changes of scale. This is ORI's proposed study design, not a reported author result. The next step is to read definitions and examples before choosing a computational statistic and calling it a measure of self-organisation.
