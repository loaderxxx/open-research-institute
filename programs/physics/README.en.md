## Programme scope

The focus is on physical systems in which storing and transforming information can be connected to energy, dynamics and measurable quantities. The programme covers the thermodynamics of computation, self-organisation and quantum information processing. Comparing models requires attention to their assumptions, scale and relationship to observable processes.

## Programme questions

- **The cost of computation.** How do energy use, speed and accuracy constrain physical information processing?
- **Self-organisation.** Which interactions produce stable collective patterns, and when do those patterns change?
- **Quantum information.** How do entanglement, noise and error correction shape the possibilities of quantum computation?

## First stage: information gathering

The next step is to gather and organise information. The proposed sequence is:

1. Define the scope of the first review and a glossary of information, entropy, computation, collective behaviour and quantum error.
2. Gather review articles and key primary studies for the three programme questions. Record each study's problem, physical model, assumptions and testable implications.
3. Compare theoretical results, experimental tests and numerical models. Identify disagreements, open questions and the conditions under which conclusions apply.
4. Select one simple example for a future reproducible simulation and specify the required parameters, data and software.

## Planned materials

- An annotated bibliography explaining the role of each study.
- A map of models and concepts connecting information processing to physical constraints.
- A comparative review of information thermodynamics and self-organisation, with a section on quantum information.
- After gathering and assessing sources: a simulation specification, followed by code, parameters and an analysis of the result.
