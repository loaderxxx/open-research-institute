# Research map: voluntary governance

[Русская версия](RESEARCH_MAP.md) · [Programme README](README.en.md)

**Checked 24 October 2026.** This is a starting map, not a systematic review or an evaluation of a universal governance model. “Voluntary” here means rules jointly created or enacted, not an absence of power. Laboratory games, commons cases, field experiments and climate commitments provide different kinds of evidence.

## Books

- Elinor Ostrom, [*Governing the Commons*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/governing-the-commons/7AB7AE11BADA84409C34815CC288CD79), 1990, Cambridge UP. Comparative cases show how resource users create monitoring and dispute-resolution rules. Useful for common-resource institutions; it does not claim local self-governance always outperforms the state or markets. Contents are public; book is paid or library access.
- Graham Smith, [*Deliberative Democracy and the Environment*](https://www.routledge.com/Deliberative-Democracy-and-the-Environment-1st-Edition/Smith/p/book/9780415309400), 2003, Routledge. Examines public deliberation in environmental decisions; helps distinguish the existence of a forum from representation quality and influence. Theoretical and institutional analysis, not one experiment. Official publisher record is public; book is paid.
- Ghazala Mansuri & Vijayendra Rao, [*Localizing Development: Does Participation Work?*](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/handle/10986/11859), 2013, World Bank. Synthesises participation programmes and conditions for their effectiveness, including elite capture and weak monitoring. A useful counterweight to optimistic case studies. Full text and record are open.

## Research and data

- Ostrom, [“A General Framework for Analyzing Sustainability of Social-Ecological Systems”](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1172133), 2009, *Science*. Offers a framework of variables associated with self-organisation and sustainability in social-ecological systems. This is a synthesis and a structure for cumulative research, not one global test. Abstract is public on PubMed; publisher full text may require a subscription.
- Ostrom, [“Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems”](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.100.3.641), 2010, *American Economic Review*. Argues for studying multiple decision centres in complex collective problems and assessing local contributions. It is a theoretical synthesis of examples, not proof that polycentricity is best in every context. Article is available from AEA.
- Olken, [“Monitoring Corruption: Evidence from a Field Experiment in Indonesia”](https://doi.org/10.1086/517935), 2007, *Journal of Political Economy*. Increasing audit probability on rural road projects reduced unexplained expenditures; citizen mobilisation had a smaller effect in this design. Limit: one public programme and setting. Abstract is public; full text may be paid.
- UNFCCC, [first global stocktake under the Paris Agreement, decision 1/CMA.5](https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/resource/cma2023_16a01_adv_.pdf), 2023. The official multilateral climate-governance process noted widespread action but insufficient aggregate progress toward the agreement's goals. This is an agreed status assessment and political text, not a causal test of voluntary commitments. Document is public.
- Pascucci, [“Theorizing Polycentric Governance of Global Socio-Ecological Challenges through a Meso-Institutional Perspective”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1744137425100325), 2025, *Journal of Institutional Economics*. A theoretical article examines how multi-stakeholder partnerships among business, government and civil society may perform connecting functions where global institutions are weak. Abductive theory building uses three cases — SAI, WCF and RSPO — selected from 42 mapped partnerships; it is not a comparative causal estimate of effectiveness. Full text is open under CC BY.
- Eakin & Shearing, [“Emergent Polycentric Governance in Response to Drought”](https://doi.org/10.1002/eet.2141), online 2024, 2025 issue, *Environmental Policy and Governance*. A Cape Town water-service case during the 2017–18 drought traces corporate involvement, city coordination, incentives, transaction costs and feedback. Limit: one city and crisis case; it does not establish general model effectiveness. Abstract and record are public.

## Historical landmarks

- **1965 — collective action as a group-organisation problem.** Mancur Olson's [*The Logic of Collective Action*](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674537514) formulates incentive and free-riding problems; it is a baseline analytical model, not a claim that cooperation is impossible.
- **1990 — an empirical institutional alternative to the state/market binary.** Ostrom's book compares local commons regimes and failures of different arrangements ([Cambridge UP](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/governing-the-commons/7AB7AE11BADA84409C34815CC288CD79)). The cases depend on historical and resource conditions.
- **2010 — polycentricity enters comparative debates on global problems.** Ostrom called for studying contributions by multiple levels and centres of climate action ([AEA](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.100.3.641)); subsequent global assessments documented a gap between action and goals ([UNFCCC 2023](https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/resource/cma2023_16a01_adv_.pdf)).

## Current directions and next reading

New theoretical models of connecting institutions and partnerships appeared in 2025; tools for monitoring digital participation are also being updated. The question is not simply whether voluntary mechanisms “work”: inclusion, rule enforcement, benefit distribution, sanctioning capacity and links to larger institutions all matter. Global climate assessments show that broad commitments can coexist with insufficient aggregate results. Open questions include which partnerships survive conflicts of interest, how responsibility is distributed across levels, how symbolic participation can be distinguished from real influence, and who bears the costs of local rules.

**Next reading:** start with Ostrom (2009) as a framework, then Olken (2007) as a causal field test and UNFCCC (2023) as an official multilateral assessment. Compare units of analysis and evidential status, then select one resource or type of collective action.
