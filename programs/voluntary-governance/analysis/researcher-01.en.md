## Research question

Pascucci studies connecting institutional processes in environmental governance. A concrete example is his 2025 partnership article, which builds theory abductively from cases. Its author details list Exeter, Auckland and Wageningen. This verifies the affiliations reported for that publication rather than every current employment appointment.

## ORI critical analysis

For the programme, the valuable question is how participants act when general rules are incomplete or inconsistent. Willingness to cooperate is insufficient: someone must define standards, verify compliance and resolve interpretive disputes. Studying these functions reveals power often concealed by the apparently neutral term coordination.

Reading should distinguish explanations of institutional emergence from assessments of public value. An institution may align members’ interests successfully while excluding affected people from decisions. ORI should therefore examine representation, complaint procedures and the distribution of information access.

Theory building from cases is particularly useful for specifying mechanisms. Its limits appear when claims concern prevalence or average effects. Those questions require additional cases, including failures and alternative arrangements, together with outcome measures independent of a partnership’s self-description.

## Why follow this work

Pascucci’s research can enrich analysis of sectoral agreements and civic platforms by identifying who constructs operational rules between declarations and implementation. A practical further-reading task is to trace one dispute and establish whose arguments became binding standards. This would connect an institutional diagram to an observable distribution of influence. Inclusion in the research map expresses interest in publicly available work; it implies neither collaboration nor the researcher’s endorsement of ORI’s conclusions.
