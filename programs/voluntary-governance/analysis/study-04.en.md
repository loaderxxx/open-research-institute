## What the document is

The first global stocktake decision under the Paris Agreement recognises widespread climate action while finding aggregate progress insufficient. Participants in a multilateral process agreed the text. It provides a status assessment and political direction rather than an experiment comparing voluntary promises with compulsory regulation.

## ORI critical analysis

The document helps distinguish three levels: an announced goal, an implemented measure and an observed result. The number of initiatives can increase without adequate emissions reductions. Participation in an agreement therefore cannot serve as the final measure of effective collective action. Analysis must connect commitments to changed practices and practices to eventual impacts.

Responsibility is a further question. Aggregate insufficiency does not identify which participants failed to fulfil promises, whose promises were initially weak or who encountered capacity constraints. Explanation requires separate information on finance, implementation and starting conditions. An agreed political text cannot replace that investigation.

The programme label also needs precision. The Paris process combines nationally determined contributions with international procedures and domestic authority. Describing it as entirely nonbinding interaction would oversimplify the arrangement. Different components have different degrees of formalisation and consequences.

## Programme relevance

ORI can use the document as primary material on how numerous participants formulate a common assessment and revise expectations. A useful comparison would examine public reporting, independent verification and actual mechanisms for correcting deficiencies. The research question is when feedback changes action. Collective acknowledgement of a problem demonstrates an agreement procedure, but does not establish that subsequent measures will be sufficient or fair.

**Access and reading limits.** Opening provisions and selected paragraphs on aggregate progress and implementation gaps were inspected. The full decision package was not analysed paragraph by paragraph. The decision was adopted in 2023; the file was issued in March 2024.
