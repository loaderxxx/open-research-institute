## Question and material

Eakin and Shearing examine cooperation between Cape Town and corporate water providers during the 2017–2018 drought. Interviews trace motivations, coordination costs and responsibilities. The abstract reports that this collaborative arrangement ended after the drought, alongside evidence of learning. This is a qualitative analysis of one crisis case.

## ORI critical analysis

Cooperation emerging under threat of failure and cooperation surviving ordinary conditions are different questions. An urgent shared danger may temporarily align interests. Once it recedes, cost allocation can again become contentious. Ending an arrangement does not necessarily establish its complete uselessness, but requires identifying which functions persisted and who lost access.

Interviews reveal participants’ motives and interpretations of events. They do not automatically provide independent assessments of service quality or all residents’ welfare. ORI should particularly seek the perspectives of people outside negotiations: users with limited access, infrastructure workers and neighbouring communities. Their absence may alter the interpretation of cooperation.

Voluntary collaboration between organisations must also be distinguished from users’ freedom of choice. Someone dependent on shared water infrastructure may have little opportunity to reject an agreed provider. Joint city-business decisions therefore require assessment of public accountability.

## Programme relevance

The study is useful for observing institutions emerge while responsibilities are still being negotiated. Further work should compare interviews with decision documents, supply measures and cost distribution. That would help distinguish changed rhetoric, learning and durable changes in practice. Transfer to another location must consider infrastructure and existing power relations, not simply the presence of a crisis.
