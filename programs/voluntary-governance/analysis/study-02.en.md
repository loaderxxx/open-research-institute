## Subject and method

Ostrom synthesises research on multiple decision centres beyond a state-versus-market binary. The repository abstract of the lecture version discusses water provision and policing, where organisational scale relates differently to different costs and economies. This is a theoretical synthesis of a research programme; it does not estimate a single causal effect of polycentricity.

## ORI critical analysis

Analysis must distinguish organisational multiplicity from coordination. Several providers may offer choice and mutual scrutiny, but may also leave responsibility gaps. Counting participants does not identify who must act during failure, finance shared reserves or resolve jurisdictional disputes.

A second question concerns matching scale to function. Local knowledge may improve contact with users, while infrastructure requires joint investment. ORI should compare individual tasks instead of labelling an entire sector centralised or decentralised. A mixed system can allocate functions across levels and revise that allocation over time.

Inequality between centres also matters. A weaker organisation’s formal autonomy means little if a stronger partner controls information, funding or access to infrastructure. Evaluating a voluntary agreement under these conditions requires examining real alternatives and the cost of exit.

## Programme relevance

The paper supports testable questions about the compatibility of rules across levels. Further analysis should establish how participants detect failures, reallocate duties and answer to affected people. Demonstrating an advantage for a particular architecture requires comparing outcomes and costs with a realistic alternative. The mere existence of overlapping authority provides no such demonstration, just as the existence of a single centre does not by itself explain failure.
