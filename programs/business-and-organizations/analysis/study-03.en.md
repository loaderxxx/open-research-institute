## Question, method and result

The working paper combines a GitHub Copilot eligibility threshold with panel data on open-source developers’ activity. A regression-discontinuity design supports the quasi-experimental comparison. The authors report movement towards coding and away from project management, relating this to greater independence and exploratory activity. Potentially flatter hierarchies appear as a wider implication, not a directly measured reorganisation of firms.

## ORI critical analysis

A threshold design is informative about developers close to the eligibility boundary. Extending its effect to users with very different experience requires additional grounds. Full reading should examine whether participants could manipulate their position around the threshold, whether other benefits changed at the same boundary and how sensitive estimates are to bandwidth choices.

Changes in activity shares are meaningful but do not automatically show changes in absolute quantities. Coding’s share can rise when discussion decreases even without more useful output. Similarly, reduced collaboration could indicate independent problem-solving or lost knowledge exchange. Change quality, later corrections, project continuity and burdens on other participants could help distinguish those mechanisms.

## Programme relevance

The source supports comparisons of AI, specialisation and coordination. ORI should distinguish digital actions from tasks, tasks from roles, and roles from formal authority. Open repositories do not reproduce every contractual relationship within a firm. Testing the organisational implication would require evidence about decision approval and responsibility, beyond platform events. Authors’ Microsoft and GitHub affiliations and the platform’s involvement make measurement transparency particularly relevant. A preprint should not be presented as a completed test of organisational theory.
