## Research question and exemplar

CEPR places Stanton’s research in personnel and organisational economics. His coauthorship of Shifting Work Patterns with Generative AI provides a concrete programme example. The experiment tests individual provision of a tool within workplaces, allowing investigation of the boundary between personal action and activities dependent on colleagues. Checked institutional sources confirm his Harvard Business School affiliation.

## ORI critical analysis

The organisational significance extends beyond speed. An employee may independently change email preparation while lacking authority to cancel a meeting or reassign responsibility. Technology evaluation should therefore describe how each outcome depends on other participants. A collective process that does not respond to an individual tool is not necessarily immune to a different intervention.

Comparisons must account for the assignment level: employee, team or company. If colleagues assist one another, controls may receive indirect benefits. If joint rules remain fixed, individual savings may fail to change the process. These possibilities require investigation rather than selection of a convenient explanation after results are known.

## Why follow this work

For ORI, the line is relevant to incentives, training and decision rights. The programme should follow studies connecting tool access with workplace rules and measuring team output alongside individual employee experience.
