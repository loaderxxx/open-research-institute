## ORI critical analysis

Connecting employee evaluation with AI adoption raises an important organisational question: does underlying ability change, or only observed performance with assistance? When performance informs hiring, promotion and pay, tool access becomes part of the comparison conditions. Attributing an outcome entirely to an individual can be unfair or analytically mistaken when it substantially depends on unequal access to support. This is ORI’s question, not a finding asserted across all Li’s research.

Empirical analysis requires baseline experience, access conditions, usage decisions and outcomes after support changes. Joint observation of speed and quality can reveal situations in which standard productivity measures conceal deterioration in one aspect of service. Employee comparisons must also account for task differences and selection into the observed sample.

## Why follow this work

The research line helps connect individual decisions with organisational evaluation rules. ORI should follow work on the distribution of benefits across employees and on how tools alter the information underlying managerial choices. Each result needs assessment through the specific study; institutional affiliation cannot substitute for method. The profile implies no collaboration with ORI, and the selected article does not exhaust the researcher’s scholarly activity.
