## Content and type of evidence

The review organises measures of firms’ AI efforts and related economic research. Its abstract distinguishes invention from use, internal capability from outsourcing, and realised activity from investor perceptions. These are different objects of observation, so findings across datasets can diverge without logical contradiction. Published online in 2026, the article is a review rather than a single experiment with a common effect size.

## ORI critical analysis

A company may purchase an existing tool without patenting anything, while another patents technology without deploying it broadly. Treating both as identical AI adopters makes the estimate difficult to interpret. The action under investigation should be specified before choosing a measure. Job advertisements may indicate an intention to build capability without confirming hiring, usage or successful outcomes.

Causal direction presents a further difficulty. Successful firms may invest more in technology; an observed association between an AI measure and growth does not separate selection from adoption effects. Even changes over time require knowledge of actual usage dates and simultaneous reforms. Mixing expectations with current output is particularly problematic when assessing company valuation: markets can overestimate future possibilities.

## Programme relevance

For ORI, the review can inform a measurement dictionary. An evidence table should separately record the object measured, data source, time lag, firm coverage and potential omission of small organisations. Those fields would clarify when apparently conflicting estimates answer different questions. Full reading should examine literature selection and the quality of individual designs. A review does not remove the need to return to primary studies before making causal claims about productivity, employment or organisational structure.
