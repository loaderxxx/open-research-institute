## Content and form of argument

The survey connects organisational and industrial economics through the dependence of firm design on product-market value. Its abstract links prices and demand to scarce professional management, firm size, market power and internal control. This is a theoretical working paper: it surveys and extends models rather than reporting one estimated effect from a shared firm dataset.

## ORI critical analysis

The approach usefully reminds researchers that organisational structure may respond to external conditions. If demand rises alongside managerial hiring and output, the entire increase cannot immediately be attributed to management. Market change could explain both events. Empirical assessment should distinguish what caused organisational change from the consequences of the reorganisation itself.

Model predictions nevertheless depend on assumptions. Full reading should examine how managerial scarcity, owners’ objectives, contracts and competition are specified. The same observed expansion can fit several mechanisms. Theory is therefore more useful as a set of conditional predictions than as a universal account of why large companies have their observed structures.

## Programme relevance

For ORI, the framework suggests examining demand together with the internal allocation of authority. A company entering a larger market might change its approval procedures; the question is whether this follows from task scale or changing error costs. AI research should similarly distinguish cheaper information processing from higher demand for products. The survey itself does not establish that AI flattens hierarchies. The next step is to read the models and derive observable measures capable of separating competing explanations of organisational structure, with explicit boundaries on where each prediction should apply.
