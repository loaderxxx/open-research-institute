## Question, method and result

This peer-reviewed article studies staggered assistant deployment among 5,172 customer-support agents. Its main outcome is issues resolved per hour, improving by approximately 15% on average. Gains vary by experience. The strategy uses difference-in-differences and adoption-cohort dynamics. This is a quasi-experimental evaluation of a real workflow, not individually randomised tool assignment.

## ORI critical analysis

The central identification question is what would have happened to the same employees without access during the same period. Staggered deployment alone does not ensure comparability: teams with particular training or workloads might receive the tool first. Parallel trends, absence of anticipatory behaviour and robustness to heterogeneous effects across cohorts therefore matter. Diagnostic checks strengthen the argument without eliminating its conditions.

Resolved issues provide a useful output measure but do not exhaust service quality. Repeat contacts, case difficulty, correction costs and customer satisfaction should also enter evaluation. Nor can improvement in a single workflow be translated directly into the same percentage increase in company profits. Expenditure elsewhere in the organisation and implementation costs remain separate quantities.

## Programme relevance

Heterogeneity is particularly valuable for ORI: an average can conceal different consequences for novices and experts. Comparisons of deployments should define experience groups in advance, assess baseline skills and follow decisions after assistance is withdrawn. Such a design could distinguish durable learning from temporary support. One customer-service operation supplies a substantive example of knowledge transmission, but not an estimate covering every occupation or future AI version. Organisational evaluation should preserve that boundary when aggregating evidence.
