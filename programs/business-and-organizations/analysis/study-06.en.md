## Question, method and result

The article asks why internal capital allocations remain stable even when adaptation is needed. Using changes in decision rights among Illinois municipalities, the authors report less reallocation under centralisation. Reduced information-processing burdens under delegation are offered as a likely mechanism. The checked abstract characterises the variation as exogenous but does not allow independent assessment of every basis for that claim.

## ORI critical analysis

Changes in resource allocation must be separated from their quality. Frequent transfers may reflect adaptation to emerging needs or inconsistent management. Outcome measures are necessary to distinguish those explanations. For a public organisation these might include service access and fulfilment of obligations; for a company, product quality and achievement of objectives specified beforehand.

The information-load explanation also needs separation from alternatives. Delegation can simultaneously change incentives, political opportunities, approval delays and access to local knowledge. Even a convincing overall effect of an institutional change does not make every mechanism equally established. Full reading should determine which additional observations specifically support the informational interpretation.

## Programme relevance

The source connects bounded-rationality theory with observable resource decisions. Municipalities nevertheless differ from ordinary firms in budget rules, goals and accountability. Transfer requires describing who proposes changes, who authorises them and which information each actor receives. AI may reduce some processing burdens without eliminating conflicts of interest or making centralisation preferable. That proposition would require its own comparison of organisational arrangements. For ORI, the paper therefore motivates a precise governance question rather than a general recommendation to delegate all decisions.

**Access and reading limits.** Wiley’s research and managerial abstracts and journal metadata were inspected. The full text, source of exogenous variation and additional checks were not separately examined.
