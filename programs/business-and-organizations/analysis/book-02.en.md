## Content and form of argument

March and Simon connect organisational behaviour to the limits of human decision-making. The publisher identifies bounded rationality, satisficing, inducement–contribution balances, attention and uncertainty absorption as central concepts. This is a conceptual theory of organisations, not an experiment establishing a universal effect of a particular structure. The institutional record confirms the second edition in 1993. Available descriptions establish its thematic scope without allowing verification of the complete argument.

## ORI critical analysis

The practical meaning of bounded rationality is not that employees invariably make mistakes. Analysis must establish which information they receive, which alternatives they consider and when they stop searching. People facing the same incentives may decide differently because their workflows expose them to different signals and demands. Organisational problems therefore cannot automatically be reduced to inadequate individual motivation.

The programme should distinguish missing information from insufficient attention to information already available. An additional report may help in the first situation and increase overload in the second. Likewise, a standard procedure can reduce the cost of recurrent decisions, but its usefulness for an unfamiliar task requires separate investigation. These are analytical implications of the framework, not effects measured here.

## Programme relevance

AI creates a specific question: does a summary release attention or conceal the grounds for a conclusion? Evaluation needs original documents, decisions before assistance, accepted recommendations and subsequent corrections. Counting produced reports is insufficient. Further reading should clarify how the authors connect individual limitations with coordination between units and conflicts among goals. The framework can then be compared with contemporary field research without treating technology as the elimination of bounded rationality.
