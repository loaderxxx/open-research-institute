## Question, method and result

The preprint describes a three-year experiment involving 1,011 established small businesses in Nigeria. Random assignment to 88 hours of market-expansion support was compared with a control group. The abstract reports entry into additional customer segments and geographic areas, alongside business growth. Firms were followed after the intervention ended. This is primary field research, but the checked record remains a preprint rather than a peer-reviewed journal publication.

## ORI critical analysis

The question’s strength is its shift from improving performance within an existing market to finding new demand. Nevertheless, consulting can combine knowledge, planning discipline, contacts and an adviser’s attention. Randomising the package does not isolate each component’s contribution. Transfer requires knowing exactly what was delivered, how consistently entrepreneurs participated and who disappeared from follow-up.

One firm’s sales growth also differs from expansion of the total market. Some customers may move from competitors. Social impact therefore requires investigation of displacement, while participant returns require accounting for consulting costs, owners’ time and additional expansion expenditure. One successful intervention does not establish that every business-support programme works or that demand is the only constraint.

## Programme relevance

For ORI, the study opens a concrete line of inquiry into entrepreneurial decisions: how assumptions about new customers become testable actions. Comparison with AI could ask which consulting functions tools might support and which depend on relationships and local market knowledge. That is a separate research question. Full reading should reconstruct profit measures, attrition and programme costs before drawing any conclusions about scaling.
