## Argument of the article

Kipke argues for considering individual capacities and circumstances when assessing meaning in life. He distinguishes degrees of meaningfulness from qualitative individuality: a life’s uniqueness and the suitability of its activities for the person. Individuality is not itself an additional meaning-making factor. The article develops objectivist theory through conceptual distinctions and examples rather than psychological measurement.

## ORI critical analysis

The programme benefits from challenging automatic comparisons between ordinary lives and exceptional achievements. A further question follows: which constraints should count as given, and which are unjustly imposed by society? Treating all circumstances as fixed may inadvertently excuse deprivation of opportunities. Ignoring circumstances entirely makes evaluation insensitive to actual lives.

Personal suitability deserves separate attention. Biography helps explain why an activity matters especially to a particular person. Yet understanding a choice does not establish its value. Criticism of destructive projects must remain possible even when they fit coherently into someone’s history.

Knowledge poses another problem: outsiders cannot easily assess another person’s capacities and unavailable alternatives. This limits confidence in interpersonal comparisons. A person’s own assessment matters but can also change with circumstances and information.

## Programme relevance

The article helps refine discussion of meaning without turning life into an achievement contest. ORI can compare it with Wolf: personal engagement, suitability and objective value need not coincide. Further reading should examine the author’s responses to objections concerning inequality and assessment of capacities. Practical applicability needs independent discussion and cannot follow from a conceptual distinction alone. This makes the paper a resource for better questions, not a ready-made instrument for evaluating people.

**Access and reading limits.** The abstract, problem statement and central passages of sections 2–3 on individuality and proportionality were inspected. The full sequence of replies to objections was not analysed.
