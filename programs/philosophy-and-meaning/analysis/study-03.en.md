## Central argument

Ota examines the consequences of assuming that determinism excludes freedom required for basic-desert praise and blame. His claim is symmetrical: this diminishes not only positive meaning in agential life but also anti-meaning associated with blameworthy activity. The argument primarily addresses hard incompatibilists and uses hypothetical manipulation cases.

## ORI critical analysis

Its conditional character is essential. A conclusion about a position’s consequences does not establish that position’s truth. The paper neither determines the physical world’s structure nor measures the psychological effects of believing determinism. ORI should explicitly identify its premises about control, authorship and deserved evaluation.

The symmetry then needs examination. Even if grounds for blaming an agent weaken, harm to victims does not disappear. Reduced anti-meaning therefore cannot be converted into claims that consequences are insignificant or that repair is unnecessary. Those are distinct normative questions requiring additional arguments.

The move from external manipulation to general causal determination is also contestable. Readers should identify which feature of a case explains the judgment: lack of control, another person’s intention or disruption of ordinary decision processes. Different answers support different conclusions.

## Programme relevance

The paper connects meaning with agency and responsibility. ORI can use it to practise precise analysis of dependent claims: what remains if a disputed premise is rejected? Comparison with psychological scales requires another investigation because self-assessment and normative evaluation differ. Further reading should recover the strongest compatibilist responses rather than treating a thought experiment as a completed proof. The useful outcome is a clearer structure of disagreement.

**Access and reading limits.** The abstract, introduction and passages of the central argument and conclusion on meaning/anti-meaning symmetry were inspected. All notes and the cited debate were not examined.
