## Research question

Kansai University’s official page identifies Ota as a professor studying consciousness, free will, decision-making and ethical questions about technology. A concrete programme example is his 2025 article on determinism’s dual impact on meaning in life. It uses philosophical argument rather than an experimental intervention.

## ORI critical analysis

This direction is useful for discussing authorship of life projects. If circumstances or technological environments shape a decision, analysis should specify what changes: control, responsibility, experienced authorship or the result’s value. These concepts are related but not interchangeable. ORI should inspect each transition between them.

Philosophy of mind and empirical behavioural research also address different tasks. Evidence may show that conditions influence choices without automatically disproving every form of freedom. Conversely, a conceptual defence of agency does not establish people’s ability to resist a particular influence. Productive comparison requires a shared question.

Thought experiments deserve particular attention. They reveal implications of premises, so their force depends on which elements can be changed. Application to actual technologies requires establishing the facts anew rather than treating an imagined form of control as a demonstrated technical capability.

## Why follow this work

Ota’s work connects meaning with responsibility and human relationships to intelligent systems. A promising ORI reading task is to compare positive authorship of achievements with negative responsibility for harm. This should preserve the distinction between evaluating agents and evaluating consequences for others. The current profile clarifies professional context without implying consensus in these debates. Mentioning the researcher does not imply his participation in the programme.
