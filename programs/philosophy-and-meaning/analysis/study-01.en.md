## Subject of the survey

The entry organises contemporary analytic discussion around what the meaning question asks, which conditions confer meaning and whether meaningful life is possible. It compares supernaturalist, naturalist and sceptical approaches while distinguishing subjectivist and objectivist foundations. The author explicitly limits its philosophical scope. This is a reference synthesis rather than a representation of every global tradition.

## ORI critical analysis

The survey is useful as a map of logical possibilities. It helps show that religiosity and objectivism are not a single axis: the source of value differs from meaning’s dependence on desires. Collapsing those distinctions creates false oppositions and obstructs understanding of an opponent’s argument.

Using the map requires separating description from endorsement. A theory’s appearance in an encyclopedia does not indicate consensus or equal plausibility among its versions. Simplified classifications can also conceal internal disagreements and borderline cases.

The limitation of tradition has substantive significance. If an entry concentrates on analytic normative theories, omission of other approaches cannot count as evidence of their weakness. ORI should independently justify expanding the corpus and compare which questions different traditions consider central.

## Programme relevance

The source provides an initial vocabulary and a route to further reading. A practical exercise is to formulate one disputed premise, identify arguments on both sides and then consult the primary works. Psychological measurement should remain separate: it may describe experienced meaning without automatically choosing between accounts of its value. Specifying the archive makes reading reproducible; it does not turn an earlier survey into a review of everything published by the end of 2025.
