## Design and result

The authors developed a scale using two sequential convenience samples totalling 646 Peruvian adults recruited through social networks and researchers’ contacts. Factor analysis and model refinement produced a four-item, single-factor presence-of-meaning scale. This is preliminary psychometric validation rather than a representative population estimate or intervention evaluation.

## ORI critical analysis

The central issue is independence of confirmation. If an initial model fits poorly and is substantially revised using the same data, final fit may partly reflect sample-specific features. Refinement does not make an instrument useless, but requires testing the final version on new participants. ORI should distinguish development from confirmation.

Convenience recruitment also limits generalisation. Internet access, social networks and willingness to respond can affect participation. Geographical diversity alone cannot remove this selection. Transfer requires investigating groups poorly represented in the original recruitment.

Brevity has a cost: a few similar questions can agree closely while covering only a narrow part of experience. Psychometric indices should therefore be considered alongside the remaining items’ content. Association with life satisfaction establishes neither conceptual identity nor a causal direction.

## Programme relevance

The article provides a concrete example of contemporary scale construction and its decisions. ORI should locate or request available materials, examine the sequence of item exclusions and seek independent validation. Until then, the instrument should not become a cross-cultural ranking of meaningfulness. Even successful replication of its statistical structure would leave the philosophical question of what makes life valuable unanswered. Measurement can sharpen empirical questions while preserving that distinction.
