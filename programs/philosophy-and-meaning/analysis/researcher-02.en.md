## Direction of work

Kipke works in practical philosophy at Bielefeld University. His profile includes theories of good and meaningful lives, human dignity, applied ethics and political philosophy. The 2025 article on individuality provides a concrete example of conceptual analysis asking how a theory relates to a particular person’s life.

## ORI critical analysis

The programme can use this work to examine the gap between general principles and personal situations. A theory may correctly identify valuable activities while telling someone with limited resources little about relating them to their history. Insufficiency of a principle should be distinguished from insufficient information for applying it.

A second question concerns the limits of individualisation. Sensitivity to circumstances should not turn unjust constraints into accepted norms. When exclusion restricts opportunities, adjusting evaluations does not replace questions about institutional change. ORI should preserve both levels of discussion.

Normative examples also require scrutiny. They can reveal overlooked cases, but their persuasiveness depends on descriptions and readers’ initial judgments. Changing individual conditions and checking whether the conclusion survives is therefore useful. This is a way to examine arguments rather than a substitute for statistical experimentation.

## Why follow this work

Kipke’s publications are relevant where theories of meaning meet human dignity. Further reading could compare assessments of capacities, identification with projects and moral requirements. None follows automatically from the others. The institutional profile establishes breadth of interest without validating every possible practical application. Including the researcher identifies a public interlocutor for critical reading; it implies neither collaboration nor approval of the programme’s content.
