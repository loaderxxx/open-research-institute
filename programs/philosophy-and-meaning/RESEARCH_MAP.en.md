# Research map: philosophy and meaning

[Русская версия](RESEARCH_MAP.md) · [Programme README](README.en.md)

**Checked 24 October 2026.** This is a starting map, not a systematic review. Normative arguments about the value of a life and empirical scales of experienced meaning answer different questions. Neither a correlation between self-report and health nor a philosophical theory by itself establishes the objective value of a particular life.

## Books

- Thaddeus Metz, [*Meaning in Life: An Analytic Study*](https://academic.oup.com/book/10038), 2013, Oxford UP. Compares supernaturalist and naturalist theories and defends an objectivist view connecting meaning with intelligent orientation toward fundamental conditions of human existence. A detailed map of analytic debate; its value theory is contested. Publisher abstract is public; book is generally paid or library access.
- Susan Wolf, [*Meaning in Life and Why It Matters*](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691154466/meaning-in-life-and-why-it-matters), 2010 (paperback edition 2012), Princeton UP. Develops a hybrid view: a meaningful life combines subjective engagement with objectively valuable activity. A concise source for comparing subjectivism and objectivism; a normative argument, not a psychometric study. Publisher record and library metadata are available; book is paid or library access.
- Thaddeus Metz & Joshua Seachris, [*What Makes Life Meaningful? A Debate*](https://www.routledge.com/What-Makes-Life-Meaningful-A-Debate/Metz-Seachris/p/book/9781032566061), 2023 (copyright 2024), Routledge. A dialogue comparing theories of meaning and presenting objections alongside replies. Publisher record is public; book is paid.

## Publications and measurement tools

- Metz, [“The Meaning of Life”](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2025/entries/life-meaning/), Winter 2025 archive, *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*; the stated substantive revision is 9 February 2021. The survey organises the meta-question “what does meaning in life mean?” and theories of what makes a life meaningful; it states its focus on Anglo-American analytic philosophy and exclusions of many other traditions and disciplines. Full text is open; this is a philosophical reference synthesis, not empirical research.
- Kipke, [“The Individuality of Meaning in Life”](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejop.13050), 2025, *European Journal of Philosophy*. Argues that objectivist theories should account for the individuality of life projects and practical usefulness to the person living them. A normative conceptual article; it does not test psychological consequences. Full text is open.
- Ota, [“The Dual Impact of Determinism on Meaning in Life”](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11406-025-00877-y), 2025, *Philosophia*. Analyses how determinism and doubts about free will may both threaten and alter the conditions of meaning. This is philosophical argument, not a well-being experiment; full text is open.
- Steger, Frazier, Oishi & Kaler, [“The Meaning in Life Questionnaire: Assessing the Presence of and Search for Meaning in Life”](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0167.53.1.80), 2006, *Journal of Counseling Psychology* 53(1), 80–93. Introduces a 10-item self-report scale with presence and search subscales. Useful for measuring experienced meaning, but validation does not turn the score into a measure of a life's objective value. Abstract is public; full text may require a subscription.
- Guevara-Tantalean et al., [“Development and Psychometric Properties of a Scale to Measure the Meaning of Life”](https://doi.org/10.3390/ejihpe15090174), 2025, *European Journal of Investigation in Health, Psychology and Education*. Develops and tests a meaning-of-life scale, showing ongoing local psychometric work. Limit: validation in a specific sample and language does not guarantee cross-cultural measurement invariance. Full text is open on PMC/publisher platform.

## Historical landmarks

- **4th century BCE — Aristotle on human good.** Book I of the [*Nicomachean Ethics*](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0054%3Abook%3D1) connects the good with activity in accordance with virtue. This is a normative teleological position; translation, historical context and social exclusions require critical reading.
- **1940s — existential and logotherapeutic accounts of personal meaning.** Viktor Frankl presented meaning as a person's response to the concrete demands of life ([publisher record for *Man’s Search for Meaning*](https://www.beacon.org/Mans-Search-for-Meaning-P697.aspx)). This is personal testimony and a philosophical-therapeutic position, not a controlled clinical study.
- **1990s–2000s — a distinct analytic field and psychometric scales take shape.** Philosophical theory increasingly separated the meaning of a person's life from whether the cosmos has meaning, while psychology developed self-report measures, including the 2006 MLQ. The SEP surveys the philosophical field; the scale measures subjective responses, not normative value ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2025/entries/life-meaning/), [MLQ](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0167.53.1.80)).

## Current questions and next reading

In 2025, normative debates on objective value and individuality of meaning continue alongside applied work on psychological scales. These strands cannot be collapsed into one metric. Open questions include how to compare subjective engagement with objective grounds of value, whether scales can be translated across languages and cultures without changing the construct, which groups and traditions are underrepresented in analytic literature, and how theories apply to work, care, creativity or political institutions without turning philosophical claims into clinical conclusions.

**Next reading:** read the SEP survey, then compare Wolf's view with Kipke's argument. Separately inspect the MLQ method and the newer local scale validation; record sample, language and scale construction, and what self-report does not measure.
