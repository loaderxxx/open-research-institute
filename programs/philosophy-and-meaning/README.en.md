# Philosophy and Meaning

[Русский](README.md)

[Research map](RESEARCH_MAP.en.md) · [Карта литературы на русском](RESEARCH_MAP.en.md)

**Central question:** How can we discuss values and meaning while preserving the distinction between argument and measurement?

This program compares philosophical accounts of a good and meaningful life, pluralism, and public justification. One topic is the psychological measurement of the experience of meaning and its limits. We explain the assumptions behind arguments, strong objections, and disagreements that cannot be resolved by a single empirical scale.

## Program questions

- What is the difference between happiness, moral value, and meaning?
- How do subjective, objective, and hybrid theories answer objections?
- What does self-report about meaning measure, and what does it fail to prove about the value of a life?

## What the sources report

### PHI-01
Aristotle connects human good with activity in accordance with virtue; this is a normative claim.

**Limitations:** The teleological assumption and the exclusionary social context of the original require critical reading.

**Sources:** [P1: Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics, book I](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0054%3Abook%3D1)

### PHI-02
Verse 5 of the Dhammapada contrasts hatred with the absence of hatred.

**Limitations:** Translation and textual history matter; one verse does not represent all Buddhist philosophy.

**Sources:** [P2: Dhammapada, Bhikkhu Sujato translation, verse 5](https://suttacentral.net/dhp1-20/en/sujato)

### PHI-03
Susan Wolf connects meaning with engagement in projects of independent value and distinguishes meaning from happiness and morality.

**Limitations:** The idea of objective value is disputed.

**Sources:** [P3: Susan Wolf 2010, Meaning in Life and Why It Matters](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/ROBERT49/teaching/ph103/pdf/WolfSusanne_2010_Meaning-in-Life-and-Why-It-Matters.pdf)

### PHI-04
Thomas Nagel explores the absurd through the human capacity to question one's own commitments in life.

**Limitations:** This is a philosophical argument, not clinical evidence; competing interpretations exist.

**Sources:** [P4: Thomas Nagel 1971, The Absurd](https://philosophy.as.uky.edu/sites/default/files/The%20Absurd%20-%20Thomas%20Nagel.pdf)

### PHI-05
Rawls's public reason and Sen's public discussion offer different arguments for pluralistic political justification.

**Limitations:** Questions remain about excluding marginalized voices and about institutional assumptions.

**Sources:** [P5: John Rawls 1997, The Idea of Public Reason Revisited](https://chicagounbound.uchicago.edu/uclrev/vol64/iss3/1/); [P6: Amartya Sen 1999, Democracy as a Universal Value](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/democracy-as-a-universal-value/)

### PHI-06
The MLQ separates self-reports of the presence of meaning from the search for meaning.

**Limitations:** Psychometrics does not establish objective value; translation and measurement portability require checking.

**Sources:** [P7: Steger, Frazier, Oishi & Kaler 2006, The Meaning in Life Questionnaire](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/the-meaning-in-life-questionnaire-assessing-the-presence-of-and-s/)

## How to read this review

A claim made by a source does not mean its result was independently replicated. Philosophical arguments, correlations, and causal experiments have different evidential status. Reading depth and limitations are listed in the [source register](../../data/sources.json).
