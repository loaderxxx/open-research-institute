## Research question and exemplar

The LSE profile connects Ginges’s research with cooperation across cultural boundaries and strong commitments to collective ideas. His methods include controlled psychological field experiments. Verified examples are coauthorship of In Their God We Trust and the Fiji study of religious perspective under conflict salience. These papers ask specific questions about thought and behaviour during interaction.

## ORI critical analysis

The programme benefits from examining cooperation and conflict together without treating either as simply the absence of the other. Strong commitment can support collective action, but consequences depend on its purpose and everyone affected. Willingness to help one’s own group does not automatically reveal attitudes towards outsiders.

Field research is closer to everyday context than an abstract laboratory task, yet remains a study of a particular sample and procedure. Even within a conflict region, a monetary game does not reproduce every risk of political action. Psychological explanations should therefore connect to institutional conditions cautiously and through additional evidence.

## Why follow this work

ORI should follow research separating personal belief, beliefs about others’ beliefs and actual choices. Cases where expectations of hostility diverge from partners’ behaviour are especially useful. They generate testable questions about mutual misunderstanding without turning a psychological intervention into an immediately applicable political recommendation.

Interest in the researcher implies neither partnership with ORI nor acceptance of an entire research programme. Each publication needs independent assessment of measurement, context and alternative explanations. Systematic comparison should include limitations and unsuccessful tests as well as striking positive findings. The institutional profile identifies a relevant direction; it does not certify every interpretation drawn from it.

**Access and reading limits.** LSE’s description of research questions and methods, author lists and abstracts of two joint papers were inspected. Full texts were not examined.
