## Research question and exemplar

UBC describes Shariff’s social-psychological research at the intersection of morality, religion and cultural attitudes. A concrete programme example is his coauthorship of the 2024 meta-analysis with Kelly and Kramer. It compares ways of measuring prosociality and shows why conclusions about religiosity depend on outcome type. This is a research synthesis rather than one new experiment.

## ORI critical analysis

A particular value of this direction is scrutiny of research instruments themselves. When religiosity and helping are measured through similar self-reports, shared response styles can affect their association. Yet brief behavioural tasks may also miss important forms of assistance. The scientific question is which observations agree across methods and where divergence arises.

Synthesis requires distinguishing an average effect from the distribution of findings. A small average does not establish that every context lacks a strong effect, while a positive average does not imply the same association in all groups. Research conditions, definitions of religion and beneficiaries must remain visible after aggregation.

## Why follow this work

ORI should follow subsequent studies connecting explanatory mechanisms with precise outcomes and public materials supporting verification of study coding. It is especially important to distinguish observed behaviour, a person’s self-conception and researchers’ moral evaluations.

The UBC profile implies no connection to ORI, and reading one abstract does not constitute assessment of Shariff’s entire scholarly activity. This reading direction is selected for its methodological question about alignment between concepts and measurements. Developing that question helps prevent the simplistic inference that one correlation determines religion’s social value. The emphasis is on evidence structure rather than institutional prestige.

**Access and reading limits.** The UBC profile and abstract of the Kelly–Kramer–Shariff meta-analysis were inspected. Neither a complete publication review nor verification of the meta-analytic calculations was performed.
