## Question, method and result

Three preregistered studies involve 1,784 participants in the United States, Israel and Fiji. The authors examine trust and reciprocity in monetary games when thoughts of God become salient. The abstract reports improvements within and across religious groups. Online publication occurred in July 2025, with the journal issue dated August 2026. The measured intervention is a particular reminder among the sampled participants.

## ORI critical analysis

Trust is an expectation about another person, while reciprocity is that person’s subsequent action. They can diverge: a group might be underestimated despite equivalent behaviour. Both sides of the interaction should therefore be examined without collapsing them into an ambiguous prosociality score. Real monetary consequences make the task meaningful but do not make it a complete analogue of friendship, commerce or political cooperation.

Preregistration limits some analytical flexibility without guaranteeing external validity. Full assessment should check primary outcomes, exclusions and variation between research sites. Three cultural contexts broaden coverage beyond one sample without representing every religious tradition or conflict.

## Programme relevance

ORI can use the work to clarify religious-cooperation hypotheses. Reminders of a divine perspective and stronger religious group identification need not constitute identical interventions. Further reading should reconstruct exact instructions, game design and dependence on prior belief. Even a causal effect of a brief reminder does not show that conversion would change a person similarly or identify the causes of peace between societies. The contribution is a more specific behavioural question, whose scope should remain attached to its manipulation and outcomes.

**Access and reading limits.** The author abstract and bibliographic details on PubMed were inspected. Protocols, full text and individual-level data were not read.
