# Sources and Reading Depth

[Русский](SOURCES.md)

This register covers the in-depth reviews in this program. Checked: 23 September 2026. Access to an abstract does not mean the full article was read.

## R01 — Cooperation and Commune Longevity: A Test of the Costly Signaling Theory of Religion

Source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1069397103037002003

Year: 2003. Reading depth: publisher abstract and author-manuscript search result.

Limitations: Historical sample of 83 communes; survival is not well-being; selection and archival differences.

## R02 — Moralistic gods, supernatural punishment and the expansion of human sociality

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature16980

Year: 2016. Reading depth: publisher abstract and publication metadata.

Limitations: Cross-sectional games in eight societies; distant co-religionists, not all outsiders; no causal identification.

## R03 — Extreme Rituals Promote Prosociality

Source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797612472910

Year: 2013. Reading depth: publisher record and excerpts from author-hosted PDF search results.

Limitations: Mauritius field context; participation selection, arousal, observer effects, donation to temple.

## R04 — Collective ritual and social support networks in rural South India

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5998092/

Year: 2018. Reading depth: primary-article abstract and search excerpt; access to PMC was challenged.

Limitations: Observational network ties; causality and portability are limited.

## R05 — Synchrony and Physiological Arousal Increase Cohesion and Cooperation in Large Naturalistic Groups

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-18023-4

Year: 2018. Reading depth: full HTML introduction, design, and discussion inspected selectively.

Limitations: Specific naturalistic group tasks; synchrony and shared affect are difficult to disentangle.

## R06 — The Boundaries of Trust: Cross-Religious and Cross-Ethnic Field Experiments in Mauritius

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10543958/

Year: 2018. Reading depth: abstract and search excerpt; access to PMC was challenged.

Limitations: Site-specific trust games; costly signals are an imperfect indicator of disposition.

## R07 — Advertising cooperative phenotype through costly signals in a public goods experiment

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9128853/

Year: 2022. Reading depth: abstract and search excerpt; access to PMC was challenged.

Limitations: Experimental signaling task is not a religious ritual; group net gains depend on costs.

## R08 — Prosocial correlates of transformative experiences at secular multi-day mass gatherings

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-29600-1

Year: 2022. Reading depth: full HTML abstract, methods synopsis, results, discussion, and limitations.

Limitations: Self-selected US/UK gatherings; generosity did not rise onsite; later association is not causation.

## R09 — Markets, Religion, Community Size, and the Evolution of Fairness and Punishment

Source: https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1182238

Year: 2010. Reading depth: publisher-abstract search result.

Limitations: Market integration and community size covary with religion; game measures differ.

## R10 — Religious Identity and Economic Behavior

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6402818/

Year: 2016. Reading depth: abstract and search excerpt; access to PMC was challenged.

Limitations: Laboratory primes; directional effects are specific to denomination and context.

## R11 — Meta-analyses are no substitute for registered replications

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4569810/

Year: 2015. Reading depth: abstract and search excerpt; access to PMC was challenged.

Limitations: Methodological critique of a priming synthesis, not evidence against every religion–behavior pathway.

## R12 — The Church, intensive kinship, and global psychological variation

Source: https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aau5141

Year: 2019. Reading depth: publisher abstract and metadata search result.

Limitations: Historical exposure proxies and correlational identification; communities and practices are heterogeneous.

## R13 — Increased Affluence Explains the Emergence of Ascetic Wisdoms and Moralizing Religions

Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0960982214013724

Year: 2015. Reading depth: publisher highlights and abstract.

Limitations: Macrohistorical modeling and dating; alternative channels of prosperity are possible.

## R14 — Complex societies precede moralizing gods throughout world history — RETRACTED

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1043-4

Year: 2019. Reading depth: publisher record and accompanying 2021 retraction note.

Limitations: Retracted in 2021 over treatment of missing data; the original temporal result must not be used as established.

## R15 — Retraction Note: Complex societies precede moralizing gods throughout world history

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03656-3

Year: 2021. Reading depth: full publicly displayed retraction text.

Limitations: Authors dispute the interpretation; three coauthors did not agree. The note records status rather than providing an independent test.

## R16 — Treatment of missing data determined conclusions regarding moralizing gods

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03655-4

Year: 2021. Reading depth: publisher/author-repository search excerpts and metadata.

Limitations: Critique of particular Seshat coding and timing, not a definitive universal history.

## R17 — Disentangling the evolutionary drivers of social complexity

Source: https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abn3517

Year: 2022. Reading depth: publisher-abstract search result.

Limitations: Longitudinal historical inference depends on model and coding.

## R18 — Cross-cultural dataset for the evolution of religion and morality

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5100682/

Year: 2016. Reading depth: abstract and search excerpt; access to PMC was challenged.

Limitations: Harmonizing measurements improves comparisons but does not solve representativeness.

## R19 — Large-scale cooperation driven by reputation, not fear of divine punishment

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6731744/

Year: 2019. Reading depth: abstract and search excerpt; access to PMC was challenged.

Limitations: Reputation-based interpretation is study-specific; it does not rule out punishment in other contexts.

## R20 — What is the association between religious affiliation and children's altruism?

Source: https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822%2816%2930670-4

Year: 2016. Reading depth: publisher record/search excerpt.

Limitations: Commentary/reanalysis of a child-altruism claim; narrow allocation task and family contexts.
