# Religion, Ritual, and Cooperation

[Русский](README.md)

**Central question:** How are religious beliefs, rituals, and joint action related?

This program examines religion through specific research questions about trust, group belonging, ritual practice, and institutions. We compare field, laboratory, and historical findings while taking cultural context and alternative explanations into account. Debates about causality and changes to historical data are part of the review.

## Program questions

- What conclusions do studies of ritual and prosocial behavior support?
- Is cooperation within a group the same as the well-being of everyone affected?
- How do missing historical data, selection, and article retraction change conclusions?

## What the sources report

### REL-01
In a historical sample of communes, the study examined the relationship between demands placed on participants and community longevity.

**Limitations:** Archival survival, selection, differences in ownership, and state protection limit transfer; participant well-being was not established.

**Sources:** [R1: Sosis & Bressler 2003, Cooperation and Commune Longevity](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1069397103037002003)

### REL-02
Across eight communities, beliefs in punitive and knowledgeable gods were associated with larger allocations in games to distant co-religionists.

**Limitations:** This is a cross-sectional association; social desirability and shared norms may have influenced it.

**Sources:** [R2: Purzycki et al. 2016, Moralistic gods, supernatural punishment and the expansion of human sociality](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature16980)

### REL-03
One field study links extreme ritual to prosocial donations in the context studied.

**Limitations:** Arousal, public signaling, and selection are possible factors; a universal long-term effect has not been established.

**Sources:** [R3: Xygalatas et al. 2013, Extreme Rituals Promote Prosociality](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797612472910)

### REL-04
The 2019 Nature article using Seshat data was retracted; the subsequent revision and the dispute about missing data should be distinguished by version.

**Limitations:** A revision does not restore the retracted original article's status.

**Sources:** [R0: Whitehouse et al. 2019, Complex societies precede moralizing gods throughout world history](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1043-4); [R4: Nature retraction note, Whitehouse et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03656-3); [R5: Whitehouse et al. 2022, corrected retake using Seshat](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A03572828-5bf1-4bd9-b0d4-4186a2860260); [R6: Treatment of missing data determined conclusions regarding moralizing gods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03655-4)

## How to read this review

A claim made by a source does not mean its result was independently replicated. Philosophical arguments, correlations, and causal experiments have different evidential status. Reading depth and limitations are listed in the [source register](../../data/sources.json).
