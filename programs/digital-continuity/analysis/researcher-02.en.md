## Research question

Binz's profile describes human learning and decision research using deep learning, reinforcement learning, information theory and Bayesian inference. The general aim is to develop computational accounts of behaviour that can be compared with experiments. This direction should not automatically be equated with building a copy of an individual person.

## Example study

The Centaur paper fine-tunes a language model on the large Psych-101 collection of psychological experiments to predict human responses across tasks. The profile, abstract and university publication record were checked. The authors' broad description of experiments expressible in natural language is treated as a proposed scope, not a replacement for validation on each new experiment.

## ORI significance

Our interpretation distinguishes average predictive accuracy, transfer across tasks and fidelity to a particular individual. A model might predict common choices in a sample while missing one participant's stable characteristics. An evaluation must decide in advance which differences matter and how they will be measured. Biographical similarity in generated text also does not establish subjective experience.

## Limitations and next step

This entry did not audit every data split, possible training–test overlap or all reported comparisons. Follow-up should separately test new tasks, new participants and changed instructions. Error patterns deserve attention alongside average performance. The group is an external scientific reference; neither collaboration with ORI nor an achieved digital continuation of a person is asserted.
