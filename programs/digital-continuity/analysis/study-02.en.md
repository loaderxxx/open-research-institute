## What was studied

The authors reconstructed about one cubic millimetre of human temporal cortex removed during surgical access. The abstract reports approximately 57,000 cells, 150 million synapses and 1.4 petabytes of data. Figure captions describe fixation, thin sectioning, electron microscopy, image alignment and segmentation correction. These materials were inspected, rather than the complete main text and supplements.

## Scientific significance

Our interpretation is that the resource demonstrates how much information and processing even a small structural fragment requires. Image volume, annotation quality and a model's explanatory power should be distinguished. A large storage requirement does not imply a complete description of a person's function, biography or internal state.

## Boundaries

Fixed tissue is not a recording of continuing live activity. Volume boundaries truncate connections, and one specimen does not establish the distribution of differences between people. The cell count includes multiple classes; it must not be relabelled as a neuron count. This analysis did not estimate reconstruction errors, inspect the donor's personal records or recalculate the reported quantities.

## Next questions

A useful follow-up would select a bounded anatomical question, establish completeness of the necessary structures and test sensitivity to segmentation errors. Moving to a functional model requires explicit additional assumptions and measurements. Moving to a claim about personal identity requires different criteria, which this resource does not provide. This sequence recognises a strong connectomics contribution while keeping its inferential scope precise.
