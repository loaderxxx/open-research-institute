## Subject

The publisher presents the book as an accessible introduction to connectomics and the idea that neural wiring helps explain human individuality. Its description moves from individual differences to treatment possibilities and hypothetical preservation of a person. This is a useful research agenda, but promises in a publisher description should be distinguished from experimental findings.

## ORI interpretation

A wiring map may provide an important constraint on a model; important does not mean sufficient. Treating a dataset as an account of a person requires specifying which capacities it can recover and which information it omits. The existence of a complex network alone does not explain its behaviour in a particular environment and state.

## Testing stronger claims

We propose distinguishing three successive tasks: reconstruct anatomy; predict previously unobserved activity; and justify continuation of individual experience. Success at the first does not replace tests of the other two. Comparisons with current research must also respect dates: the mapped human tissue fragment and functional mouse-cortex resource appeared substantially after the original book.

## Requirements for a deeper analysis

The full book was not read; this entry uses the publisher description and verified edition details. Close reading should distinguish explanations of established methods, research hypotheses and speculation about distant futures. Every strong hypothesis needs a corresponding observation that could challenge it. Such separation preserves the scientific value of connectomics while preventing its findings from becoming an unsupported promise of personal survival.
