## Subject

The book's abstract identifies four connected inquiries: self-defeating moral and rational theories; attitudes towards different times in one's life; personal identity; and obligations to future generations. Presenting the book solely as a discussion of copying consciousness would therefore misrepresent its scope. Personal continuation belongs to a wider investigation of reasons for acting and caring about the future.

## Significance for the programme

Our interpretation of this structure is that a technical proposal for continuing a person needs an account of why the proposed outcome matters. Preserving habits, relationships, obligations and subjective experience may represent different goals. Even a highly accurate behavioural model cannot decide which goal should be decisive. This is an ORI analytical application, not a reconstruction of unread chapters.

## Reading limits

The publisher record and book abstract were checked; a complete text was not obtained for sequential argument analysis. This entry therefore does not reconstruct particular thought experiments, supply page references or claim that Parfit established digital immortality. The abstract explicitly leaves a difficult question concerning future population size and quality of life unresolved.

## Questions for close reading

A deeper analysis should distinguish psychological connections, continuation and numerical identity, then examine the steps linking them to practical conclusions. It should also compare personal interests with those of other people affected by a decision. For ORI, this separates a technology's desirability, technical feasibility and philosophical evaluation. A successful model prediction cannot settle all three questions.
