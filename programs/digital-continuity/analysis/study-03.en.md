## Main idea

The official MICrONS portal describes calcium-imaging recordings aligned with electron-microscopy connectivity reconstruction from the same mouse. The resource spans primary and higher visual cortical areas, connecting structural and functional properties of the same cells. This entry inspected the consortium description; the complete journal article was not retrieved through the publisher interface.

## Why it matters

Our interpretation is that two observation types are brought together, reducing uncertainties that either leaves alone. Researchers can ask which connectivity features help predict stimulus responses. For digital-continuity research, this exemplifies a bounded, measurable modelling task with specified inputs and observable outputs, rather than an undefined promise to recreate a brain.

## Limitations

One mouse's visual cortex does not represent a person's entire life. Functional recordings depend on particular conditions and signals, while anatomy requires matching cells across methods. Matching uncertainty must be considered when evaluating a model. An open portal also does not mean that we downloaded, audited or reproduced the entire dataset; none of those operations was performed here.

## ORI application

A proposed starting point is prediction on new stimuli, comparing different ways of incorporating anatomy. The unit of generalisation should be declared in advance: new cells, new stimuli or new animals. These tests address different questions and cannot substitute for each other. Even accurate activity prediction does not measure personal identity. Its scientific value lies in constraining a model with observations and exposing identifiable failures.
