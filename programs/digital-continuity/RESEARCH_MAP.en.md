# Source map: digital continuity of persons

[Русский](RESEARCH_MAP.md) · [Program README](README.en.md)

**Status:** a starter annotated map, not a systematic review. **Checked:** 24 October 2026. None of the works below demonstrates consciousness transfer or preservation of a person's identity in a computational system. A behavior model, wiring reconstruction, memory, and continuation of subjective experience are different claims.

## Three orientation books

| Book | Why it helps; access |
|---|---|
| John Locke, *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*, Book II, Chapter 27 (added in the 2nd edition, 1694), [public transcription](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10615/10615-h/10615-h.htm) | A historical source for a theory of persons tied to consciousness and memory; read “Of Identity and Diversity,” keeping in mind that seventeenth-century concepts differ from modern neuroscience. Locke added the chapter in the 1694 second edition ([SEP: Locke on Personal Identity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/locke-personal-identity/)); the open Gutenberg transcription includes it, though its title page gives 1690 and spells *Humane*. |
| Derek Parfit, *Reasons and Persons* (1984/1986), [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12484) | Classic thought experiments on psychological continuity, survival, and branching. The record, contents, and selected chapters are available; the full text may require subscription or purchase. This is a philosophical argument, not an experiment in copying a person. |
| Sebastian Seung, *Connectome: How the Brain's Wiring Makes Us Who We Are* (2012 eBook; 2013 paperback), [Penguin publisher page](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/180235/connectome-by-seung-sebastian/9780241951873) | An accessible introduction to connectomics and early hopes for brain mapping. The page is open; the book is paid or available through libraries. Read its identity and uploading claims critically: later maps cover limited tissue and species, not a complete, functioning human person. |

This models memory mechanisms; it does not reconstruct a person's memories. The retrieval-augmented-generation analogy is not evidence for digital transfer. |

## Three historical milestones

1. **1694 — memory and consciousness as criteria of personal identity.** In the second edition Locke added “Of Identity and Diversity” (Book II, Chapter 27), where he ties personal identity to the reach of consciousness ([public transcription of the chapter](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10615/10615-h/10615-h.htm); [history of the chapter, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/locke-personal-identity/)). This is a philosophical criterion, not a neuroscience theory of memory storage.
2. **1957 — the H.M. case distinguishes memory from other abilities.** Scoville and Milner described severe impairment in forming new memories after bilateral surgery in medial temporal structures ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13406589/)). Historical clinical evidence is limited to a small, heterogeneous set of cases, but it changed memory research.
3. **1984 — psychological continuity and branching become an explicit philosophical problem.** Parfit analyzes imagined division and copying cases in *Reasons and Persons* ([Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12484)). The argument shows that psychological similarity or continuation does not automatically settle who survived.

## Active researchers and teams

- **Jeff W. Lichtman and the Harvard Connectomics group** build large-scale maps of cellular structure and connectivity ([Lichtman's official profile](https://lichtmanlab.fas.harvard.edu/people/jeff-lichtman) · [lab publications](https://lichtmanlab.fas.harvard.edu/papers)). Recent work includes *SmartEM* (2025/2026), using machine learning to guide electron microscopy, and 2026 studies of neuromuscular network formation. This advances mapping methods; it is not a person-transfer project.
- **Marcel Binz and Human-Centered AI at Helmholtz Munich**, with a broad cognitive-science team, model behavior and cognition ([group profile](https://hcai-munich.com/marcel.html)). A specific recent work is Centaur and the open Psych-101 dataset (Nature, 2025), trained on over 10 million choices from 160 experiments ([paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09215-4)). The model predicts behavior across groups and tasks; it is not an individual copy or evidence of consciousness.
- **Viktor Jirsa and The Virtual Brain / Virtual Brain Twin**, Aix-Marseille University, INSERM, and CNRS, build personalized models of brain dynamics ([TVB leadership profile](https://thevirtualbrain.com/tvb/zwei/teamwork-leaders); [European project](https://www.virtualbraintwin.eu/project/team.html)). In 2026 the project and EBRAINS described using multimodal brain twins for clinical simulation and personalized planning ([EBRAINS, September 2026](https://ebrains.eu/news-and-events/2026/from-brain-maps-to-brain-twins)); this models activity and treatment, not subjective life transfer.

## Current work and open questions

By 2026, increasingly detailed maps of selected brain areas, combinations of structural and functional data, computational memory models, and clinical “virtual twins” are emerging. These areas provide new ways to constrain models with physiological data and test predictions about brain dynamics. They do not solve digitizing a person's memories, do not contain a complete functioning human brain, and do not establish first-person continuity. A commercial team's public claim about modeled animal movements should also be distinguished from a peer-reviewed neuroscience result.

Open questions include which kinds of memory and skill can be predicted from limited data; how to validate individual models in new conditions; which dynamic and molecular processes are lost in a static connectome; how behavioral accuracy relates to the contents of particular memories; and whether psychological continuity is sufficient for personal identity. The last remains a philosophical dispute unresolved by experiment.

**Suggested next reading:** compare the fixed human-tissue map (Shapson-Coe) with the mouse structure-function map (MICrONS), then read the Virtual Brain Twins review, checking exactly which signals the model takes in and what it predicts. Read Locke and Parfit separately from neuroscience claims to understand the identity question.
