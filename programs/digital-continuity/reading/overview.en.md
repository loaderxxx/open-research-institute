## 1. Three different promises

It's useful to first define the assertion object. A digital archive stores traces of a person for other people; the behavior model predicts answers to some new questions; a model of a neural system attempts to reproduce its measured responses. None of these definitions imply that the emerging object is numerically identical to the former person. The confusion begins before the technical discussion when the same verb “save” is used in all three senses. It is especially dangerous to mix these meanings in one advertising claim.

The complete contact pattern of the adult Drosophila, the local anatomy of the human cortex, and the functional map of the mouse visual cortex answer different questions. The first describes the wiring of an insect, the second a fragment of human tissue, the third links some of the recorded responses to the reconstruction. No result captures a person’s subjective biography.

The promise to “preserve a person” is sometimes quietly replaced by narrower goals: to store information about him, reproduce characteristic responses, or simulate a nerve site. These goals are useful in themselves, but the same vocabulary does not make them identical.

What outcome would be considered successful for each goal? Public data supports connectivity and prediction research. They do not yet provide an experimental criterion that one person's experience has continued in the new system.

## 2. Scale and organism

The size of published data sets tempts us to build a ladder from insects to humans. But the ladder has different steps: a complete anatomy of the small nervous system and a subtle reconstruction of a small part of the large system answer irreducible questions. In one case, you can trace distant pathways in the brain, in the other, you can see local contacts with exceptional detail. Functional recordings add a third axis of comparison, which the number of synapses does not replace. A useful comparison table should retain species and region labels.

FlyWire reconstructed 139,255 neurons and approximately 54.5 million chemical synapses in the adult fruit fly brain. The human study processed approximately a cubic millimeter of cortex, and MICrONS combined activity recording with electron microscopy of a volume of mouse visual cortex. The term “connectome” brings together achievements at different scales.

A fly is not a miniature human; the mouse visual cortex is not the entire mouse brain; and the surgically removed human tissue fragment does not capture the brain’s activity in the living body. Comparing numbers without specifying an object gives the false impression of a gradual assembly of the same individual brain.

How should maps be compared across organisms, specimens, and living states? The correct unit of assessment is a specific sample, recording method, and tissue area, not a generalized “map of consciousness.”

## 3. What electron microscopy records

When preparing the sample, the cells no longer transmit signals in the usual living mode. The researcher sees a preserved spatial structure from which possible paths of influence can be deduced, but not a specific thought or momentary state of the person whose tissue it was. This difference is similar to the difference between a road map and traffic records: the plan essentially limits the routes, but does not tell who went where on a particular day. Therefore, the map and the record of neural activity should be kept as different types of evidence.

Electron microscopy allows the tracing of membranes, processes and putative synaptic contacts in fixed tissue. Huge volumes of images require segmentation and verification, after which a graph links the identified cells. FlyWire and the human cubic millimeter show how detailed such an anatomical archive can be.

Fixation stops living dynamics; visible contact in itself does not determine the strength of transmission at a given moment, the state of receptors, the action of modulators and the entire history of plasticity. Segmentation and annotation errors also distinguish a reconstruction from a directly observed natural object.

What physiological properties should be measured simultaneously with ultrastructure? The answer depends on what function one wants to predict: a specific visual response requires some variables, while long-term personal memory may require others.

## 4. Function over structure

Structural-functional research becomes especially powerful when the same area is first observed in operation and then reconstructed. It allows you to associate the anatomical path with the actual response to the stimulus. However, the transition from the recorded visual response to the general state of the body requires separate evidence. The mouse cortex processes selected stimuli under experimental conditions, and model training on a fly is also limited by the selected task and recording method. The accuracy of the prediction depends on the distribution of the presented stimuli.

MICrONS compared neuronal activity during visual stimuli with subsequent anatomical reconstruction of the corresponding area of ​​the mouse cortex. A model of the fly visual system used the connectome as a network constraint and adjusted unknown parameters to predict recorded responses. Reading these works together reveals the power of structural-functional comparison.

The recorded responses depend on the limited set of stimuli and cells measured. Successful prediction does not mean that the model has reproduced all the internal states of the organism; multiple models can produce the same observations in the same problem. It is the need for optimization in working with a fly that shows the insufficiency of the contact list.

Will the prediction generalize to new conditions without reconfiguration? This is an open empirical question about the generalization of the model, not an answer about transferring consciousness.

Section sources: [I3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08790-w); [I4](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07939-3)

## 5. Simulation of the whole brain of a fly

The insect simulation demonstrates the practical value of an anatomical map: one can define a computational system with thousands of corresponding cells and obtain testable consequences about chains of actions. The researcher then compares these predictions with experimental interventions and behavior. Such success is rightly called a model of sensorimotor function. If it becomes a statement about the transfer of the life of an animal, the subject of the inference changes; The study did not test this change. The list of tested behaviors is more important than the word “whole” in the model name.

Shiu and co-workers constructed a model of Drosophila brain neurons using connectivity and putative transmitter identity. They studied the circuits of sensory processing, nutrition and body care, testing individual consequences of experiments. The work demonstrates that a large-scale map can be a workable tool for studying insect behavior.

The impulse integration and generation model simplifies individual cells and synapses, and behavior is assessed through selected circuits. Even agreement between several predictions and experimental results does not establish that the digital system has become that same fly or is experiencing its sensations. The connection between causal adequacy and identity is philosophically controversial here.

What set of independent tasks reveals the missing mechanisms? Testing against new interventions and conditions strengthens the model, but does not transform the organism and its computational description into a single observable event.

Section sources: [I1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07558-y); [I8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07763-9)

## 6. Memory as a distributed process

The “memory cell” metaphor is too crude for mouse experiments. Cells tagged during learning can later be activated to produce an observable response, and mapping across many regions reveals a broader ensemble associated with the same learning experience. These methods reveal causal connections, but do not read the memory like a diary page. Fear freezing is particularly informative for one laboratory association and reveals little about the nuances of autobiographical experience. Individual behavioral markers do not automatically form an autobiography.

Liu et al tagged learning-active cells in the mouse hippocampus and later optogenetically induced a freezing response. Roy and colleagues' work looked for ensembles associated with a single contextual memory across multiple regions of the mouse brain. Together, these studies move away from the idea of memory as a single file.

Freezing is a behavioral indicator in a fear experiment, not the animal's first-person account of the episode. A partial map of ensembles does not exhaust the meaning of the memory, its reinterpretation and connection with other events. The activity label depends on the cell selection conditions.

Which memory components are retained when the ensemble changes over time? The demonstrated causal relationship between cells and reaction does not prove either a complete reading of memory or the ability to transfer personal history to a computer.

Section sources: [I5](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11028); [I10](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-29384-4)

## 7. False memory and reconstruction

The laboratory creation of incorrect contextual associations is also important for a broader understanding of the archive. When a system produces a coherent story about the past, the story may reflect a reconstruction from available traces rather than an unchanging original object. Experiments with mice do not prove the equivalence of animal reactions and human conversation. They offer a narrower lesson: the relationship between trace, context, and subsequent response is mutable. A new association can change later behavior without a fictional narrative.

Ramirez et al activated cells associated with one context in mice during fear learning in another context. Then the animals froze more strongly in the first one, where there was no electric shock. This causal experiment suggests that memory retrieval may reflect a laboratory-generated association.

The behavioral outcome does not provide access to the content of the mouse’s experience in the human sense. The result also warns against the idea that memories can be considered immutable units of data addressed to a single storage location. Manipulating one association is not the same as constructing an entire biography.

How can a recovered memory be distinguished from a newly formed association? Such uncertainty is important when assessing a digital archive: a compelling narrative of the past may be a reconstruction rather than evidence that the same subject has continued.

Section sources: [I9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23888038/); [I5](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11028)

## 8. Patients and the need for the hippocampus

A clinical series from the mid-twentieth century provides a natural comparison of post-injury capabilities. Patients may have lost the ability to form new long-term records and still maintain other skills and social life. It is the heterogeneity of functions that makes such observations valuable. It is impossible to call memory one variable and assume that its digital list has exhausted the entire range of human mental abilities. Clinical observation does not determine what information a digital copy would need to contain.

Scoville and Milner described impairments in the formation of new long-term memory after bilateral surgery on the medial temporal structures in humans. Classic clinical cases have provided strong causal indications of the role of these structures. They also show that different human abilities are not affected equally by local damage.

The historical series are small, surgical volumes are variable, and inference of specific substructures is more complex than the original designation “hippocampus.” Severe memory disorder does not resolve the philosophical problem of personal identity: the patient cannot be considered to have ceased to exist on the basis of a single memory test.

How are biographical memory, abilities, and social recognition related to a person? Clinical observations clarify memory mechanisms, but do not establish a necessary and sufficient set of data for personal continuity. In the patients described, the retention of early memories and acquired skills, along with the impairment of new memorization, especially clearly shows that memory consists of distinct functions.

## 9. Behavioral model

The cognitive predictor is trained on many short experimental episodes. It can estimate the probability of an unfamiliar participant's response well because it recognizes general patterns of choice. But the model’s training is not a continuation of the nervous activity of this participant. If the model says similar words, it tests a behavioral profile in a given format, rather than a transfer of a biography as a lived sequence of events. Personal identification cannot be obtained by averaging the reactions of different participants.

Centaur was trained on Psych-101 data about human choices in psychology experiments. The authors tested the ability to predict decisions and some new experimental situations. A later independent evaluation indicated that strong prediction of individual responses may be coupled with discrepancies in generating the entire behavior of the synthetic participant.

This model learns the statistical patterns of many people, rather than recording the complete history of one person. Generalization within the limits of formalized tasks does not confirm predictions in any life situation. The critical work is a preprint; its specific tests also have their own boundaries.

When does accurately predicting behavior mean reproducing a mechanism, and when does it merely approximate it? For the idea of ​​a digital twin, both publications ask a more rigorous question about external validity, not about a subjective extension of a person.

Section sources: [I12](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09215-4); [I13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07887)

## 10. Neural digital twin

In a scientific paper, the term “digital twin” is usually tied to a specific output variable. A model of a population of visual neurons predicts how that population responds to video; a model of an epileptic network might address a different clinical question. Calling either one a digital twin of a person would expand the term’s meaning without a new experiment. Defining the scope of applicability does not detract from the accuracy of the scope itself: it allows the tool to be used without false expectations. The name should specify the visual variable that was actually measured.

A fundamental model of mouse visual cortex activity predicts the dynamic responses of recorded neurons to images and videos. In other MICrONS analyses, the term “digital twin” refers to the cortical population response predictor. Such tools are useful for searching for stimuli and comparing the functional similarity of cells.

The term “digital twin” here means a computational approximation of the measured function of a certain area. It does not promise to reproduce the senses, the body, all areas of the brain, or the history of an individual mouse. Success on the test distribution does not rule out errors under new conditions and does not answer the question about sensations.

How can we test the robustness of predictions as the context and state of the animal changes? The precise term prevents substitution: the digital twin of the visual response is not a digital personality.

Section sources: [I14](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08829-y); [I3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08790-w)

## 11. Psychological continuity

Psychological theory is interested not only in a list of information, but in the relationship between past and present states. The present intention may follow from a previous plan, and the memory may be formed through personal experience. This causal chain is different from the random similarity of questionnaire responses. But the question of whether the chain is sufficient to continue the first person becomes difficult when two equally compelling branches appear. The relevant relation must be defined before the copying scenario can be interpreted.

Locke associated personal identity with consciousness and the ability to relate past actions to oneself. Modern versions of psychological continuity speak of chains of memory, intentions, beliefs, and traits rather than one unchanging part of the body. This approach helps clearly articulate the appeal of the idea of ​​the preserved character.

Memory is fallible and can be copied; the criterion of psychological connection must explain forgetting, gradual change and duplication. The existence of a similar set of responses does not show that it was the previous experience that carried over into it. Locke's text is an argument about the conditions of identity, not an experiment.

What type of connection between states is needed if two successors are equally similar? Different versions of psychological theory interpret this case differently; There is currently no single empirically established answer. Branching reveals this difficulty more clearly than ordinary forgetting.

## 12. Biological continuation

The biological criterion makes forgetting less mysterious: a person can lose access to part of the past while remaining a living organism. At the same time, the biological criterion rejects the easy conclusion from informational similarity to identity. Critics ask what would happen with a radical replacement of nerve tissue or brain transfer. This dispute cannot be resolved by simply improving the quality of the synthesized voice, because it relates to the conditions for preserving the object over time. Memory loss and death of the body have different biological criteria.

Animalism asserts that we are human organisms and are preserved by the continued life of the animal. From this position, gradual changes in memory do not destroy the existence of a person, whereas the creation of a separate device creates a different object. The theory forces consideration of the body, metabolism and ongoing physiology.

The possibility of brain transplantation and radical prostheses remains a subject of debate between biological and psychological approaches. The philosophical thesis does not measure the experience directly and does not prejudge the meaning of digital heritage for the family. The thesis only disputes the conclusion that mental similarity is sufficient for numerical identity.

Can technological continuation preserve the organism while deeply replacing its parts? This question is different from the possibility of teaching a digital interlocutor similar words and habits.

## 13. Fork and copy

The thought experiment with two successors disciplines language. As long as the original and the separate copy exist, both can honestly claim a connection with the past, but their subsequent actions belong to different histories. Philosophers can recognize the valuable survival relation without the usual numerical identity. This is a more nuanced position than declaring one branch false solely because of its physical substrate; however, the experience of any branch does not become directly accessible to an external observer. Each branch after the split can modify the original shared archive.

Cerullo views non-destructive uploading as a thought experiment: the biological original remains, and the digital successor appears simultaneously. The proposed theory of branching psychological identity allows both successors to count as continuers. The case is useful in that the external similarity of the branches can be presented as equal without hiding their subsequent divergence.

Ordinary numerical identity is transitive: if the original is strictly the same as each of two divergent copies, there is difficulty in distinguishing them from each other. Cerullo offers a philosophical answer, not evidence of actual uploading or a measurement of consciousness.

Can a survival relationship be important without a strict numerical-identity relation? The answer determines the assessment of copying, but behavioral testing of both branches does not resolve the first-person question.

## 14. Similarity and causal history

It is convenient to judge from one conversation, but the conversation is only a small window into the causal origin of the answer. Humans are characterized by bodily habits, silent preferences and unexpressed plans that never made it into the archives. The model can successfully guess the response without reconstructing these hidden circumstances. Even if the answers coincide on many questions, there remains a difference between external regularity and the continuous history of the mechanism. A true quote from a diary does not replace the actual subject.

Two interlocutors can answer questions in the same way, although one was trained on a biographical archive, the other copied from the state of a functioning brain, and the third randomly reproduces the same words. Observing a conversation captures the similarity of the outcome, but not the same causal story. The Centaur study concerns the prediction of choices, the philosophical debate concerns the continuation of a particular life.

Even a rich record of behavior is limited to what the person said and did under the conditions provided. Silence, unexpressed intentions, bodily habits and reactions to a new event remain partially unknown. A wider archive will reduce the uncertainty of the forecast, but will not logically exclude multiple interpretations of the identity.

How to compare models that talk about the past in the same way, but were created differently? A separate account of causal provenance and separate behavioral tests are necessary to fairly evaluate each claim.

## 15. Scale error

The technical scope should be discussed relative to the task. To identify a cell type, one class of data is sufficient; to predict a reaction, a structural-functional link may be needed; and for a complete biography, even the exact definition of the necessary information is unknown. Therefore, the number of images or computational operations does not indicate the readiness for mind uploading. Achievements can be compared by the question tested, the metric used, and the object measured. Enlarging the file does not reduce the uncertainty of the philosophical criterion.

The number of synapses is staggering, but a map of an entire fruit fly brain, a millimeter-scale sample of a human, and a limited volume of mouse cortex are not sequential versions of the same object. The geometry, cell types and condition of the samples vary. The difficulty of reconstructing the human brain is determined by more than just file size.

Local detail can be scientifically more valuable than a large, coarse map for a particular question. The converse is also true: a circuit without the required region will miss the causal chain. Neither megabytes nor petabytes of images by themselves determine the completeness of the model.

What minimum spatial, temporal, and molecular detail is sufficient for a given prediction? The answer may differ depending on whether the target is identifying a cell, reconstructing a memory, or accounting for an experience; existing studies do not report a universal figure. It is especially important not to confuse the size of a digital image array with the number of independently validated functional predictions; these quantities can grow at different rates.

## 16. Ambiguity of the mechanism

The same output often allows for different hidden explanations. Two images can differ in pixels and cause the same visual response, and two models with different parameters can give the same prediction on the studied stimuli. To learn more about the mechanism, researchers look for situations where predictions diverge. But even an ideally selected functional mechanism does not in itself resolve the philosophical question of whether digital performance is the same subject. Predictive success must be verified on data beyond the training set.

A model of the fly visual system combines observed wiring with learnable parameters; a model of mouse cortical activity extracts the statistical structure of responses. Both are capable of predicting data within test limits, although they are built on different simplifications. This difference illustrates the problem of identification: the observed response does not always highlight a single internal mechanism.

To favor one explanation requires new conditions or interventions where competing models diverge in their predictions. Even such success will be limited to the list of tested conditions. The complexity of measurement should not be confused with the logical impossibility of research; the strength of the inference depends on the study design.

What evidence really differentiates transmission, plasticity, and global state rules? This is a published methodological modeling problem, not an instruction for assembling a copy of a person.

Section sources: [I4](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07939-3); [I14](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08829-y)

## 17. Individual differences

General patterns of choice are important for psychology, but predicting an individual’s behavior requires distinguishing among people. Average accuracy can hide people whose backgrounds and circumstances fall outside the sampled population. Expanding the sample may correct some errors, but rare future events will remain a challenge. Even predicting an exceptional decision does not establish that the model experiences the decision alongside the person being modeled. The distribution of errors across individuals is more informative than a single average-accuracy score.

Psych-101 aggregates millions of decisions from many participants. Centaur aims to explain general patterns and transfer between psychological tasks. Between average accuracy across people and fidelity to an individual biography lie individual differences: life experiences, language, social relationships, and situation change choices.

A person also sometimes makes mistakes in predicting himself, so a perfect similarity test is impossible by simply checking one answer. A critical study of synthetic participants found significant divergences in generation distributions even when individual predictions were strong.

How to test the stability of an individual profile under new circumstances? Convincing imitation of general cognitive regularities supports a scientific model of behavior, but not a claim about the preservation of a particular individual. A rare decision by a person can weigh more on his life than thousands of routine choices in a laboratory problem, although the average accuracy remains high.

## 18. Observer and first person

An observer has access to another person’s speech, actions, and physiological recordings, but cannot directly experience that person’s first-person perspective. In relation to familiar people, we use rich indirect signs and general biology. For a separate digital medium, the evidence would be different, and the issue of transfer adds the requirement to connect the two stages of existence. The uncertainty here is epistemic and philosophical; it does not override the verifiable properties of the models. The first-person speech remains an indirect report to the observer.

An external observer can check whether the interlocutor remembers events, recognizes people and maintains the usual style of decisions. The question of whether the original flow of experience has continued is formulated in the first person. Theories of psychological and biological identity offer different rules for its interpretation.

We do not have a generally accepted measurement procedure that simultaneously confirms the transfer of subjective perspective between two physically separate media. This absence does not prove the impossibility of any future forms of continuation. It limits the current claim: behavioral similarity does not serve as a direct measurement of first-person experience.

How to distinguish between two continuations with the same behavior and different media histories? The research description must separate the observable criteria from the philosophical inference about identity. Even a very detailed description of previous states does not in itself indicate the direction of the subjective transition.

## 19. Digital heritage

An archive of a living or deceased person can preserve their voice, documents, and ways of explaining important events. This can help loved ones if the model clearly distinguishes reliable records from newly generated guesses. Specific data and consent rights go beyond neural reconstruction. The practical usefulness of such a legacy does not require that we attribute to the archive the author's experience or promise the author's continued life. This distinction also helps avoid assigning new promises to the archive.

Voice recordings, texts and decisions create an archive or conversational model useful to posterity and historians. Psychological connection theory explains why recognition can have emotional power. Behavioral modeling experiments provide a means of assessing the limited predictive properties of such systems.

The practical value of the archive does not depend on the claim of immortality. The model may make mistakes, generate thoughts the person never recorded, or perpetuate a biased selection of the past. Human consent, the ability to indicate artificial origin and the interests of loved ones become important precisely because the similarity is convincing.

What responsibilities do living people have when using such a representation? Publications on connectomes and cognitive models do not address this normative issue; it requires independent discussion. When creating a new response, it is useful to be able to track which part was taken from the document and which part was completed by the system.

## 20. Summary of open questions

Scientific evaluation is stronger when each result remains on its own scale. The map answers the question about contacts, experimental stimulation - about the causal role of the ensemble, the predictive model - about the correspondence to certain answers. The philosophical theory then explains why some forms of continuity might count as survival. There are still unresolved transitions between these levels that cannot be closed with a beautiful single technology name. An untested level connection should be designated as an open issue.

Insect anatomy, human fragment structures, mouse cortical activity, and causal effects on mouse memory are confirmed in relevant tasks. The models also show the ability to predict individual neural or behavioral responses. These achievements expand specific sciences, each at its own level.

Counterevidence and caveats apply to transfer: structure does not define a single dynamic, memory does not equal a single behavior, and a similar cue does not establish a numerical identity. Philosophical concepts are not limited to forecast statistics. Lack of results at one level cannot be hidden by achievement at another.

What future research will narrow the gap between measurement, model, and self-description? The open status of the issue requires precise statements of what has been achieved and a refusal to call existing reconstructions personality loading. The power of a review lies in comparing strictly different results, rather than lumping them into one metric of progress.
