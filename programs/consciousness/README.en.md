# Consciousness and perception

[Русский](README.md)

**Main question:** What can be measured in consciousness research, and what remains interpretation for now?

This program compares theories of consciousness, neuroscientific findings, and proposed indicators for artificial systems. We distinguish subjective experience, access to information, the ability to report, and outward behavior. Adversarial tests of theories are considered alongside criticism of which predictions were actually tested.

## Program questions

- How do the predictions of competing theories differ, and how are they tested?
- How do reporting, attention, and a participant's task affect measurement?
- What do functional indicators for AI offer, and why are they not a ready-made test of subjective experience?

## Sources and limitations

The materials do not establish that any particular model is conscious, and do not treat functional similarity as proof of experience.

## What the sources report

### C-01
A theoretically derived indicator is a basis for evaluating a hypothesis, but not calibrated evidence of subjective experience in AI.

**Limitations:** Functional theories may assign greater ontological weight to performing the relevant role.

**Sources:** [C1: Identifying indicators of consciousness in AI systems](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41219038/)

### C-02
An adversarial test with 256 people found partial support for, and substantial problems with, specific predictions of IIT and GNWT.

**Limitations:** GNWT proponents dispute the centrality of offset ignition and note the absence of an unconscious contrast.

**Sources:** [C2: Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1); [C5: GNW theoretical framework and the adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2025/1/niaf037/8280147)
