# Butlin et al. — Identifying indicators of consciousness in AI systems

[Русская версия](study-02.md)

**Access and reading limits.** Checked the abstract, highlights and publication metadata. The article is open access, but the full text was not obtained because direct publisher and repository access was restricted. Online publication was in 2025; the journal issue is June 2026.

**Authors’ proposal.** The paper proposes deriving indicators from neuroscientific theories of consciousness and using them to assess particular AI systems. Its logic moves from a theoretical commitment to an investigable system property while retaining uncertainty about the theory itself. This is a methodological contribution, classified by the publisher as Opinion. It does not report experimental establishment of subjective experience in a particular language model or offer a universal theory-independent detector.

**ORI critical analysis.** The programme’s central question is how effectively an indicator discriminates between competing explanations. A property might be necessary under one theory, redundant under another and absent from a third. An assessment should therefore expose its dependence on assumptions rather than conceal it within a single score or completed checklist.

Several indicators may also reflect one shared architectural feature. They should not automatically be treated as independent evidence and added together as separate confirmations. Self-descriptions require particular care: training a system to reproduce human language about experience supplies an alternative explanation for a convincing report. Investigating internal mechanisms and performing interventions may constrain some alternatives, but those procedures still need a justified connection to the theory.

A practical ORI application is a table recording each indicator’s theoretical origin, measurement procedure, possible imitation and disconfirming conditions. Theoretical uncertainty should be recorded separately from measurement uncertainty: accurate architectural inspection does not remove disagreement about its interpretation. This approach can improve research procedures without a premature positive or negative diagnosis. Its value is a transparent argument, not a promise to settle artificial experience immediately. The next substantive reading should examine how the full paper handles correlated indicators and differences between implementation and surface behaviour.
