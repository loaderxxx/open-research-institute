# Anil Seth — Being You

[Русская версия](book-02.md)

**Access and reading limits.** Checked the publisher’s description of the 2021 edition. The full text and separate publisher sample were not read.

**Source position.** The publisher presents the book as an accessible account of an approach in which perception is shaped by the brain’s predictions and their correction by incoming signals. Questions about why the world and self are experienced in particular ways are connected with the brain’s biological operation. This is an author’s interpretation of a research direction rather than a neutral catalogue of all theories. The available description does not permit checking the book’s individual experiments or extended arguments.

**ORI critical analysis.** The programme should separate three levels of claim. First, expectations can affect what is perceived. Second, a particular computational model predicts the size of that effect. Third, the mechanism explains why subjective experience exists. Evidence for one level does not automatically establish the next, even when an appealing overall narrative connects them.

A testable study of predictive perception should distinguish changes in perception, decision and reporting. More frequent selection of an expected answer might reflect a decision criterion rather than altered experienced content. Independent measures, controls for task demands and conditions under which rival models diverge are therefore valuable.

Discussion of selfhood requires similar care in separating body representation, estimation of bodily state and autobiographical self-description. Their interdependence does not establish a single mechanism sufficient for every form of self-awareness. For ORI, the book can generate concrete experimental questions: which expectations change, what is measured, and what outcome would challenge the model? Comparing competing explanations allows the accessibility of popular science to be useful without turning a vivid metaphor into an established law. Assessing how consistently the book itself maintains these distinctions still requires reading the complete edition.
