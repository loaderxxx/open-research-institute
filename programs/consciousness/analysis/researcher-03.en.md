# Martin Monti and the UCLA team — consciousness and coma

[Русская версия](researcher-03.md)

**Access and reading limits.** Checked the indexed laboratory page, UCLA profile and abstract of Toker et al. 2026. The complete text of that paper was not obtained.

**Research direction.** Monti’s laboratory studies consciousness and cognition after severe brain injury, alongside relationships between language and thought. UCLA’s profile describes behavioural and neuroimaging methods; the laboratory page connects the research with improved diagnosis and investigation of possible interventions. A recent concrete example is Monti’s participation in Toker et al.’s 2026 modelling study of disorders of consciousness. This is clinically motivated neuroscience, not an investigation of consciousness in the algorithms themselves.

**ORI critical analysis.** The direction is particularly important because outward response capacity may be impaired. A negative behavioural result has several possible explanations: absence of the target state, failure to perceive instructions, motor impairment or unstable attention. Diagnostic quality therefore depends on combining independent measurements and considering the individual clinical context.

A neuroimaging response is not a literal reading of experience either. Its meaning depends on the task, control conditions and plausibility of alternative physiological explanations. Monitoring should distinguish signal detection, state classification, recovery prediction and evidence of intervention efficacy. Success at one stage does not guarantee success at the others.

A useful ORI priority is research with prespecified clinical outcomes, time points and criteria for state change, retaining uncertainty at the level of the individual patient. Model comparison is valuable when it improves testable predictions and respects the independence of samples. This direction helps clarify relationships between report, behaviour and physiology without equating those quantities. Listing the team implies no medical recommendation, access to restricted patient data or collaboration. It identifies a set of carefully bounded methods for testing consciousness hypotheses, particularly where the usual assumption that an individual can simply report an experience is unavailable.
