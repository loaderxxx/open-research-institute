# Anil Seth and the Sussex Centre for Consciousness Science

[Русская версия](researcher-01.md)

**Access and reading limits.** Checked the centre membership and Seth’s role, the 2025 comment’s bibliographic record and accessible sections of the October 2026 essay. These comments are not represented as experimental studies.

**Research direction.** Sussex’s page confirms Seth as director of a centre spanning computational, psychological, philosophical and clinical directions. The programme map associates his work with perception and selfhood. Two recent examples have a different genre: a 2025 comment about scientific debate and a jointly authored 2026 essay taking a sceptical position on AI consciousness. The latter stresses the distinction between intelligent behaviour and experience; it is a position argument rather than a new experiment.

**ORI critical analysis.** Following this researcher is useful at two levels, but those levels should be evaluated separately. An experimental model needs discriminating predictions, whereas a public position requires examination of its premises and treatment of alternatives. Expertise in perception does not by itself make a conclusion about artificial systems uncontroversial.

The programme should particularly ask which biological properties are proposed as causally necessary. A substrate-based argument needs to specify the relevant characteristic and explain why a functional analogue would be insufficient. A behavioural argument should examine whether absence of a familiar signal is being mistaken for absence of the state under investigation. Both approaches need precise commitments rather than intuitive comparisons between humans and machines.

Practical monitoring of the centre can prioritise publications that independently measure perception, report and bodily state and compare them with explicit models. Position essays can supply contested assumptions for examination but do not replace that examination. ORI includes this direction for comparative analysis of arguments and methods, not as a declaration of collaboration or acceptance of a final answer about AI consciousness. The useful question is which new evidence would change the argument, rather than whether a prominent author has expressed it confidently.
