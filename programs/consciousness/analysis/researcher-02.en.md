# Patrick Butlin, Tim Bayne and colleagues — consciousness indicators

[Русская версия](researcher-02.md)

**Access and reading limits.** Checked Butlin’s Eleos profile, authors and affiliations in PubMed, and the indicator paper’s abstract. The short response on mimicry has a bibliographic record without an abstract; its detailed arguments are not summarised.

**Research direction.** Butlin, Bayne, Fleming, Mudrik and colleagues develop theory-grounded approaches to assessing consciousness in artificial systems. Eleos’s profile connects Butlin’s work with consciousness, agency and moral status. PubMed confirms the interdisciplinary and multi-institutional authorship of the short 2026 response concerning mimicry and internal system variants. The main example is the paper proposing that investigable indicators be derived from consciousness theories. This is a methodological direction, not a positive diagnosis of a particular model.

**ORI critical analysis.** The direction is worth tracking through the quality of transitions from general theory to a concrete testing procedure. A useful indicator needs a clear theoretical origin, measurable content and conditions under which a result would weaken the initial hypothesis. If every outcome can be reconciled with it, the indicator offers little discrimination between explanations.

It is particularly useful to ask whether a procedure can be repeated on internally different systems with similar behaviour. Such a design could reveal which conclusions genuinely depend on architecture and which arise from interpreting text or a chosen interface. This is ORI’s own research question, not a reconstruction of the inaccessible short response. Similarly, lack of access to internal states should remain a testing limitation rather than become a confident assertion about their properties.

Monitoring should distinguish three products: a theoretical indicator, a technical test and a normative decision. The last may consider the consequences of mistakes, but it does not add empirical evidence to the first two. Comparing these layers makes uncertainty more transparent. The team is relevant as a source of developing methodology; inclusion implies neither institutional partnership, complete agreement among all coauthors nor ORI’s adoption of a final assessment of machine consciousness.
