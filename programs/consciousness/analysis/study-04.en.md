# Bariach et al. — Seemingly conscious AI risks

[Русская версия](study-04.md)

**Access and reading limits.** Inspected the abstract, introduction and definition of the target phenomenon in the open article. Survey data were not independently reanalysed.

**Research target.** The paper combines a narrative review with an expert survey about risks from systems that appear conscious to people. It groups cues into affective capacity, anthropomorphic features, autonomous action, self-reflection and social interaction. Proposed consequences include individual dependence and wider social conflict. The target is human attribution of consciousness; subjective experience in the system itself is not required by the definition of the phenomenon.

**ORI critical analysis.** This usefully separates an ontological question from measurable social effects. Researchers can study how an interface changes trust or attachment without resolving whether a machine has inner experience. Expert judgment about a possible risk, however, is not an observed incidence of harm, and a list of scenarios does not establish the causal sequence producing them.

A narrative review can organise scattered ideas, but its coverage depends on literature selection. Detailed reading should examine the search process, inclusion criteria and composition of the expert group. It matters whether the study distinguishes plausibility judgments, severity of possible consequences and confidence in a proposed mechanism. Combining those scales can make the resulting prioritisation difficult to interpret.

A practical ORI follow-up would compare interface features under controlled conditions with separate outcomes: accuracy of users’ beliefs, forms of trust and interaction decisions. Duration of use and prior attitudes should be considered rather than relying only on an immediate rating of human likeness. Such research would test the proposed risks empirically. The paper is useful as a map of hypotheses and concepts; it does not establish that every emotionally expressive AI causes the listed harms or possesses the state that a user attributes to it. Keeping these claims distinct is necessary for both scientific assessment and sensible design decisions.
