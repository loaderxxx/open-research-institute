## Scope

The programme concerns the structure of state institutions, the implementation of public decisions and the relationships between citizens, public bodies and levels of government. It covers service access and quality, the distribution of authority and resources, participation in decisions and oversight of implementation. Comparisons will distinguish institutions' observed outcomes from their normative grounds, including rights, fairness and legitimacy.

## Priority questions

1. **Institutions and public services.** How are authority, resources and work organisation related to service access, quality and continuity? Which measures reflect recipients' experiences, including those facing barriers to access?
2. **Participation and accountability.** When does citizen participation influence decisions? How do feedback, appeals, audits and public explanations of actions work? What conditions help identify errors and correct implementation?
3. **Levels of government and coordination.** How are responsibilities distributed among local, regional and national institutions? How are authority, funding and accountability aligned? What happens to tasks that span several territories?

## Next stage: collecting information

1. Map the existing materials on participation, accountability, polycentricity and public justification of decisions; identify questions they do not cover.
2. Clarify concepts and a framework for comparing institutions. Select types of public services and levels of government for the first review.
3. Find scholarly reviews, primary studies, official data and programme evaluations. Record search and selection conditions, distinguishing descriptions of rules, observational studies and causal evaluations.
4. Compare institutional arrangements, context, measures and study limitations. Use this comparison to prepare a review and a list of questions for further investigation.
