## What the resource contains

The 2026 WGI release supplies six composite governance dimensions for 1996–2025. It combines perceptions from 35 sources, including surveys and expert assessments. An unobserved-components model produces estimates with uncertainty; a 0–100 scale is also available. Historical values were recalculated for consistency. This is a statistical comparison resource, not an experiment or definitive ranking of state systems.

## ORI critical analysis

The first risk is substituting perceptions for the phenomenon under investigation. Citizen and expert assessments matter, but can respond slowly or change for reasons different from service performance. A second risk is confusing a broad national signal with a particular organisation. A low governance score does not locate the obstruction within an implementation chain.

Comparisons over time should use one data release and record its version. Otherwise a change may reflect recalculation rather than an event within the country. Small differences require interpretation alongside uncertainty intervals. Combining sources does not eliminate potentially shared perceptual errors; agreement among several assessments is not necessarily independent confirmation.

## Programme relevance

ORI can use WGI to describe context and choose comparative cases while preserving its separate dimensions. Service-access questions need evidence about refusals, delays, appeals and the experiences of excluded groups. Causal evaluation of a reform requires its own counterfactual comparison. The next step is to inspect the underlying questions for a selected country, their coverage and changes in the source mix. The availability of reproducibility materials should not be mistaken for calculations already verified in this entry.

**Access and reading limits.** The official release page and documentation on aggregation and use were inspected. Data files and the reproducibility package were not downloaded; calculations were not reproduced.
