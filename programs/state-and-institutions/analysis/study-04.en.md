## Content and type of evidence

The report brings together literature on local development and participation. Its official page connects community programmes with decentralisation; the authors’ account stresses differences between organic initiatives and large externally induced interventions. The civil-society-failure framework questions whether representative collective voices emerge automatically. This is a research synthesis, not one experiment or a general verdict on democracy’s value.

## ORI critical analysis

The normative demand to give citizens a voice should be separated from the empirical performance of a particular mechanism. A formally open meeting may retain substantial barriers: time costs, unequal information or no influence over the final decision. Attendance is therefore insufficient evidence of participation, and participation alone is insufficient evidence of implementation oversight.

Comparisons should describe who sets the agenda, selects representatives and controls the budget. What follows expression is particularly important: does someone respond, does the decision change, and is appeal possible? Without these links, the same participation label combines very different interventions. Scaling a local success requires examining whether trust relationships and opportunities to observe implementation survive.

## Programme relevance

ORI can use the report to organise conditions of participation: power distribution, inequality, the state’s role, monitoring and observation horizon. Comparison with Olken’s experiment must preserve the difference between synthesis and an individual causal evaluation. The next step is to obtain the full open text and examine study selection, heterogeneous outcomes and evaluation gaps. Until then, this entry does not claim comprehensive familiarity with the report or attribute a single quantitative participation effect to it. Its contribution here is a disciplined set of institutional questions.
