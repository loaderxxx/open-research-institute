## ORI critical analysis

This direction helps explain digital government as a set of connected administrative processes. A user-facing form may function while an internal financial or staffing process remains incompatible. Evaluation should therefore trace a decision across agencies rather than stop at the visible interface.

A diagnostic inventory of capabilities is not a causal evaluation. A new system may coincide with personnel reform, increased funding or changed service rules. Establishing technology’s contribution requires measuring accompanying changes and choosing an appropriate comparison. Institutional experience can inform research questions without making every project a demonstrated success.

## Why follow this work

ORI should follow materials on interoperability, data quality and allocation of responsibility. Accounts of failed implementation and maintenance costs are especially valuable because they distinguish launch from sustained operation. Further work should compare technical maturity with recipients’ experiences and opportunities to correct administrative errors. The institutional profile implies no collaboration with ORI and cannot replace assessment of a particular study’s methods. Following this field means seeking testable evidence, including outcomes that fail to support initial expectations about digitalisation.
