## Content and form of argument

The book offers a historical explanation of development differences through political and economic institutions. Its publisher contrasts institutional explanations with geography or culture treated as destiny and presents divided Korea as an illustration. The programme map describes the inclusive–extractive framework. The available description confirms the broad thesis without permitting independent verification of historical evidence or every causal step.

## ORI critical analysis

The framework usefully asks who can make decisions and appropriate their benefits. Its broad categories nevertheless require observable criteria. If institutions are labelled inclusive because a country became wealthy, and wealth is then explained by inclusive institutions, reasoning becomes circular. Classification should use rules of access and constraints on power measured independently of the outcome.

Historical comparisons can generate explanations, but a political change followed by growth is insufficient for evaluating a specific reform. Security, trade, education and international support may change simultaneously. Analysis must establish sequence and mechanism while considering cases that do not fit the expected outcome. Testing alternatives does not invalidate institutional analysis; it gives it more content.

## Programme relevance

ORI should translate the general question of power into a narrow service question: who obtains access, can challenge refusal and oversee the provider? National income growth cannot replace assessment of service availability or minority rights. Full reading is necessary to examine particular historical arguments, and primary studies are needed to evaluate individual mechanisms. This entry retains the book as a competing framework without presenting its publisher’s description as a completed test of its theory.

**Access and reading limits.** The official publisher record and description were inspected, confirming the 2012 and 2013 editions. The full text and historical chapters were not read.
