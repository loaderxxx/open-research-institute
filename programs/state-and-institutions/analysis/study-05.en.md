## Content and type of evidence

The report treats standards as conditions for compatibility, trust and coordination in economic activity and public services. Its official main messages relate requirements to institutional implementation capacity. It proposes adapting standards to local conditions, aligning with international norms as capabilities develop, and participating in priority rule-making. This is a global analytical report with recommendations, not a single causal evaluation of standardisation.

## ORI critical analysis

A standard can facilitate exchange without guaranteeing compliance. A service-quality requirement needs measurement, an inspecting body and a correction procedure. Without these, formal harmonisation may increase reporting without improving outcomes. The appropriate analytical unit is therefore a particular standard together with its implementation mechanism, not a country’s formal adoption of a rule set.

The distribution of compliance costs also matters. An identical requirement may be affordable for a large provider while excluding a smaller organisation despite a useful service. Compatibility benefits should be compared with verification expenditure, entry barriers and alternative ways to achieve the objective. These are ORI’s analytical questions, not evaluations established here for every case in the report.

## Programme relevance

For public services, coordination of standards across government levels is especially relevant. Who funds compliance, updates the rule and answers for incompatible systems? Digitalisation additionally requires clear data-access rights and ways to correct records. Further reading should select one domain, reconstruct its supporting studies and distinguish empirical results from normative choices of requirements. The proposition that more standards necessarily produce better government does not follow from the report’s synthetic form and cannot replace this assessment.
