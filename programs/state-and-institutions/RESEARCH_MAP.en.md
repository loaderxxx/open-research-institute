## Books

- Elinor Ostrom, [*Governing the Commons*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/governing-the-commons/7AB7AE11BADA84409C34815CC288CD79), 1990, Cambridge UP. Compares self-governance of common resources with state and private solutions. Useful for analysing local institutions and conditions for success; cases do not prove self-governance works universally. Record and contents are public; book is paid or library access.
- Daron Acemoglu & James A. Robinson, [*Why Nations Fail*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/205014/why-nations-fail-by-daron-acemoglu-and-james-a-robinson/?facet=app&mode=light), 2012, Crown. A popular historical account of inclusive and extractive institutions. Useful as a framework for discussing power and development, though the broad historical thesis is contested and does not replace causal identification. The official record confirms the 2012 hardcover and 2013 paperback reissue; the book is paid.
- Francis Fukuyama, [*Political Order and Political Decay*](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9781429944328/politicalorderandpoliticaldecay/), 2014, Farrar, Straus and Giroux. A comparative history of state institutions, rule of law and accountability; useful context, but a synthesis is not a causal evaluation of a particular reform. Official publisher record is public; book is paid or library access.

## Research and data

- World Bank, [Worldwide Governance Indicators, 2026 update](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/worldwide-governance-indicators), 2026 (annual series for 1996–2025; methodology uses perception sources). Six composite dimensions draw on dozens of survey and expert sources. Purpose: cross-country comparison. Limits: perceptions, aggregation and uncertainty intervals mean small movements should not be treated as precise performance rankings. Data and methods are public; the World Bank cautions that scores are estimates, not definitive ratings.
- World Bank, [GovTech Maturity Index 2025](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/govtech/gtmi-2025-update), 2025. Forty-eight indicators describe government systems, online services, digital participation and institutional enablers across 197 economies; the release uses self-reports from 158 economies and public information for the rest. It is a digital-government maturity diagnostic, not a measure of all service quality or a causal estimate. Data and dashboard are public.
- Olken, [“Monitoring Corruption: Evidence from a Field Experiment in Indonesia”](https://doi.org/10.1086/517935), 2007, *Journal of Political Economy*. A field experiment on rural road projects found that increasing audit probability reduced missing expenditures; public meetings had a smaller effect. Limits: one country, programme and period; transfer to other institutions is not automatic. Abstract is public; full text may require a subscription.
- Mansuri & Rao, [*Localizing Development: Does Participation Work?*](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/handle/10986/11859), 2013, World Bank. A synthesis of participation programmes finds outcomes depend on context, inequality, elite capture and monitoring. It is a review, not one experiment or an evaluation of every form of democracy. Open World Bank book and record.
- World Bank, [*World Development Report 2025: Standards for Development*](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/wdr2025), 2025. Synthesises how standards and organisational capacity to apply rules can support development, distinguishing measurement standards from process-quality standards. Limit: global policy synthesis, not one causal estimate. Full report and materials are public.

## Historical landmarks

- **17th–18th centuries — formation of the modern state.** Fukuyama's historical synthesis relates taxation, bureaucracy and law to state capacity; this is a comparative interpretation, not the only route to modernisation ([publisher record](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9781429944328/politicalorderandpoliticaldecay/)).
- **1922 — legal-rational bureaucracy becomes an object of analysis.** Max Weber's posthumously published [*Economy and Society* (UC Press 2013 edition)](https://www.ucpress.edu/book/9780520280021/economy-and-society) systematised types of legitimate authority and bureaucratic organisation. These are sociological ideal types, not normative endorsement of bureaucracy.
- **1990 — comparative turn to common-resource institutions.** Ostrom documented diverse local rules and collective mechanisms ([Cambridge UP](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/governing-the-commons/7AB7AE11BADA84409C34815CC288CD79)); evidence applies to the cases studied.

- **Cem Dener — World Bank, Lead Governance Specialist / GovTech Global Solutions Group.** Works on digital government. A related institutional resource is the 2025 GTMI update for 197 economies; personal coauthorship of that release was not established in this check. [Institutional profile](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/team/c/cem-dener) · [GTMI release](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/govtech/gtmi-2025-update).
- **Aart Kraay — World Bank, Chief Economist, Prosperity Vertical.** Works on governance and institutions; his work with Daniel Kaufmann on the 2024 WGI methodology account is confirmed. The 2026 WGI update is attributed to the World Bank; personal authorship of that update was not separately established. [World Bank profile](https://www.worldbank.org/en/about/people/a/aart-kraay) · [WGI data and methods](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/worldwide-governance-indicators).
- **Carmela Barbera (University of Bergamo), Laurence Ferry (Durham University) and Noel Hyndman (Queen’s University Belfast, emeritus) — public-accountability researchers.** Together with Mariannunziata Liguori, Sean McCandless and Henry C. Midgley, they coauthored the introductory article [“Governance and Accountability in Reshaping Public Services: Open Challenges and Wicked Problems of the 21st Century”](https://doi.org/10.1111/padm.70012), *Public Administration* 103(3), 677–692 (2025), first published 9 August 2025. It connects accountability with legitimacy, power, citizen engagement and new service-delivery models; it is a conceptual introduction to a 14-paper special issue, not an empirical evaluation. [Barbera profile](https://unibg.unifind.cineca.it/resource/person/53029?language=en-US) · [Ferry profile](https://dur.ac.uk/business/our-people/laurence-ferry/) · [Hyndman profile](https://www.qub.ac.uk/schools/queens-business-school/people/academic-staff/AllAcademicStaffProfiles/Hyndman.html).

## Current directions, gaps and next reading

In 2025–2026, visible directions include refreshed global governance indices, digital services and institutional capacity to apply standards. Indices help with diagnosis, but self-reporting, perception measures and aggregation do not provide causal estimates of service quality. Open questions include who faces digital exclusion, whether digital channels change practical accountability, how authority, resources and responsibility can be aligned, and how to compare administrative outcomes with rights and equality criteria.

**Next reading:** start with WGI/GTMI methods, then compare Olken's field experiment with the Mansuri–Rao synthesis. For a next review, select one service and one pair of government levels, document the search, and do not use a composite score as a substitute for outcomes experienced by citizens.
