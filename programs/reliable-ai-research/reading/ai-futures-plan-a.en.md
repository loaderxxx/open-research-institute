# AI 2040: can we negotiate a safer future?

[Русский](ai-futures-plan-a.md) · [Program](../README.en.md)

**Reviewed 12 November 2026. Original ORI overview, not a translation of the source site. Policy scenario distinguished from empirical findings. No external peer review or experimental replication.**

## 1. A future designed, not scheduled

Imagine not another chatbot but a world in which governments negotiate what intelligence may be built. AI 2040: Plan A explores that question through a desirable scenario: international restraint, research transparency and time for safety work. Its title does not establish that superintelligence will arrive in 2040. [S01](https://ai-2040.com/)

For ORI, the useful question is not whether the entire story will happen, but which conditions its recommendations require. A persuasive narrative can turn several disputed assumptions into an impression of inevitability. We therefore examine the load-bearing arguments rather than merely retelling the plot.

## 2. Who is speaking, and in what capacity

AI Futures Project prepared the project with Lightcone Infrastructure. Named authors are Thomas Larsen, Romeo Dean, Brendan Halstead, Eli Lifland, Ryan Greenblatt and Daniel Kokotajlo. The authorship page describes responsibilities and AI assistance; this is the team's account, not an independent audit. It follows AI 2027 and AI Futures Model. [S02](https://ai-2040.com/about)

Read it as a research-informed policy proposal, not a company product, an enacted international agreement or an established scientific result. Contributors' credentials do not remove the need to evaluate each argument. Acknowledging a reviewer does not establish that they endorse every conclusion.

## 3. Where assumptions carry the scenario

The authors disclose their assumptions separately. US government cooperation is intentionally optimistic; timing, development speed and verification feasibility remain uncertain. Policy success therefore depends on more than technical progress. [S03](https://ai-2040.com/supplements/plan-a-assumptions)

Our analytical observation: examining assumptions individually is insufficient. A political conflict could simultaneously weaken auditor access, reporting quality and willingness to stop an unsafe experiment. ORI could build a dependency matrix: which assumption failed, which conclusions no longer follow, and which observations might have warned us. This is a proposed analysis, not a completed experiment.

## 4. Verifying computation does not prove safety

Verification Plan distinguishes oversight of known computing resources from the problem of hidden capacity. It explicitly acknowledges that inexpensive reliable verification is immature. Future coverage and success levels are conditional estimates, not measurements of today's global infrastructure. [S04](https://ai-2040.com/supplements/verification-plan)

There is a logical gap to avoid. Even a convincing audit of which computation ran on hardware does not prove that a model will avoid deception, misuse of tools or failures in a new environment. Compute accounting, behavioral control and safety justification address different layers. Substituting one for another produces an attractive but incomplete safety case.

## 5. Transparency: visible to whom, at what cost

Transparency Plan compares research disclosure regimes. The authors' preference for broad research transparency does not mean unrestricted release of model weights or sensitive data. They discuss tensions between external scrutiny and information security. [S05](https://ai-2040.com/supplements/transparency-plan)

Our question for any regime is who can inspect evidence, challenge a decision and obtain a correction. An open report has limited value if critics cannot reproduce its checks. Full access is not an unconditional benefit either: it may violate rights or spread dangerous capabilities. ORI should connect every transparency requirement to a concrete verification task instead of choosing between slogans of total openness and total secrecy.

## 6. Alternatives and the unresolved trust problem

Comparing Possible Plans contrasts international restraint, domestic regulation, voluntary caution by a leading project and racing. Numerical assessments are author judgments, not outcome frequencies from repeated trials. They must not be presented as scientific consensus probabilities. [S06](https://ai-2040.com/supplements/comparing-possible-plans)

Alignment Roadmap is explicitly unfinished research notes and primarily concerns Plan C. It is not a certificate that Plan A's technical problems have been solved. Handing research to more capable AI itself requires a justification of trust. [S07](https://ai-2040.com/supplements/alignment-roadmap)

Our conclusion: comparison is valuable when it makes the costs of a choice explicit. Restraint can buy time without ensuring that time is used well. Competition can widen access while rewarding premature release. A useful research program should state the conditions under which its preferred option ought to be reconsidered.

## 7. What this adds to ORI

The ORI connection is safety and verifiability, not digital immortality or personal continuity. The [international safe AI landscape](safe-ai-landscape.en.md) compares technical approaches; [LawZero architecture](lawzero-architecture.en.md) addresses system design; [assurance analysis](lawzero-assurance.en.md) separates conditional proof from real protection; [evidence and citations](evidence-and-citations.en.md) distinguishes references from evidence. AI 2040 adds an institutional scale. These perspectives complement each other without automatically validating one another.

We propose three testable follow-ups. First: a claim register distinguishing observations, forecasts and recommendations, with review dates and possible falsifiers. Second: a tabletop audit-failure analysis in which an independent team identifies overlooked dependencies, without developing protection-bypass methods. Third: compare open and restricted access on a safe synthetic research task, measuring error detection, reproducibility and disclosure risks. These experiments have not been conducted; no results are claimed.

The site's value is not a promise of the correct 2040. It turns general AI concern into proposals that can be scrutinized and challenged. ORI's next step is not to adopt the entire scenario but to formulate testable questions about its most fragile premises.
