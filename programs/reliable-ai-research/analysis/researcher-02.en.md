# Yiyou Sun and Dawn Song’s group — long-horizon agent evaluation

[Русская версия](researcher-02.md)

**Access and reading limits.** Checked the author profile and abstract of Agents’ Last Exam v3 dated 9 October 2026. The living benchmark’s size differs between the profile and preprint; their counts are not merged.

**Research direction.** Yiyou Sun’s profile confirms work in Dawn Song’s Berkeley group and interests in learning new skills and evaluating agents on long, out-of-distribution tasks. A concrete 2026 example is Agents’ Last Exam: an expanding collection of professional workflows with verifiable outcomes and expert participation. It studies delivery of practical work, not scientific truth itself; its findings should not automatically be transferred to research conclusions.

**ORI critical analysis.** Long tasks create a particular problem of accumulated errors. An agent can complete most operations successfully yet leave an unusable final artefact. Full success, partial completion and required human repair should therefore be distinguished. An aggregate percentage without that decomposition says little about where a system actually helps.

A living benchmark introduces another methodological difficulty. Expansion can improve relevance while making comparisons over time harder. A ranking change may reflect a new task sample or revised criteria rather than a change in the agent. ORI should prioritise fixed versions, reevaluation of older configurations and explicit accounts of composition changes.

Monitoring should focus on tasks where final outcomes are checked independently of an eloquent report. Transfer to science additionally requires attention to evidence provenance, method validity and the possibility of an informative negative result. Professional usefulness and scientific justification can overlap, but neither replaces the other. The group offers methods for measuring sustained work and recovery from mistakes. Inclusion does not imply partnership, private-data access or acceptance of any leaderboard score as a universal measure of research competence. The useful comparison keeps task, version, tools and verification criteria visible together rather than reducing them to the model’s name.
