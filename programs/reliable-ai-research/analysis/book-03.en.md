# The Marquand House Collective — Auditing AI

**Access and reading limits.** MIT Press and Harvard confirm an open edition. The description and contents were checked, but MIT Direct returned an access restriction; the main text has not yet been read.

**Source content.** The publisher presents the book as guidance for designing audits of automated decisions, interpreting findings and changing practice afterwards. The contents move from defining an audit through its steps to follow-up action and conditions for a healthy auditing ecosystem. The collective brings computational, social and legal perspectives together. This is an applied and normative framework, not proof that every possible audit works or a universal certification procedure.

**ORI critical analysis.** Auditing research AI begins with a specific claim about the system. A promise of accurate citations requires checking whether a source supports the associated statement. A promise of reproducible analysis requires checking data, execution and environment. A general quality score replaces neither procedure. The audit target can be the whole workflow around model outputs, not only the model itself.

It is important to distinguish a problem not being detected from a problem being demonstrated absent. Query sampling, log access and construction of control cases determine what an audit can observe. A system adapted to known tests may score well without improving in real research. Independent assessment and an explicit account of access limitations are therefore necessary.

A practical ORI implication is to connect each discrepancy with a reproducible example, possible explanation and testable correction. The normative choice of an acceptable error rate should be explained separately from its observed frequency. System changes require retesting the affected behaviour and examining possible side effects. The book offers an orientation connecting measurement with accountable action; detailed assessment of its examples and recommendations remains a task for subsequent reading of the open text.
