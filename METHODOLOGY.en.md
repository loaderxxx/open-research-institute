# Research Methodology

[Русский](METHODOLOGY.md)

Open Research Institute develops programmes by defining questions, studying published work and comparing explanations. Our current output is a [library of reviews](LIBRARY.en.md). We have not conducted original experiments; new programmes are at the agenda-setting stage. This document describes public working principles and requirements for the next stage, rather than reporting completed investigations.

## 1. Questions and scope

Work starts with a specific question: what needs explaining, comparing or testing? We identify the subject, scale, key concepts and conditions under which an answer is possible. A broad programme theme is not itself a testable hypothesis.

The question determines an appropriate output: a review, model comparison, analysis of open data or future experiment. We specify which neighbouring questions fall outside its scope. Factual description, causal explanation, philosophical argument and value judgment remain distinct. Connecting disciplines can help formulate a question, but does not itself establish a theory.

## 2. Finding primary sources

We prioritise original studies, methods, datasets and responses within scientific debates. Reviews help locate primary sources. For new reviews, we plan to record the search question, resources, date and selection rationale, making the boundaries of the collection visible.

We provide bibliographic details, links and reading depth: full text, abstract or excerpt. Preprints are distinguished from peer-reviewed publications. We check available information on corrections and retractions, although a limited search cannot guarantee finding every change. The existing library contains focused reviews, not an exhaustive systematic search.

## 3. Evidence and alternatives

For a significant conclusion, we relate the authors' claim to their methods, data and research conditions. We consider sample size and composition, measurement, comparisons, uncertainty and limits on generalisation. Correlation differs from causation; success on one test differs from a system's general capability.

We retain strong objections and competing explanations. When studies disagree, we examine differences in questions, measurement or analysis rather than count supporting publications. A conclusion may remain unresolved. Describing another team's result does not mean that our initiative independently reproduced it.

## 4. Data and reproducibility

Future data analyses should document dataset provenance, version, usage terms, observation selection and missing-data handling. We plan to preserve transformations, code, dependencies, parameters and random seeds where relevant. Published materials should support repeating computations and understanding consequential decisions.

Repeating calculations on the same data and independent testing on new data address different questions. Access restrictions or barriers to reproduction should be explicit. An openly accessible dataset retains its usage conditions; text provenance and rights are explained [separately](RIGHTS.en.md).

## 5. Protocols for future studies

Before a new study starts, its question, hypotheses, data, methods, comparisons, measures, inclusion and exclusion criteria, analysis plan and stopping conditions should be agreed. An experiment also requires suitable expertise, resources and any approvals relevant to that particular work. A completed protocol should precede results.

The plan should explain which observations would support an explanation, challenge it or leave the question unresolved. Changes should be dated and explained; exploration after inspecting data should be distinguished from planned tests. Negative or inconclusive results also deserve reporting. Until these projects begin, their pages describe research agendas rather than completed studies.

## 6. Review and corrections

Published materials were prepared with AI assistance for searching, extraction, comparison and editing, followed by internal editorial review. They have not undergone external scientific peer review. AI assistance does not replace checking claims against sources; the [editorial note](EDITORIAL_NOTE.en.md) explains the current corpus and its limitations.

Corrections can be proposed through [Issues](https://github.com/loaderxxx/open-research-institute/issues): identify the file and section, disputed claim, primary source and suggested change. Repository history records revisions. Do not include personal data or confidential material in public discussions. A revised conclusion should explain which new evidence or identified error changed its meaning.
