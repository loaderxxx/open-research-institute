# The clinical microbiome: what changes a decision?

**Open Research Institute · Clinical evidence review · 18 November 2026**

For a gastroenterologist, a microbial measurement is most interesting when it changes what happens to a patient. This review asks where that standard has been met in digestive and liver disease, which results merit follow-up, and what the available Gulf cohorts can tell a clinic in Dubai. It draws on published human research, including papers released in November 2026.

## A two-minute briefing

| Clinical question | Finding | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Can a microbiota-based intervention prevent recurrent *C. difficile* infection? | After antibiotics for recurrent infection, eight-week recurrence in the pivotal oral product trial was **12.4% vs 39.8%** with placebo. [US prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=99e5a37a-930c-4641-bcdd-7013ec1c15fe&type=display) | A defined product, population and clinical outcome support this indication. US regulatory status is specific to its jurisdiction. |
| Does that success transfer to IBS? | In the 2026 phase 3 REFIT2 trial, **40% vs 38%** met the 90-day symptom-response endpoint with donor material versus autologous placebo. [REFIT2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42785338/) | The trial did not demonstrate superior symptom relief. A substantial response in both groups makes uncontrolled comparisons misleading. |
| Does a microbial signature improve colorectal screening? | Among 1,034 FIT-positive Norwegian participants, adding microbial data to FIT, age, sex and a lifestyle measure increased held-out discrimination for neoplastic findings from **0.61 to 0.64**. For invasive cancer specifically, FIT alone performed better in the authors’ training analysis. [Study, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13507204/) | Identify the lesion and intended decision first. This study does not justify withholding colonoscopy. |
| Could stool profiling improve liver fibrosis referrals? | In a 96-person cohort, AUROC was **0.85** for clinical features, **0.87** with microbial data added and **0.93** for transient elastography. [Direct comparison](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-66241-0) | A promising question for Dubai is whether a fixed stool score finds additional advanced fibrosis after routine assessment, at an acceptable cost in false referrals. |
| Can a wellness test reliably classify an individual? | Seven providers assessed the same standardised donor material and returned materially different profiles. Local cohorts also show associations with age, adiposity and diet. [Analytical comparison](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-09301-3) · [Emirati and Lebanese cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41713465/) | Before interpreting an individual score, establish that it is reproducible and calibrated for the patient group in which it will be used. |

**A question for Dr. Kayasseh.** Of the decisions you face in clinic, would it be more valuable to identify advanced liver fibrosis earlier, or to improve endoscopy referral for patients with suspected IBD? The [fibrosis proposal](scientific_synthesis.md) and the [IBD referral question](03-uae-and-clinical-priorities.md) specify the usual-care comparator, the patients most likely to be missed, and the evidence that could justify further work.
