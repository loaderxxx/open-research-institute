# Five further questions from the UAE Microbiome Project agenda

[← Review contents](README.md)


The [ESCM conference slides](https://escm.ae/3rd-conference/presentation/05%20Nov/02.30-02.50%20pm%20-%20Azzam.pdf) identify additional areas in which a microbiome signature might be informative. The proposed use determines the evidence required. Predicting a diagnosis, explaining a mechanism and changing a patient's outcome are three different research questions.

## Antimicrobial resistance: does a microbiome intervention clear carriage?

Narang and colleagues randomized 114 patients with persistent intestinal carriage of multidrug-resistant organisms and gastrointestinal disease in an Indian tertiary centre to faecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) or sham. Diversity changed, yet the trial found no significant between-group improvement in decolonization. A 2026 synthesis of four randomized trials, including this one, estimated a short-term decolonization risk ratio of **1.21 (95% CI 0.79–1.85)** in 202 participants, assessed at approximately four to seven weeks. Clinically important benefit and harm remain compatible with this degree of uncertainty. Clearance at one visit, a later infection and a patient's need for antibiotics should be counted separately. [Randomized trial](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2026.0655) · [Randomized evidence synthesis](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13756-026-01823-7)

## Type 2 diabetes: what does a stool signature add to existing measurements?

A 10-cohort analysis examined **8,117 metagenomes** from the US, Europe, Israel and China. Metformin was an important confounder. In comparisons between metformin-naïve patients and normoglycaemic controls, adding microbial species to age, sex and BMI raised average AUROC from **0.63 to 0.69**. The baseline model omitted the blood measurements used for diabetes diagnosis. This comparison therefore cannot establish additional diagnostic value over an HbA1c or glucose-based clinical pathway. A 2026 analysis of 433 European and Asian samples also detected cohort-level differences but leaves the Gulf transfer question open. A stronger question is whether a baseline profile improves *prospective* prediction in an untreated cohort after accounting for standard metabolic measurements. [International analysis](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03067-7) · [2026 analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41927536/)

## Pancreatic cancer: can a test detect disease at an actionable stage?

A Spanish case-control study enrolled 57 people with pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, 29 with chronic pancreatitis and 50 controls. An independent German case-control cohort of 76 yielded AUROC **0.83** for the stool classifier. Combining microbial information and CA19-9 gave an AUROC as high as **0.94** in the authors' analyses. Both comparisons start with people whose disease status is already known. A 2026 diagnostic-accuracy review rated the evidence very low certainty and identified high risk of bias across all 20 model-based rows. Several estimates came from the same studies. The open question is whether a fixed threshold identifies additional early-stage cases in a defined risk group at an acceptable burden of false-positive imaging and invasive investigation. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1136/gutjnl-2021-324755) · [September 2026 review](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2026.1866794)

## Breast cancer: which sample and which clinical endpoint?

A 2025 systematic review identified 48 studies, including 3,735 women with breast cancer and 2,023 without. The most common specimens were stool in 29 studies and breast tissue in 14. Findings varied substantially. A separate pilot compared 46 postmenopausal women with newly diagnosed hormone receptor-positive breast cancer and 22 healthy postmenopausal controls. It found differences in gut bacterial groups linked to hormone metabolism and in progesterone levels. The groups overlapped substantially and differed in mean age. Work on a circulating-hormone mechanism is distinct from evidence for a screening assay. A more informative comparison would use prediagnostic samples, a prespecified cancer subtype and a defined screening population. [Systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40885976/) · [Hormone receptor-positive pilot](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00432-025-06338-z)

## Autism and gastrointestinal health: which direction does the association run?

A 2024 study of 1,627 children aged 1–13 reported AUROC **0.91** for a 31-feature stool panel in its primary analysis. An Australian study of 247 children instead found evidence consistent with autism-associated dietary preferences shaping diet diversity and then microbial profiles. In November 2026, investigators studying 720 autistic children and 151 matched controls reported associations with clinical severity, particularly sensory features. Each study asks a different question. A classifier can capture diet, transit or sensory-related eating patterns accurately without identifying a causal trait or an effective intervention. For gastroenterology, priority endpoints could include pain, constipation, feeding concerns and quality of life, evaluated directly. These papers do not establish a stool-based substitute for developmental assessment or a microbiome treatment for autism. [2024 panel](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-024-01739-1) · [Diet and reverse causation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34767757/) · [November 2026 cohort](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-79124-1)

A second November report provides a useful check on the severity claim. Among 1,072 autistic participants, an index based on the absence of selected organisms was associated with selective eating. Its association with overall severity was inconsistent across rating scales. This was a retrospective comparison within a clinical cohort, with no non-autistic control group. It strengthens the case for recording diet and gastrointestinal symptoms before interpreting a stool result. [Sharma et al., BMC Pediatrics, 16 November 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12887-026-07844-w)

## What would change the interpretation?

| Question | Evidence that would move the finding closer to practice |
| --- | --- |
| Resistant-organism carriage | Consistent randomized results on durable clearance, infection and serious adverse events in the intended population |
| Diabetes | Improved calibrated prediction over standard blood measurements, with an actionable decision and independent replication |
| Pancreatic cancer | Prospective, stage-specific sensitivity and positive predictive value at a prespecified threshold in a defined referral group |
| Breast cancer | Reproducible prediagnostic signal at a specified body site, with evidence of incremental value beyond usual screening |
| Gastrointestinal symptoms in autistic children | Prospective studies that account for diet, medications and stool consistency and measure outcomes identified as important by children and families |
