# Gut microbiome interventions in liver disease

[← Review contents](README.md)


**Human clinical evidence through 18 November 2026.** Focused review of selected randomized trials, society guidelines, regulatory communications and late-stage protocols. The evidence is organized by indication because MASLD, recurrent hepatic encephalopathy and post-transplant infection have different populations, outcomes and standards of care.

| Indication | What the human evidence shows | Clinical assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) | A masked 2025 fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) trial found no significant MRI-measured liver-fat benefit despite shifts in composition. Several 2025–26 probiotic or synbiotic trials report short-term laboratory changes. | Liver fibrosis regression, steatohepatitis resolution and clinical benefit remain unproven. |
| Recurrent overt hepatic encephalopathy (OHE) in cirrhosis | The 60-participant THEMATIC trial found fewer recurrences with any FMT in a pooled post hoc comparison. Standard lactulose and rifaximin have stronger, indication-specific evidence. | An adjunctive FMT efficacy signal merits independent confirmation. The 2026 ACG guideline confines FMT for HE to clinical trials. |
| Decompensated cirrhosis beyond OHE | Two 2026 phase III protocols are testing clinical events. Rifaximin-based strategies have not shown general protection against decompensation in major 2025 trials. | Outcome data from phase III FMT studies are pending. |
| Liver transplantation | Older perioperative studies reported fewer bacterial infections with specific products. A 2026 placebo-controlled postoperative trial observed infection in 38.5% with synbiotic and 33.3% with placebo. | Product, timing and population matter. A reproducible benefit for routine prophylaxis, rejection, graft survival or mortality remains unestablished. |

## MASLD and MASH

| Study | Population and comparison | Outcome and interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Groenewegen et al., *Gut Microbes*, 2025 | Masked Dutch RCT, 20 adults with MASLD randomized 1:1 to repeated donor FMT or autologous FMT, 12 weeks. | Primary MRI–proton density fat fraction (MRI-PDFF) relative change −6.13% vs −0.79%, p=.50. Glucose tolerance and liver biochemistry showed no significant overall between-group benefit. Microbiota composition diverged at week 12, with donor-specific engraftment unrelated to response. Small sample, two donors and short follow-up leave meaningful uncertainty. The direction and magnitude of clinical benefit cannot be inferred from engraftment. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/19490976.2025.2541035) |
| Faghieh Dinavari et al., *Eur J Med Res*, 2026 | Double-blind placebo-controlled RCT in 80 adults with MASLD, 40 per arm, 12 weeks. Both arms received individualized diet and physical-activity programs. | The synbiotic group had lower endpoint ALT, AST and selected metabolic/inflammatory laboratory measures versus placebo. Anthropometry did not differ. These endpoints cannot establish reduced steatosis or fibrosis, and the trial gives no long-term event data. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40001-026-03901-3) |
| Multispecies probiotic RCT, *Nutrition*, 2026 | Double-blind placebo-controlled trial, 64 adults with MASLD randomized to probiotic or placebo for 12 weeks. | FIB-4 change −0.05 vs +0.10 (p=.002, interaction q=.007); AST −2.87 vs +1.87 U/L (interaction q=.003); APRI −0.03 vs +0.02 (interaction q=.001). No significant effect on Fatty Liver Index (interaction q=.942), anthropometry, glucose or lipids. AST contributes to both FIB-4 and APRI, so concordant shifts are partly mathematically coupled. These scores cannot demonstrate structural fibrosis regression over 12 weeks. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nut.2026.113325) |
| Next-generation probiotic RCT, *J Transl Med*, 2025 | Single-center, four-arm, double-blind, placebo-controlled proof of concept. Three strain-specific formulations, all on silymarin background, 8 weeks. | Reported unadjusted ALT and AST improvements for one formulation. Baseline-adjusted ANCOVA found no significant between-group ALT/AST effects (lowest adjusted ALT p=.06). Abstract and methods differ on participant flow, with the methods reporting 110 randomized and 103 completing follow-up, while the abstract reports 110 participants. Short follow-up and no structural liver outcome. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-025-07478-z) |

**Earlier anchors.** Scorletti et al. randomized 104 UK adults with legacy NAFLD criteria to a synbiotic or placebo for 10–14 months. Microbiota composition changed, while quantitative liver fat and noninvasive fibrosis measures showed no significant efficacy. Craven et al. randomized 21 adults (donor versus autologous FMT) and found no significant MRI-PDFF or insulin-resistance effect; improved small-intestinal permeability was observed in the small high-permeability subgroup. Witjes et al. (n=21; donor versus autologous FMT) documented microbial and metabolite changes in a paired-biopsy pilot. The evidence for hepatic necroinflammation was exploratory, with no established histologic or fibrosis efficacy. [INSYTE](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.gastro.2020.01.031) · [Craven](https://doi.org/10.14309/ajg.0000000000000661) · [Witjes](https://doi.org/10.1002/hep4.1601)

**Guideline position.** EASL–EASD–EASO judged the evidence insufficient to recommend nutraceuticals, including gut microbiota-modifying agents, for reducing liver damage, fibrosis or liver-related outcomes, or to establish safety. ALT and AST are injury markers, MRI-PDFF quantifies fat, and neither establishes fibrosis regression or improved survival. Treatment trials aimed at MASH need fibrosis-appropriate outcomes and follow-up. [EASL–EASD–EASO guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11299976/)

## Cirrhosis and hepatic encephalopathy

**Standard treatment benchmark.** The March 2026 ACG guideline recommends lactulose after a first overt HE episode and adding rifaximin for recurrence despite maintenance lactulose. In the pivotal 299-participant placebo-controlled rifaximin RCT, over 90% also used lactulose. Six-month breakthrough HE occurred in 22.1% vs 45.9% (HR 0.42, 95% CI 0.28–0.64). HE-associated hospitalization occurred in 13.6% vs 22.6% (HR 0.50, 95% CI 0.29–0.87). Lactulose and rifaximin are distinct from microbiota transplantation and the trial does not establish a single microbial mechanism. [ACG 2026](https://doi.org/10.14309/AJG.0000000000003899) · [Bass et al.](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0907893)

**Covert HE, 2026.** A multicenter open-label RCT randomized 50 patients to rifaximin or no rifaximin over 12 weeks. The primary Stroop performance change was −4.45 versus −0.98 seconds, between-group p=.056. A secondary cirrhosis-related adverse-event composite occurred in 0/24 versus 7/26, p=.006. The small, open-label study and secondary-event signal limit practice implications. [Inada et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/apt.70712)

**THEMATIC, phase II, 2025.** Sixty US adults with cirrhosis and prior overt HE on lactulose and rifaximin were randomized into four masked groups, 15 per arm, spanning all placebo to differing combinations of active and placebo FMT. The primary outcome was FMT-attributable adverse events. None were reported over six months. Ten participants had recurrent HE. The pooled post hoc comparison showed 4/45 (8.9%) with any active FMT compared with 6/15 (40.0%) with all placebo (OR 0.15, 95% CI 0.04–0.64). The small comparator group and small number of events limit precision, subgroup inference and detection of rare harms. Frequency of all-cause hospitalization and death did not differ significantly. The 2026 multi-omic follow-up is from the same trial and its associations need external validation. [THEMATIC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2024.12.047) · [Same-cohort multi-omics](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhepr.2025.101634)

**Earlier randomized HE trials.** Two 20-person studies by the same group supported feasibility and gave preliminary clinical or cognitive signals. The initial study was open label and the active arm also received antibiotic pretreatment. The later masked capsule study reported similar HE episode frequency over follow-up. The October 2026 Bayesian synthesis of three small trials estimated a median recurrence RR of 0.255 (95% credible interval 0.057–1.039), with sensitivity to excluding individual studies and low certainty. The trials were led by the same investigator at one center. The ACG guideline discusses these data and restricts FMT for HE to trials. [2017 RCT](https://doi.org/10.1002/hep.29306) · [Capsule RCT](https://doi.org/10.1002/hep.30690) · [2026 synthesis](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40001-026-05286-9) · [ACG 2026](https://doi.org/10.14309/AJG.0000000000003899)

**Broader cirrhosis.** The 237-participant LIVERHOPE phase III trial tested simvastatin plus rifaximin versus double placebo on top of usual care in decompensated cirrhosis for 12 months. Acute-on-chronic liver failure occurred in 17.9% vs 14.2% (HR 1.23, 95% CI 0.65–2.34). The composite of transplant or death was 18.8% vs 24.2% (HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.43–1.32). These results concern the combination in decompensation. A separate masked trial in severe cirrhosis with ascites also found no significant survival or complication benefit from rifaximin as primary spontaneous bacterial peritonitis prophylaxis. A 2026 mixed-GI RCT (114 adults, 43 with cirrhosis) tested FMT against sham for multidrug-resistant organism carriage. At four weeks, eradication in the overall cohort was 31.0% vs 30.4% (absolute difference 0.6 percentage points, 95% CI −16.2 to 17.6, p=.94). This is a colonization outcome in a mixed population. [LIVERHOPE](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2024.27441) · [Ascites trial](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2025.06.019) · [Decolonization trial](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2026.0655)

**Phase III watch list.** PROMISE plans 300 participants with alcohol-related, MetALD or MASLD cirrhosis (MELD-Na 8–16), placebo controlled, with the first infection or decompensation leading to emergency presentation or hospitalization over 24 months as the primary endpoint. LiverGut plans 190 adults with decompensated cirrhosis from eight Spanish hospitals and measures time to first new decompensation over 12 months, with transplant-free survival, mortality and hospitalization among secondary outcomes. TransImmune is a separate phase IIa placebo-controlled protocol in cirrhosis with ascites emphasizing immune activation. These are published protocols. [PROMISE](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13063-026-09914-x) · [LiverGut](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2026-118486) · [TransImmune](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2026-119299)

## Liver transplantation

| Study or population | Result | How to use the evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Plank et al., *Liver Transpl*, July 2026. Auckland, double-blind RCT, 82 randomized, 81 in author-designated ITT (39 active, 42 placebo). Synbiotic initiated after transplantation for 14 days. | The day-30 bacterial-infection primary outcome occurred in 15/39 (38.5%) vs 14/42 (33.3%), adjusted p=.68. During 90-day follow-up, rejection occurred in 12/39 (30.8%) vs 17/42 (40.5%), p=.49, and deaths were 1 vs 2. Median postoperative length of stay was 9 days in both groups, p=.90. | Recent controlled evidence did not reproduce the expected reduction in bacterial infection. Single center, specific formulation and postoperative timing, with insufficient events to estimate mortality or rejection with precision. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/LVT.0000000000000951) |
| PREPRO trial, older double-blind, 100 adult living-donor liver transplant recipients in India. Intervention began before transplant. | Thirty-day culture-proven bacterial infection 22% vs 44%, p=.019 (OR 0.359, 95% CI 0.150–0.858). Abstract reverses the percentages in one phrase, while the direction follows the reported odds ratio and author conclusion. | Positive product- and setting-specific signal, with perioperative timing and eligibility that differ from the Auckland trial. Earlier small perioperative and preoperative studies also reported favorable infection results. [PREPRO](https://doi.org/10.1002/jhbp.1182) · [Independent trial appraisal](https://doi.org/10.3389/ti.2022.10769) · [Rayes](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-6143.2004.00649.x) · [Grąt](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2017.04.021) |
| Living-donor microbiota-directed RCT protocol, *BMJ Open*, 2025. | A placebo-controlled transplant synbiotic trial with paired microbiome analyses in the first 50 participants. | Protocol publication. The identified source provides no efficacy outcome. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2024-092984) |

**Recurrent *Clostridioides difficile* infection (CDI) is a separate indication.** A multicenter retrospective solid-organ transplant series included 94 recipients treated for recurrent or severe CDI. It informs that indication with the limitations of retrospective mixed-organ data. AGA conditionally supports selected conventional FMT use after antibiotics in mildly or moderately immunocompromised adults with recurrent CDI and advises against fecal microbiota–based therapies in severely immunocompromised adults with recurrent CDI. FDA has documented transmission of invasive multidrug-resistant infection through investigational FMT, including one death in the cited alert. Product approval and indication must be interpreted in their own jurisdiction. The clinical studies reviewed here establish no basis to generalize CDI treatment outcomes to liver graft outcomes. [Solid-organ series](https://doi.org/10.1111/ajt.15058) · [AGA guideline](https://gastro.org/clinical-guidance/fecal-microbiota-based-therapies-for-select-gastrointestinal-diseases/) · [2026 AGA CDI update](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cgh.2026.05.007) · [FDA safety communication](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medical-product-safety-information/fecal-microbiota-transplantation-safety-communication-risk-serious-adverse-reactions-due)

## Clinical questions for a hepatology discussion

1. For OHE recurrence, what independent randomized result, follow-up and safety precision would justify moving beyond research use on top of well-delivered lactulose and rifaximin?
2. For MASLD, which fibrosis-stratified outcomes would lead to clinical action, and what prognostic value would remain after accounting for weight change, diabetes treatment and baseline fibrosis?
3. For transplantation, which subgroup and patient-important outcome could justify another efficacy study given the 2026 postoperative null trial and the distinct evidence for recurrent CDI?
