# Gut microbiota applications in IBD and IBS: clinical evidence landscape

[← Review contents](README.md)


**Evidence cut-off: 18 November 2026.** This review concerns human clinical evidence and clinical guidance. Microbiome associations are not treated as causal mechanisms or validated clinical tests. “Microbiome-directed” includes live microbial consortia/FMT and microbial metabolites; antibiotics and diets are included only where trials explicitly evaluated microbiome predictors or effects.

## Executive synthesis

- In IBD, dysbiosis is a reproducible group-level observation, but no stool taxonomic or functional microbiome test is currently established for routine diagnosis, treat-to-target monitoring, or choosing an advanced therapy. Individual signatures vary with disease location/activity, diet, medication, sampling and analytic pipeline. Current actionable biomarkers remain conventional inflammatory measures (especially fecal calprotectin, CRP) interpreted alongside endoscopy/imaging and symptoms.
- FMT in ulcerative colitis (UC) has a modest efficacy signal in some small, heterogeneous trials, but results depend on regimen and population, certainty remains low, and professional guidance does not support routine use. Crohn disease (CD) evidence is weaker: the published small placebo-controlled trial did not meet its combined clinical/endoscopic remission endpoint; a January 2026 meeting abstract reported a nonsignificant clinical-response difference in another trial. FMT remains investigational for IBD.
- The 2026 REFIT2 phase 3 IBS trial is practice-relevant negative evidence: donor FMT did not outperform autologous-stool placebo on the primary symptom endpoint (40% vs 38%, absolute difference 1.9 points, 95% CI −8.1 to 12.0, p=0.76). It strongly argues against routine donor FMT for IBS.
- IBS microbiome associations and treatment-response signatures remain exploratory. A 2026 small randomized comparison of low-FODMAP diet and rifaximin explored microbial predictors but cannot yet establish a clinical selection test. Probiotic evidence is product/strain- and outcome-specific, inconsistent, and insufficient to support a general “microbiome restoration” claim.

## IBD

### Biomarkers and clinical utility

IBD cohorts consistently show altered community composition and microbial function versus controls, including reduced abundance of some obligate anaerobes/short-chain-fatty-acid producers and enrichment of inflammation-associated taxa in subsets. These are population-level patterns, not a single disease-specific fingerprint. Stool profiling is affected by stool consistency, inflammation, bowel preparation, diet, antibiotics and IBD drugs; studies also differ in sequencing, reference databases and definitions of response. Thus association, discrimination within a research cohort, and externally validated clinical utility must be kept distinct.

Recent translational work illustrates both promise and present limits. A treatment-naïve pediatric study (online 2025, February 2026 issue) included 103 children with IBD, 75 symptomatic controls and 356 healthy controls. The species model reached AUROC 0.96 against healthy controls. Against symptomatic controls, the clinical-only model, including fecal calprotectin and CRP, achieved AUROC 0.90, the species model 0.71 and the model combining all layers 0.80. Induction response prediction was limited (AUROC 0.63 overall and 0.67 for nutritional therapy). In a separate cohort (260 IBD, 166 at-risk and 57 healthy participants), an enterotype was associated with treatment escalation (HR 1.71, 95% CI 1.10–2.66) and higher fecal calprotectin. Treatment escalation is clinician-mediated and the association does not establish incremental utility beyond inflammation markers. [Pediatric study](https://doi.org/10.1093/ibd/izaf184) · [Enterotype study, 5 Oct 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/ibd/izag176)

**Clinical implication:** Do not use commercial “dysbiosis” panels, stool relative-abundance reports, or an unvalidated microbial signature to diagnose IBD, rule it out, select biologic/small-molecule treatment, or declare remission. For suspected IBD, use standard evaluation; for monitoring, interpret fecal calprotectin/CRP with clinical context and objective assessment. AGA guidance for Crohn disease biomarkers supports fecal calprotectin and CRP in defined monitoring settings, as inflammatory biomarkers. [AGA Crohn biomarker guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37981354/)

Across trials, heterogeneous interventions, control groups and background therapies limit transferability. Short trials cannot reliably detect uncommon harms, including donor-derived pathogen transmission. In February 2026 the sponsor reported clinical remission by partial Mayo in 12/19 MB310 recipients versus 3/10 placebo recipients in COMPOSER-1, an exploratory phase 1b study of a defined oral consortium. These figures are sponsor-reported top-line data. [EU trial record](https://euclinicaltrials.eu/ctis-public/view/2023-507376-50-00) · [Sponsor report](https://microbiotica.com/microbiotica-announces-impressive-results-in-its-phase-1b-trial-of-mb310-in-ulcerative-colitis/)

### Other microbiota-related interventions

**Probiotics:** Effects are not class-wide. Evidence for selected preparations in UC or pouchitis cannot be generalized to other products or Crohn disease. The 2025 BSG guideline suggests against probiotics for inducing or maintaining UC remission (low certainty). Strain, formulation and endpoint matter. [BSG guideline](https://doi.org/10.1136/gutjnl-2024-334395)

**Microbial metabolites:** A double-blind placebo-controlled 2025/2026 trial randomized 140 patients (60 CD, 80 UC) to microencapsulated butyrate plus conventional therapy or placebo plus conventional therapy. It assessed microbiome composition and disease activity, but a metabolite supplement trial is not proof that a microbiome assay can guide treatment. Interpret any subgroup or biomarker findings as exploratory unless replicated and linked to durable objective outcomes. [PubMed record / DOI](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41354580/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dld.2025.11.014).

## IBS

### Biomarkers

IBS is a disorder of gut-brain interaction diagnosed clinically using symptom criteria and appropriate limited testing for alarm features/alternatives. Stool microbiome profiles show group differences in some studies, but findings are inconsistent and overlap substantially with healthy populations and other disorders. There is no accepted microbiome-based IBS diagnostic assay or validated stool “dysbiosis” score for routine subtype assignment, prognosis, or therapy selection. Fecal calprotectin may help distinguish inflammatory disease from IBS in appropriate patients; it is not a microbiome marker.

The 2026 randomized rifaximin-versus-low-FODMAP study analyzed 65 adults with IBS-D over five weeks. Both arms improved abdominal pain and bloating, with no significant difference between them. Exploratory microbiome predictors still require external validation and a prospective test-guided trial. [Clinical Gastroenterology and Hepatology, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cgh.2026.04.014).

### FMT: REFIT2 phase 3 trial

REFIT2 was a five-hospital Norwegian randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial. It enrolled adults 18–65 years with moderate-to-severe IBS; 450 were randomized (299 donor FMT, 151 autologous-stool placebo), with 448 included after two treatment-allocation switches. The primary endpoint was a ≥75-point decrease in IBS Severity Scoring System (IBS-SSS) at day 90. It was met by 119 (40%) with donor FMT and 57 (38%) with placebo. The absolute difference was 1.9 percentage points (95% CI −8.1 to 12.0, p=0.76). Thus no statistically significant or persuasive clinical benefit was shown. [Lancet, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01424-8); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42785338/).

Limitations/context: participants were mostly women (65%), median age 36, and the 90-day primary endpoint does not resolve very long-term outcomes. But the large phase 3 design directly tests a clinically meaningful symptom outcome, with a substantial placebo response; engraftment or microbiome shifts cannot substitute for the null symptom comparison. AGA’s 2024 recommendation against conventional FMT for IBS outside trials remains consistent with these findings. Routine donor FMT for IBS is not supported.

### Probiotics, prebiotics and diet

Guidelines differ in how permissively they discuss probiotics because the evidence is heterogeneous. Effects vary by strain/product, IBS subtype, dose and outcome, and positive findings for one formulation do not establish efficacy of “probiotics” generally. The AGA probiotic guideline limits its IBS recommendation to clinical trials. Low-FODMAP diets have evidence for symptom management. That clinical outcome does not establish that microbiome changes mediate symptom improvement. [AGA probiotic guideline](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.gastro.2020.05.059)

## Evidence-quality and reporting cautions

1. **Separate association from prediction and clinical utility.** A statistically significant taxon difference, a within-cohort AUC, donor engraftment, and improved symptoms are different findings.
2. **Name the specimen and assay.** Stool, mucosal biopsy, 16S, shotgun metagenomics, metabolomics and multi-omics are not interchangeable.
3. **Report the comparator and denominator.** In FMT, autologous stool is an active procedural placebo; percentages without randomized denominators can mislead.
4. **Treat 2025–26 results by maturity.** REFIT2 and published trials are peer-reviewed clinical outcomes; conference abstracts and small single-center pilots are provisional.
5. **Do not infer causality from “normalization.”** A microbiome change may follow improved inflammation, diet or medication rather than cause the improvement.
