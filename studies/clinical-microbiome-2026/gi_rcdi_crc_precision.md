# Gastroenterology clinical evidence

[← Review contents](README.md)


*Evidence checked through 18 November 2026. This is a targeted literature review of human trials, regulatory decisions and clinical guidance. US approvals and UAE screening recommendations are identified separately.*

| Application | Clinical status | Best-supported interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Microbiota therapy after antibiotics for recurrent *Clostridioides difficile* infection (rCDI) | Established in selected adults. Two FDA-approved products have randomized comparative evidence. | Prevents further symptomatic CDI. Products have different pivotal populations and endpoints, so their effect sizes cannot rank the products. |
| Colorectal cancer (CRC) screening with fecal immunochemical test (FIT), multitarget stool DNA/RNA, or blood-based cfDNA | Clinical use is established for guideline-specified tests and settings. | Stool and blood assays measure hemoglobin or human molecular markers. Their evidence does not establish clinical utility for a microbial panel. |
| Microbial markers added to CRC screening | Investigational. | Some screening cohorts report added discrimination for precancer in FIT-positive individuals. Prospective evidence on missed lesions, referrals and interval cancers is still needed. |
| Community-wide stool microbiome tests for individual GI care | Research use. | Laboratory characterization and cohort discrimination do not establish a diagnosis or show that test-guided care improves an individual patient's outcomes. |

## 1. Prevention of recurrent *C. difficile* infection

FDA's fecal microbiota product list contains Rebyota and Vowst. Both indications are for prevention of recurrence after antibacterial treatment of recurrent CDI in people aged ≥18 years. Neither label covers treatment of active CDI or treatment of IBD/IBS. [FDA product list](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/fecal-microbiota-products) · [Rebyota](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/vaccines/rebyota) · [Vowst](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/vowst)

| FDA-approved treatment | Pivotal comparison | Main outcome and follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| **Rebyota** (fecal microbiota, live-jslm, formerly RBX2660), rectal, FDA approved **30 November 2022** | PUNCH CD3 phase 3 enrolled adults with ≥1 recurrence after treatment for the qualifying episode. Of 289 randomized, 267 received blinded product/placebo (180/87). The prespecified primary Bayesian model incorporated earlier PUNCH CD2 data. | Estimated 8-week **absence of recurrent CDI** was **70.6% vs 57.5%** for placebo (difference 13.1 percentage points, 95% Bayesian credible interval 2.3–24.0). Six-month sustained clinical response was 65.5% vs 56.5% (difference 9.1 points, 95% CI −3.6 to 21.7). [FDA label](https://www.fda.gov/media/163587/download) · [PUNCH CD3, Khanna et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40265-022-01797-x) |
| **Vowst** (fecal microbiota spores, live-brpk, formerly SER-109), oral, FDA approved **26 April 2023** | ECOSPOR III phase 3 in adults with ≥3 CDI episodes within 12 months inclusive of the qualifying episode, N=182. | **Recurrence** was **12.4% (11/89) vs 39.8% (37/93)** for placebo through week 8 (RR 0.32, 95% CI 0.18–0.58). Through week 24 it was **21.3% vs 47.3%** (RR 0.46, 95% CI 0.30–0.73). [FDA label](https://www.fda.gov/media/167579/download) · [ECOSPOR III, Feuerstadt et al.](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2106516) |

The placebo risks, eligibility, diagnostic criteria and analyses differ across the two studies. Both approved products are donor-derived. Labels warn of potential transmission of infectious agents. FDA documented serious infections after investigational conventional FMT and describes limited enforcement discretion for conventional FMT for standard-therapy-refractory CDI under specified conditions. Approved-product labeling, investigational FMT and expert guidance on rescue use describe distinct settings. [FDA infectious transmission notice](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medical-product-safety-information/fecal-microbiota-transplantation-safety-communication-risk-serious-adverse-reactions-due) · [FDA enforcement guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/enforcement-policy-regarding-investigational-new-drug-requirements-use-fecal-microbiota)

**Clinical guidance.** The 2024 AGA GRADE guideline supports selective post-antibiotic use in immunocompetent patients with rCDI, suggests selective conventional FMT in mild/moderate immunocompromise, and suggests against microbiota therapy for recurrence prevention in severe immunocompromise. It conditionally supports conventional FMT as rescue for selected hospitalized adults with severe or fulminant CDI unresponsive to standard antimicrobials. A 2026 AGA adult expert update advises offering FMT or an approved product after treatment of the second recurrence (third episode), with earlier consideration in selected high-risk or especially morbid cases. The 2026 AGA IBD-specific expert update advises offering microbiota therapy after at least one CDI recurrence in people with IBD. This advice concerns CDI recurrence prevention in an IBD population. [2024 GRADE guideline](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.gastro.2024.01.008) · [2026 adult expert update](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cgh.2026.05.007) · [2026 IBD-specific expert update](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.gastro.2026.03.008)

**Follow-up evidence and pipeline**

- PUNCH CD3-OLS, published in the 2025 journal issue, was an open-label Rebyota cohort of 697 treated adults. Treatment success was 73.8% at 8 weeks. The 6-month 91.0% sustained response applies to the subgroup with initial treatment success. The study broadened representation, including patients at their first recurrence, and lacked a concurrent comparator. [Feuerstadt et al., Clinical Infectious Diseases](https://academic.oup.com/cid/article/80/1/43/7740543)
- VE303 is an investigational defined bacterial consortium. In its small phase 2 randomized trial, high-dose recipients had week-8 recurrence of 13.8% (4/29) versus 45.5% with placebo (10/22) under the broadest prespecified recurrence analysis. Under the narrow toxin-positive definition, recurrence was 13.8% vs 22.7% (p=0.29). The broader lab-confirmed analysis gave 13.8% vs 36.4% (p=0.04). The RESTORATiVE303 phase 3 trial was listed as recruiting in the November 2026 registry update and had no posted efficacy results. [Louie et al., JAMA](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2023.4314) · [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06237452](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06237452) · [NCI trial record](https://www.cancer.gov/research/participate/clinical-trials-search/v?id=NCI-2024-07593)
- In a June 2026 randomized phase 1b safety study of investigational MTC01, recurrent CDI was prevented at 8 weeks in 7/9 recipients of the defined consortium and 8/9 recipients of conventional FMT from the same donor. Efficacy was secondary. The 18-person study cannot establish comparative effectiveness, equivalence or rare-event safety. [Nature Medicine, 2 June 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04442-2)

## 2. Colorectal cancer screening and precision assays

The 2026 American Cancer Society guideline recommends starting screening at age 45 in average-risk adults. Preferred choices include annual FIT, multitarget stool DNA or RNA every three years, and colonoscopy every ten years among other visual options. Blood tests are reserved for people who decline or have not completed preferred tests, primarily because of lower sensitivity for advanced precancerous lesions and stage I cancers. A positive noncolonoscopy result requires timely colonoscopy, preferably within six months in the ACS guideline. The guideline applies to average-risk screening. Colitis-associated dysplasia surveillance and symptomatic diagnostic evaluation have separate pathways. [ACS 2026 guideline](https://doi.org/10.3322/caac.70083)

Abu Dhabi Public Health Centre lists screening for men and women aged 40–75, with annual FIT, decennial colonoscopy, and Shield every three years for eligible individuals meeting its criteria. For an Abu Dhabi study, the applicable local screening pathway is the reference standard for access and follow-up. The PREEMPT CRC validation study included 12 evaluable participants from a UAE site among 27,010 overall, so it provides no precise UAE-specific performance estimate. [ADPHC CRC program](https://www.adphc.gov.ae/en/Public-Health-Programs/Cancers-Program/Colorectal-Cancer-Program) · [PREEMPT CRC](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2025.7515)

The DNA and RNA studies allow within-cohort comparisons with their specific FIT assays and thresholds. Other cross-study comparisons are descriptive. A program's benefit depends on participation, repeat testing, colonoscopy completion, interval cancers and mortality. The 2026 SCREESCO report randomized 278,280 Swedish adults aged 60 and at median 4.8 years found stage III–IV CRC incidence lower in the two-round FIT arm versus usual care (IRR 0.71, 95% CI 0.58–0.86). The colonoscopy arm estimate was 0.86 (95% CI 0.67–1.11). These diagnostic-phase results are not the definitive mortality endpoint. [SCREESCO](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04225-9)

## 3. Microbial signatures for CRC screening

- **Cross-cohort feasibility, 2025.** A pooled study of 3,741 fecal metagenomes across 18 cohorts reported average leave-one-dataset-out AUROC **0.85** (individual held-out datasets **0.71–0.97**) for CRC versus controls. This is a reproducibility result across cohorts. Spectrum differences and varying performance limit inference for a fixed clinical cutoff when applied to asymptomatic screening attendees or to advanced precancer. [Nature Medicine, 3 June 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-03693-9)
- **Screening-based evidence, Norway, 2026.** The CRCbiome study examined residual FIT material from **1,034 FIT-positive participants**. Adding microbial profiles to quantitative FIT and clinical covariates improved model discrimination for premalignant lesions. Quantitative FIT remained better for invasive CRC. Most microbial associations were with distal lesions. A FIT-positive-only cohort cannot establish performance in FIT-negative people or justify withholding colonoscopy from any FIT-positive participant. [Birkeland et al., Nature Communications, 23 July 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75962-1)
- **Screening-program sampling, Italy, 2026.** The Turin population-based case–control analysis used residual FIT specimens for **44 CRC, 269 advanced adenomas, 134 early adenomas and 478 FIT-negative controls**. It supports the feasibility of studying microbiome–neoplasia associations within a screening program. The case–control analysis and use of FIT-negative controls do not establish prospective operating characteristics for a locked classifier. [British Journal of Cancer](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41416-026-03495-x)

A clinically meaningful intended use for UAE research would be an observational add-on comparison against the existing screening pathway, with advanced precancer detection and colonoscopy burden as co-relevant outcomes. Assay assessment would require a prespecified comparator and threshold, blinded reference assessment, representative enrollment, and external and temporal validation. Separate analyses for stage I CRC, advanced adenoma, advanced serrated lesions and proximal lesions would uncover differences that an aggregate AUROC can obscure. A post-FIT prioritization study and a front-line screening accuracy study answer different questions and need different populations.

## 4. Precision testing beyond CRC

The international consensus on microbiome testing in clinical practice identified insufficient evidence to widely recommend routine community-wide testing or report a generic dysbiosis index. A 2026 multidisciplinary Delphi statement formalizes minimum clinical metadata and endpoints to reduce variability in microbiome research. Clinical validation depends on the individual intended claim, test reproducibility, incremental value over clinical standards, and evidence that acting on the result changes a meaningful outcome. A stool pathogen test for symptomatic CDI, a fecal inflammatory marker such as calprotectin, a CRC screening assay measuring human markers, and broad microbial community profiling have distinct purposes. [Porcari et al., Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-1253(24)00311-X) · [2026 Delphi research standards](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41575-026-01229-1)
