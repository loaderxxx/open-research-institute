## Foresight and method fidelity

[Inayatullah's original CLA paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S001632879800086X) lays out four layers: observable litany, social causes, discourse/worldview and myth/metaphor. It presents a method for opening up alternatives. A four-cell table is not evidence that a model correctly grounded any cell in a specific workshop.

[Deconstructing ChatGPT, part 1](https://jfsdigital.org/2023/01/23/deconstructing-chatgpt-part-1/) provides a direct record of Inayatullah correcting the model's account of CLA. The transcript is proof of an example, not an error-rate estimate, a blind comparison or evidence that present systems fail equally. The verified layer correction is a useful test case when paired with independent scoring.

[Entering the age of hybrid futures](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.futures.2025.103705) compares human-written and GPT-generated scenarios with text analysis and a panel of experts. It asks how generated and human scenarios differ as outputs. It does not test whether a model retained who said what, or preserved a particular group's dissent through translation and iterative summarization. Evidence here is based on the publisher description.

## Adjacent deliberation research

| Primary source | What it tests | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Zhu et al., large-scale opinion aggregation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05154), preprint | Empirical evaluation of model summaries of public opinions; authors report persistent challenges representing minority views and sensitivity to input order. | Distinct from causal-layer coding and local workshop translation; systems, participants and scoring objectives differ. |
| [Fulay, Dimitrakopoulou & Roy, The Empty Chair](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13812), author manuscript | Fielded AI characters in a student deliberation and studied whether they could surface missing perspectives. | A synthetic character can suggest an unrepresented question; it does not speak for an absent community. A small student setting limits external validity. |
| [Tessler et al., AI and common ground](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq2852), Science article and author abstract | A trained system supported convergence on a group statement and received favorable participant ratings in the reported comparison with human mediation. | A consensus objective, small-group setting and selected policy topics are not a test of preserving low-frequency disagreement across all summary formats. |
| [Bakker et al., preference aggregation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15006), author manuscript | Models group statement choice from individual evaluations; aggregation objectives can be explicitly defined rather than left implicit. | A statement chosen as agreeable does not show that every view is retained or that all participants authorized its wording. |

## Cross-analysis

The literature on consensus and the literature on minority-view loss answer different questions, so there is no contradiction in finding both useful compromise proposals and poor coverage of dissent in other formats. A system should keep a “shared statements” layer distinct from a “dissent and unresolved questions” layer. A plausible concern generated by the AI belongs in a third, clearly synthetic layer. Participants' own statements, especially translated ones, should be traceable to a source span with a version and explicit status.

This map is a targeted reading, not a systematic review or claim of novelty. No effectiveness claim is made for CLA in clinical settings or for AI inference of feelings.
