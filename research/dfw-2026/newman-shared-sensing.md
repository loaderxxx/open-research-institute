# Knowing when autonomy should stop

Editorial status: editorial_ready

Paul Newman's Oxford profile identifies navigation and mobile autonomy as core interests. Research on localisation and shared camera networks motivates a practical question: how should a system represent uncertainty when its sensors cannot see enough? Our proposed evaluation distinguishes clear, occupied and unknown areas rather than treating missing evidence as safety.

## Limitations

A research proposal, not a validated safety system. Disputed timing comparisons are omitted. No Oxford or Oxa collaboration or endorsement is implied.

## Next question

Explore localisation uncertainty and fail-safe evaluation criteria.

## Adjacent ORI concept: Road Surface Alerts

A proposed silent-mode study would use consented, securely mounted fleet phones on two repeat routes from one Dubai fleet or depot. Candidate road defects would require independent repeat passes, explicit direction and position uncertainty, and human verification. Evaluation would compare confirmed defects, false alerts, wrong directed road segments and time to verification. Raw traces should be minimised and access restricted; no driver-facing alerts during the study. Conceptual reference: Churchill and Newman (2013) accumulate visual experiences for long-term localisation. Our proposed concept instead uses repeated phone observations; this is a methodological analogy, not a validated transfer of their system.

Concept stage; no field results, fleet agreement or partnership. The link to Oxford is methodological localisation relevance, not a claim that Newman works on this pothole project.
