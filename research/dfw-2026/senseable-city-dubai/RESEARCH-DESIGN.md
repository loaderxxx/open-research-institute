# Proposed evaluation: a shade recommendation people can actually follow

**EN** · [Русский](RESEARCH-DESIGN.ru.md)

[Study overview](README.md) · [Evidence atlas](ATLAS.md)

**Research question.** Does a shade-aware choice remain favorable when access is verified at the time of the trip and the trade-off is calculated against the shortest route that meets the same accessibility criteria?

## Comparisons

Preselect two metro catchments and the same fixed public origin–destination pairs for each method. For each scheduled time, compare (1) shortest verified accessible route, (2) shade-aware outdoor route, (3) shade-aware route using verified-open indoor passages, and (4) a diagnostic recomputation that allows every mapped indoor link. The last comparison reveals sensitivity to assumptions; it is never a recommendation.

Freeze graph and imagery dates, map licenses, entrances, travel direction and accessibility definitions before comparing results. Record official hours and observed access separately. A public facility listing should not be treated as evidence that a particular entrance is open. Evaluate unknown links by removing them and testing whether the same route still wins. Do not collect device identifiers, faces or private location histories.

## Evaluation and interpretation

Record additional distance and time, estimated minutes in direct sun, percentage of valid routes and count of closed or uncertain links. Report downtown and less dense catchments separately; individual trip distributions matter more than an aggregate when a route is invalid. Assess ranking stability across times, walking-speed assumptions and shade weights. Use targeted on-site shade checks when permitted and publish coverage by time. If measuring local microclimate later, report it separately from human physiological outcomes.

A result fails to support the recommendation if the estimated exposure benefit disappears when indoor access is verified, the alternative is not walkable, or small plausible modeling changes flip the ranking. In that case report the shortest confirmed route and label the shaded option unverified. This proposed evaluation does not establish heat-health advice or forecast real pedestrian behavior.
