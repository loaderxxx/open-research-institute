# From conference prompt to evidence atlas

## Where the research started

The event programme supplied the initial leads. The Claude return archive contained seven edited long-form research notes, underlying first-pass research records, a cross-check, a review and change log, a gap list, and a 50-row evidence ledger. The first public release used compact editorial notes. These atlases expand those notes by mapping each question to sources and adjacent research, adding claim-level limits and separating proposed tests from observed results. First-pass work, personal contact material, and unrelated drafts are not republished.

## How we selected and read sources

1. **Start with the actual claim.** Is it a claim about a tested artifact, observed behavior, measured performance, or a proposal? Identify who asserted it, under which conditions, and against what comparator.
2. **Prefer the original artifact.** Start from the author manuscript, journal article, public dataset description or organiser record as relevant. Search by a precise title or DOI first. Follow contrasting or complementary work where it addresses a specific boundary in the original evidence.
3. **Record the best version actually inspected.** An inspected methods section, publisher abstract, author description and programme entry support different degrees of detail. An inaccessible page is not a source we have read; an archive record remains archive-reviewed unless independently re-opened. No claim is accepted just because two derivative articles repeat it.
4. **Check transfer separately.** Record the unit of observation, intended setting, original comparator, principal assumption and limit. If a study does not test the transfer of interest, describe the gap rather than implying validation.
5. **Separate the next test.** Each study links to a design or appraisal note, not a results section. Proposed comparisons, metrics, examples and thresholds are editorial or hypothetical unless explicitly attributed to a paper.

These steps describe a targeted, AI-assisted narrative evidence map. We did not perform a systematic review or meta-analysis, preregister a search, pool effect sizes, rerun primary experiments, or request author endorsement. The sources named in each atlas are the auditable inclusion list, not a claim of exhaustive coverage.

## How to read evidence status

| Status | What it can support |
| --- | --- |
| Methods or full text read | Specific design and reported findings scoped to the version inspected; not independent replication. |
| Abstract or excerpt checked | Claims stated there, not undisclosed details about sampling, controls or effect uncertainty. |
| Catalogue / programme checked | What was listed, which prototype or proposal was described; not performance or endorsement. |
| Archive-reviewed, not independently re-opened | A research lead with explicitly limited evidentiary weight. |
| ORI inference / proposal | A reasoned comparison, definition or next test; not a result. |

If a discrepancy remains open, report it rather than average the conflicting claims. A useful correction identifies the source version, the exact affected claim, and which interpretation the evidence supports.
