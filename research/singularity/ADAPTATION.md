# Adaptation: which decision should remain human?

[Track overview](README.md) · [Русский](ADAPTATION.ru.md)

**Proposed local test, not observed performance.** Compare two ways to produce a short research brief from public sources: the usual human workflow and AI assistance followed by human review. The endpoint is a brief an editor would release, not a first draft or a fast model response.

### Before the first task

- Define acceptance: the brief answers the question, each consequential claim traces to its source, and quotations and units are correct. A fabricated citation is a critical error even if the rest reads well.
- Select comparable tasks before seeing outputs. Balance difficulty and order across reviewers; do not have the same reviewer solve the same question twice with the answer already known. Blind the final factual review to workflow when feasible.
- Name the human responsible for publication and the person who may stop the trial. Use public or synthetic inputs. During evaluation the assistant only drafts; distribution, purchases and changes in permissions remain separate decisions.

### Measure the entire job

For each assigned task log elapsed time through review and correction, active human minutes, cash cost including retries, acceptance and critical errors. Include unfinished tasks in the assigned total. Report first-pass acceptance **and** final acceptance separately. A reduction in drafting time can disappear if verification takes longer. A small first run describes feasibility; zero observed critical errors is not a safety guarantee.

### What to do under three outcomes

- **Faster at comparable quality:** repeat on fresh tasks with a different reviewer before expanding. Keep review in place; speed confers no extra authority.
- **Speed improves but factual or privacy errors appear:** pause release and inspect the failure. Restrict the task or restore the manual process; do not average a critical failure into an overall quality score.
- **Service unavailable or gains vanish:** use the saved source list, instructions and acceptance rubric to complete one task manually. Record time and missing dependencies. A documented fallback is only a plan until someone completes the drill.

The [NIST Generative AI Profile](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence) is a governance reference, not empirical proof that this procedure works. ORI's proposal gives the duty to approve, interrupt and recover to identifiable people. The forecasting system, even if accurate, gets none of those powers.
